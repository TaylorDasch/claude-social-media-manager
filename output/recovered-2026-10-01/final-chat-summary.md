## 1. Diagnosis from the inspected samples

Observational critique first; predicted performance is in a separate paragraph at the end.

**Problem specificity (titles): strong and improved.** The September titles name a decision and a cost consequence: "Worth the Higher Price?", "Why Your Payment Can Jump After Closing", "What to Check Before You Buy", "How Much Cash Do You Need?". The tour-era title ("Perfect Starter Home!") names a listing, not a decision. This is the clearest evidence that the shift to pillar content happened in practice, not only in intent.

**Evidence (thumbnails): the packaging contradicts the positioning.** The channel description promises honest, local, verify-before-you-buy guidance. Six of the seven pillar thumbnails use one template: a wide-eyed or alarmed expression, large block text, and illustrative or stock imagery. Specifics seen:
- "Brutally Honest · Temple, TX · No Nightlife · Low Cost" over a rendered modern glass house that is not a Temple home.
- "Tax Hike · Paying More" with "$5,000 (2023)" versus "$7,000 (2024)": illustrative figures, and the year labels read as stale on a video published September 23, 2026.
- "Enough Saved?" with a dark stock house and a "Mortgage Approval DENIED" stamp.
- "New Build Secrets" with a question-mark circle on a house and a pointing gesture.
- "Three Creeks Worth It?" with a "SOLD" stamp and a "DANGER" sign on a rendered streetscape.
- The market update's thumbnail shows "2024 $327,500 vs 2026 $281,300" (a 14.1% fall) under a title that says prices "dropped 4.7%": the two numbers cannot both describe the same measure, and neither source is on the thumbnail.
- The exception is "Harker Heights: NO HOA?": a calm expression, a real aerial of a Harker Heights street, one specific question. That one matches the channel's stated voice.

**Firsthand judgment:** promised in titles ("My D.R. Horton Verdict", "Where I'd start with…") and in the description; not verifiable without audio.

**Structure, pacing, clarity, naturalness:** not assessable from the outside. Conditional: the 8–14 minute lengths are consistent with chaptered explainers; whether chapters exist, whether the first ten seconds carry the name and the decision, and whether there is dead setup can only be judged from the export.

**Visual explanation:** the thumbnails suggest text-over-face graphics; nothing inspectable shows an on-screen table, a map with the viewer's constraint drawn on it, or a document on screen. This is the largest likely gap for a cost-and-tax channel, but it is a hypothesis until the videos are viewed.

**Viewer next step and trust:** the channel description names Taylor and EG Realty and the website; video descriptions were not retrievable, so the presence of a page link, a pinned comment, or an invitation per video is unknown.

**Tour-era comparison (one sample):** the Stylecraft starter-home tour thumbnail uses a real photo and a listing-style promise ("Perfect Starter Home!"); it is honest about what it is, a tour, and makes no cost claim. The pillar era made a bigger promise (honesty, numbers) and packaged it with borrowed drama. The improvement in the question being asked is real; the trust layer has not caught up.

**Predicted performance (separate):** the one 2026 video that broke 1,000 views is a decision video with the loudest thumbnail, so loud packaging is not obviously losing clicks; what cannot be known from view counts is whether it is costing the second click, the one that turns a viewer into an inquiry. That is why the pilot tests the trust layer and the proof layer together, measured on inquiries rather than views.

## 2. The one next-level format

**Chosen: a matched-pair, proof-led ownership-cost decision: a new build and a resale at the same budget in Temple, walked side by side, with every cost input verified on screen.**

Why this format and not the others considered:
- **Field-tested commute trade-offs** are cheap and differentiating, but nothing in the ledger or the channel's view pattern shows commute as the buyer's binding question; new construction is the channel's most consistently viewed topic (the D.R. Horton Shorts and the "What to Check" long-form), and the Stylecraft and new-construction playlists show Taylor already has the access and the footage habit.
- **A buyer-decision case reconstruction** needs consent and de-identification and would tempt a reconstructed story; the owner direction forbids invented experiences.
- **A generic comparison with matched assumptions** (areas, cities) is good and is the natural second pilot; the new-build-versus-resale pair is the version where the inputs are most checkable (incentive sheet, tax treatment, HOA, warranty) and where the viewer's mistake is most expensive.

What makes it "next level" is not footage. It is that every number on screen has a visible source and date, the two homes are matched on budget and area so the viewer sees a real trade-off rather than a feature list, and the video ends with a one-page table the viewer can reuse without calling anyone.

## 3. The same subject three ways

Subject for all three: a roughly $300,000 budget in Temple, one new build and one 2015–2020 resale in the same area. **All numbers below are scenario inputs or placeholders, labeled; no current builder incentive, client, or firsthand story is asserted.**

### A. Ordinary tour (the earlier era)

*Opening (Taylor's voice):* "What's up, it's Taylor with EG Realty. We're in a brand-new Stylecraft build here in Temple, three-two, two-car garage, and it's listed at $264,000. Let's take a look."

*Scene:* walk-through, kitchen, primary suite, backyard; "the builder's offering some incentives right now, so reach out if you want details."

*Decision offered to the viewer:* none. The viewer learns what one house looks like and that incentives exist. The next step is "contact me," which is a request for the viewer's time before any value is given.

### B. Useful pillar explanation (the current era)

*Opening:* "I'm Taylor Dasch with EG Realty. If you're deciding between a new build and a resale in Temple, there are five costs the sales office won't walk you through, and two of them show up after closing."

*Scene:* talking-head chapters: incentives and lender conditions, the first-year tax bill on a new build, HOA and special districts, warranty versus inspection, resale negotiation in a slower market.

*Decision offered:* a framework. Useful, general, and honest, but the viewer still has to apply it to real homes and real numbers, and nothing on screen is sourced.

### C. Proof-led decision demonstration (the pilot)

*Opening, first five seconds:* "Same money, same part of Temple. This one is new. This one is nine years old. By the end you'll know which one costs more in year two, and I'll show my math."

*Seconds five to ten:* "I'm Taylor Dasch with EG Realty; I sell both, so I don't win either way."

*Scene 1, the pair:* two exteriors, two list prices on screen with the MLS date; the matching rule stated ("within 10% on price, same school district, similar bed and bath count").

*Scene 2, the incentive, read from the sheet:* the builder's current incentive sheet on camera (whatever it says that week; nothing assumed here), then the conditions: preferred lender, points, how long any temporary rate lasts. *Scenario input:* "If the incentive is a 2-1 buydown, here is the payment in year one, year two, and year three at the lender's quoted note rate" (table below; the rate is a labeled illustration until a lender quotes it).

*Scene 3, the tax bill, read from the appraisal district:* the stacked 2025–2026 rates for a Temple address on screen with their sources; the new build's first-year bill if the county assessed land only versus the full value the following year; the resale's reassessment toward the purchase price once the seller's exemption drops off.

*Scene 4, what the inspection and the warranty actually cover:* Taylor's own verification list, filmed at the two homes, and what he would ask for on each (an honest "I don't know until the inspector opens it" is allowed).

*Scene 5, the decision:* the one-page table, three lines of judgment, and the honest downside of each choice.

*Invitation:* "The table is on my site, no form. If you want it run on your own two houses, that's a 30-minute call and you leave with the numbers either way."

## 4. The recording-ready pilot brief

**Working title options**
1. "New Build vs Resale in Temple TX: I Walked a $300K Pair and Ran the Real Numbers"
2. "Same $300K, Two Temple Homes: Which One Costs More in Year Two?"
3. "Temple New Construction vs Resale (2026): The Incentive, the Tax Bill, and the Honest Answer"

**First five seconds:** "Same money, same part of Temple. New on the left, nine years old on the right. One of these costs more in year two, and I'll show my math."

**Name and brokerage inside ten seconds:** "I'm Taylor Dasch with EG Realty. I sell both kinds, so I don't win either way."

**Story spine (chapters):** 1) The pair and the matching rule → 2) The incentive, read from the sheet, with its conditions → 3) The tax bill, read from the appraisal district, year one and year two → 4) Inspection and warranty: what each one really protects → 5) HOA, special districts, lot and neighborhood facts → 6) The table and the decision → 7) The honest downside of the one I'd lean toward → 8) Where to get the table.

**Three consequential decisions the viewer leaves able to make**
1. Whether the incentive is worth more than a resale concession after lender fees and after the temporary period ends.
2. What the real year-two payment is on each home once taxes reset, so the budget is set on year two, not month one.
3. Which protections matter at each home (builder warranty and inspection on the new build; inspection, systems age and negotiation on the resale), and what to ask for in writing.

**Evidence and B-roll requirements (none may be assumed on camera):**
- Two active listings in the same school district within about 10% of each other on price; MLS sheets with print date (addresses on screen only with the listing brokers' consent to feature; otherwise area and price band only).
- The builder's incentive sheet as of the filming week, on camera with its date; the lender-condition language.
- Bell CAD rate screens for one address in each home's taxing units, with the 2025–2026 figures and the date viewed.
- A lender's written quote for the scenario loan (rate, points, MI, date), or, if none is obtained before filming, the on-screen label "illustrative rate, not a quote."
- Exterior and walk-through clips of both homes; the inspection checklist in Taylor's hand; the HOA or PID/MUD disclosure page if any.
- The reusable table (below) rendered as a simple on-screen graphic and as a page on templetxhomes.net.

**On-camera prompts (speak, don't read):**
- "Here's why I matched them this way, and what I couldn't match."
- "Read the incentive sheet out loud and stop at the first condition."
- "Point at the line on the tax screen that changes next year."
- "Say the thing a buyer told you last month that surprised them, without naming them." (Only if true; otherwise skip.)
- "Which one would you lean toward for someone staying five years, and what would make you wrong?"

**Exact verifications before recording**
1. Both listings still active the morning of filming; prices and MLS dates noted.
2. Builder incentive sheet obtained in writing that week; expiry date noted.
3. Bell CAD rates captured for both addresses; the Temple College rate confirmed (not confirmed in this document).
4. Lender quote in writing, or the "illustrative" label prepared.
5. Permission to film inside each home; listing broker consent for the resale; builder consent for the model or spec.
6. TREC advertising rule met on the title card and description: Taylor's name and EG Realty's name at the required prominence.
7. Nothing in the script implies a guaranteed rate, incentive availability, appraisal result or outcome.

## 5. Authenticity, defined operationally

- **Keep:** spontaneous explanation while reading a real document on camera; Taylor's own lean and the sentence that could make him wrong; a visible "I'd have to check" instead of a guess; the uneven pace of walking a house.
- **Remove:** setup before the decision is stated (the first sentence is the question); inaudible audio (re-record the line; a clip-on microphone Taylor already uses for tours is sufficient, no purchase by default); any number that cannot be sourced on screen; stock or rendered houses standing in for Temple.
- **Do not manufacture** mistakes, "bloopers," or shock faces; do not assert that rough footage is always more trusted. **Test polish only where it changes comprehension or trust:** a legible on-screen table, a steady shot of a document, captions on the numbers. Everything else stays as filmed.
- **Thumbnail standard for the pilot:** a real photo of the two homes, one question, Taylor's normal face. This is a test against the current template, not a verdict on it.

## 7. Measurement plan

**Diagnostic (YouTube Studio, per asset, equal days since publication):** first-7-day and first-28-day impressions, click-through, average view duration and percentage, traffic sources, and end-screen/card clicks. Compare the pilot with the September 16 "What to Check" video (same topic family, different format) and with the market update (same era, different packaging). **Delivered value (the real test):** page visits to the table (site analytics), downloads or copies if the page offers one, tagged inquiries that name the video, first consultations held with the appointment ledger's three qualification flags populated, signed clients, and funded transactions by cohort month. A repeat tour is work, not a new household.

**Confounds to state every time:** publication age (the pilot will be younger than its comparators), topic demand (new construction may simply draw more interest than taxes), seasonality (November–December), the thumbnail change on the comparators if the low-cost improvement is applied first (apply it, then date-stamp it so the windows are clean), and attribution (an inquiry that cites the video may have arrived via the page).

**Lag:** buyer-side closings in the ledger show 60–150 days from first contact to funding is ordinary; the pilot's business result is read at 30 days (inquiries, consultations), 90 days (signed), and at the end of Q1 2027 (funded). No statistical winner will be declared on one local pilot; the decision rule is in the owner page.
