## 1. Decision memo: what should you do, what should you stop, and what should you build around?

**Diagnosis.** Earnings are capped by fragmentation, not effort. From September 2025 through August 2026 the business produced 28 closings and $109,194 of known net, about $9,100 a month, from roughly a dozen parallel engines: long-form YouTube, Shorts, site pages, TikTok, BiggerPockets, Facebook, an investor newsletter, cold seller dialing, warm calls, REO, a new-construction campaign, and self-built software. No engine runs at a volume where conversion can be measured; the latest scoreboard shows two new leads in a week. Known net per closing is about $4,200 (calc: $109,194 ÷ 26 closings with recorded net), low against a median sale price near $265,000–$275,000 [R1][R3]. The recorded target of eight held appointments a month, at the source's own ~$1,100 expected value per held appointment, is about $8,800 a month: the status quo. The binding constraint is owner attention spread across sub-scale sources with an unmeasured funnel; the secondary constraint is per-deal economics.

**The one source.** Build around search-intent relocation video on the Living in Temple channel, converting through one relocation-consultation page on templetxhomes.net and the Lofty follow-up you already own. The customer decision: "We are moving to Temple or Belton for a job with a start date. Which area fits, what will it really cost each month, and how do we buy from out of state without getting it wrong?" Initial audience: households relocating for work at Temple's major employers, led by the Baylor Scott & White and VA workforce (7,000+ and about 2,750 employees in Temple [R7][R8]) and the several hundred residents and fellows cycling through roughly 55 graduate-medical-education programs [R9]; Fort Cavazos-connected households who prefer Temple or Belton are served by the same content. This is a marketing focus, not a service restriction.

**Why it beats the strongest alternative.** The strongest alternative is your warm database (829 CRM records, 50 Tier B candidates) worked through the existing relationship-touch program: cheaper and more controllable, but finite, already running under its own review gates, and without recorded closing evidence in the packet. The content group produced 13 deals and 57% of known net at the highest per-deal net among scalable sources ($5,681 for YouTube-attributed deals); the library and attribution plumbing exist; your 2026 relocation video surfaces first in a web search for moving to Temple while competitors are Killeen- or Austin-centric [R30]. Cold outbound loses on net per deal ($3,082 per expired deal), on legal exposure since Texas extended its telephone-solicitation law to text messages on September 1, 2025 [R19], and on willingness. Rental-property investors lose on market economics: at today's Temple rents and a roughly 2.3% non-homestead tax stack, a leveraged rental does not cash flow (calc, section 3), so out-of-state turnkey demand is structurally weak.

**The operating change.** Stop treating activity as the product. Cut production to the September 25 policy (one long-form video, one page, Shorts only when cut from the long-form), aim every video at the relocation decision, define the funnel in the existing appointment ledger, and run a one-hour inquiry-response standard. Stop funding with time: the cold dial ramp (paused 90 days), new software builds, and separate native posting engines. Stop funding with money: nothing new before the day-30 gate.

**Expected benefit.** Working case: about 2.4 closings a month at a blended ~$4,900 known net, roughly $11,900 a month by mid-2027, in fewer hours. Three closings a month by Q1 2027 is reachable only in the upside case.

**Major uncertainty.** Whether the 13 content deals came from a few old videos (survivorship) rather than a repeatable topic formula, and whether the inquiry rate can reach eight to ten a month.

**What would reverse this.** The October 5 YouTube attribution readout showing fewer than six YouTube-attributed closings in the twelve-month cohort, or the appointment ledger showing most held appointments came from past clients and sphere. Either makes the warm database primary and video the maintenance channel.

### The choice, and why the others do not win now

**A wins** because it is the only source with multi-deal evidence at the highest per-deal net among scalable options, it is already built, it matches your sustained willingness to execute, and its weakness (reach) is fixable with focus rather than money.

**B is the strongest alternative and the designated fallback.** It does not win now because the pool is small, it is already running under its own gates, and nothing in the packet shows what it closes. It becomes primary if the ledger shows most held appointments and closings are sphere-sourced (threshold in section 9). Its specific role in this plan: the follow-up muscle. The same daily call window serves inbound relocation follow-up first and relationship touches second.

**C is paused as a growth program, not deleted as a capability.** Lowest net per deal; a buyer's market raises unrecovered listing costs; and legal exposure: soliciting a listing from a FSBO or expired owner on the National Do-Not-Call Registry is prohibited, with FTC penalties up to $53,088 per violation [R20]; Texas Chapter 302 lists no real-estate exemption, requires a $200 registration with $10,000 security, and since September 1, 2025 covers text messages with private actions up to $5,000 per violation [R18][R19]. Warm and inbound follow-up falls under the express-request and existing-relationship exemptions; cold outreach does not. Broker and attorney review precedes any resumption.

**D stays maintenance-only** because the market does not support out-of-state turnkey demand at current prices. Investor cash-flow test (calc; assumptions labeled): a $250,000 house renting at $1,600 a month (above the $1,360 typical asking rent reported for July 2026 [R12]; your own site's $1,500–1,700 figure is not independent) grosses $19,200. Non-homestead tax near 2.3% (Temple ISD $1.0619 [R13] + City $0.6975 [R14] + Bell County $0.3125 [R15] + Temple College ~$0.20, 2023 rate [R16] + county road ~$0.03) is $5,750; insurance $2,200 (assumption); management 10%, maintenance and capital reserve 10%, vacancy 5% total $4,800. Net operating income ≈ $6,450, a 2.6% unlevered yield. A 75% loan at 6.5% (assumption) costs about $14,200 a year, so leveraged cash flow is roughly −$7,800; breakeven rent at that rate is about $2,460 a month. The 1.46% median effective rate reported for Temple [R17] reflects homestead exemptions investors do not get. Qualified and repeat investors still deserve service; they are not a growth engine until rates or prices change.

**E does not win** because a 30–35% referral fee (Realtor.com ReadyConnect) or up to 40% (Zillow Flex) [R27], or ~35% in lender networks per court filings [R28], applied to an already-low $4,200 net leaves roughly $2,700 a deal, builds no owned asset, and dictates your response behavior. Google Local Services Ads is the one paid-intent option worth a bounded pilot later, after the funnel is measured, because it is pay-per-lead and license-screened [R29]; no reliable local cost per lead exists, so it is a day-90 question, not a day-1 one.

**F is the most promising later distribution for the same offer**, not a separate engine. A single, no-fee outreach to the GME office or house-staff association offering the relocation guide costs an hour and could matter from the March match through July 1 start dates. It is sequenced after day 60 only if exposure is working, and it must respect that no referral fee or gift of value can flow to unlicensed people [R21].

### Is the reachable pool economically plausible?

- Temple's population was 96,268 (ACS 2024 1-year); 15.6% (14,689 people) moved in the prior year [R6]. The share arriving from outside the metro is not published in that profile. If one fifth came from outside the metro (assumption), that is roughly 2,900 people or about 1,100 households a year entering Temple alone, before Belton.
- Employer inflow: Baylor Scott & White employs 7,000+ in Temple, McLane 1,700+, the VA about 2,750 [R7][R8]; the Temple campus runs roughly 55 residency and fellowship programs [R9]. Residency and fellowship turnover alone implies well over a hundred incoming households each July (calc from program count; not a published figure).
- Market size: Killeen-Temple MSA closed 1,799 homes in Q2 2025 and 5,370 through October 2025 [R2][R3]; in January 2025, 32% of MSA sales were at $300,000 or above, and the $250,000–$400,000 cohorts had median year built 2019–2020, which means near-new and new construction dominate the relocation price band [R1].
- Viability arithmetic: the working case needs 10 relocation inquiries a month, or 120 a year. Against even 1,100 arriving households a year, that is an 11% inquiry share of the out-of-area pool, which is demanding but not implausible for the channel that already ranks for the core query. The conservative case (6 a month) needs 6.5%. This is the gap to validate, and it is validated by counting inquiries, not views.

### Minimum viable playbook

- **Identify opportunities:** inquiries arrive through the page form, YouTube comments and DMs, email, and calls. Each is tagged with source and video in Lofty and the appointment ledger. Comments that ask a question get a public answer plus an invitation to the call.
- **Deliver:** the call, the written shortlist, and the cost model. Nothing bespoke beyond that until a signed agreement.
- **Response workflow:** first reply within one business hour (next morning for after-hours), by the inquirer's channel; offer two call slots; if no reply, follow-ups on day 1, 3, 7, 14, then monthly market note. All texts go only to people who asked to be contacted; opt-out honored immediately.
- **Qualify (proceed):** confirmed employment or orders, move window inside nine months, price band inside the inventory that exists, willing to speak with a lender, buying in Temple/Belton or within about 35 minutes.
- **Disqualify or defer:** no timeline beyond twelve months (nurture monthly), investors requesting analysis without completing the screened packet (send the packet), areas outside the radius (refer to a licensed agent for a referral fee paid through your broker), rent-only needs (send a resource; keep in the nurture list).
- **Next-step standard:** every held call ends with a dated next action and a ledger entry; absence of an entry is treated as a process miss, not as proof nothing happened.
- **Warm versus unsolicited:** a web inquiry or comment is an express request; calling or texting it back is exempt from Texas Chapter 302 and the DNC rules [R18][R19]. Nobody from a purchased list, an expired list, or a scraped roster is contacted under this source.

## 5. What would the economics and cash reality look like?

### Definitions used throughout

- **Gross compensation (G):** negotiated buyer-side or listing-side compensation on the contract.
- **Brokerage and referral deductions:** split, transaction fees, and any referral fee. Your split is not in the packet; it is an input (s = your share after split).
- **Direct deal costs (d):** photography, signage, client gifts, mileage, unrecovered listing expenses.
- **Known net (N):** the packet's historical figure. Assumption: it is your side after brokerage deductions and before overhead, acquisition, and taxes. If "known net" already deducts overhead, the model below understates your margin; confirm in the GCI tracker.
- **Owner cash:** N minus business fixed costs (B), acquisition and test spend (X), and tax provision (T).
- Formula per closing: N = G × s − d. Historical average N ≈ $4,200 (calc). Illustrative relocation closing: price $330,000 × 2.5% × s 0.75 − $300 ≈ $5,900 (assumption on every input; shown to make the lever visible, not as a forecast).

## 10. What could make this plan wrong?

### The strongest case against it

Content earned 57% of known net, but over 222 videos and several years, and reach is small and flat. The 13 content deals may trace to two or three older videos (survivorship), and last-touch attribution may credit a video for a past client's referral. The held appointments you actually produce probably come from people who already know you, and a buyer's market with active listings up 25% and 112 days on market [R3] is when a disciplined dial week on expireds and the warm database produces listings faster than a video produces a relocation closing. On that reading, B is primary now, cold outbound stays at policy intensity, and video is a maintenance halo.

That case has real force. It does not win today because the packet holds no evidence of what the database closes, cold outbound's economics are the worst in the ledger and its legal exposure has grown, and the day-30 and day-90 gates let the ledger, not preference, decide. Where I may be overweighting: your stated preference for inbound, inherited AI operating documents that already assume content, a 28-closing sample, and my own familiarity with relocation channels. Most exposed assumption: six to ten qualified inquiries a month at the channel's current scale.

### Does the plan survive the stress cases?

- **Less owner time:** the compressed week keeps response, client service, and one video; everything else drops. The plan degrades to maintenance rather than breaking.
- **Slower conversions:** the 90-day cash sketch already assumes no new-source closings in the quarter; the reserve rule exists for this.
- **Weaker economics:** if net per relocation closing comes in at the historical $4,200 rather than $5,900, the working case falls to about $9,650 a month, roughly flat to history; the plan then earns its keep only through fewer hours, and the price-band focus becomes the repair.
- **Platform disruption:** library content lives on the site; inquiries live in Lofty with consent; B takes over inside a week with no new assets. Evidence threshold for switching: twelve weeks under the pause rule above, or the October 5 readout result described in the memo.

### Three implications that change how you run the business

1. **The appointment target reproduces the status quo.** Mechanism: 8 held × ~$1,100 ≈ $8,800 a month, the current run rate. Evidence: the packet's own figures. Consequence: appointments must change in kind (source intent and price band), not in count. Uncertainty: the $1,100 estimate's cohort and deductions are unknown.
2. **Rental-property investors cannot be the growth engine at a 2.3% non-homestead tax stack and today's rents.** Mechanism: a 2.6% unlevered yield and negative leveraged cash flow (section 3 calc) mean out-of-state turnkey demand is structurally thin, and the inquiries that do arrive skew toward unqualified analysis requests. Consequence: investors stay screened and repeat-client oriented; your analytical skill is more valuable in a relocating household's monthly-cost model. Uncertainty: the insurance and management assumptions, and any rate or price change.
3. **Cold outbound is now the highest-legal-risk source per dollar earned, and warm follow-up is exempt by design.** Mechanism: Texas Chapter 302 registration with security, the September 1, 2025 extension to texts and $5,000-per-violation private actions [R18][R19], and federal DNC penalties up to $53,088 per violation [R20], against $3,082 per expired deal. Consequence: the call window should be spent answering people who asked to hear from you. Uncertainty: your brokerage may already hold a registration and a compliant process; broker review decides.
