## 3. What is the thesis, and what would reverse it?

**Thesis.** The next iteration should spend owner effort on fewer, better-evidenced long-form decision videos for Temple–Belton buyers, each tied to one existing page and one invitation, with intake capture so the video can be credited. This should create more suitable conversations per unit of effort because (a) the buyer-side transactions in the ledger came from this kind of intent, not from tour traffic; (b) the evergreen Temple search results are held by dated 2022–2023 overviews from Austin- and Killeen-based channels, and Taylor's own January 2026 decision video shows a current local video can take that position; (c) decision videos produce a natural, non-pushy next step (a bounded consultation about the viewer's own numbers), whereas news Shorts and tours do not; (d) the same research feeds a website page, so the work compounds across two owned channels.

**Strongest competing approach:** high-frequency Shorts and property tours (the Brina Gordon and Living in Central Texas pattern, and Taylor's own September cadence). It generates more impressions per hour of filming and keeps the channel visible. It loses on intent: the channel's best-viewed Shorts are about data centers, a topic that attracts residents and opponents, not buyers choosing an agent, and tours advertise one listing rather than a decision.

**Evidence that would reverse the choice:** a YouTube Studio export showing tour or Short-driven sessions producing more tagged inquiries per owner hour than decision videos over the 90-day window; decision videos failing to beat tours on first-28-day impressions and click-through at equal days since publication; or the intake question showing that inquiries cite Shorts, not long-form.

**Website organic and native TikTok/Instagram** are support and independent paths, not forbidden and not equal campaigns: every video gets its page (the page is the conversion asset and an organic source in its own right, L/D014, D017, D019); phone-shot verticals continue as natural capture when Taylor is already in the field, with no quota and no separate production program.

### Brief 1 — Where to buy in Temple–Belton on a $250K–$350K budget (2026)

- **Buyer decision:** which three or four areas fit a stated budget, commute and home age, before touring anything.
- **Evidence required:** current MLS actives by area in the band (counts and median list, pulled by Taylor); 2025–2026 stacked tax rates by city and school district from Bell CAD and the taxing units; HOA presence by subdivision; two measured drives (Taylor's car, 7:15 a.m., one weekday) to the two largest employers he chooses; flood-zone check per area from FEMA's map service; new-build share by area from MLS.
- **Tentative title:** "Where to Buy in Temple–Belton on a $250K–$350K Budget (2026)". Alternatives: "Temple vs Belton at $300K: Which Areas Actually Fit" · "$300K in Temple TX: 4 Areas, Taxes and Commutes Compared".
- **Thumbnail concept:** a simple map with four labeled areas and one bold price band; Taylor at the edge; no arrows, no red circles.
- **First 5-second hook:** "Three hundred thousand dollars buys four very different lives in Temple and Belton. Here's which one fits your commute."
- **Entity in first 10 seconds:** "I'm Taylor Dasch with EG Realty, and I live and work here."
- **Chapter logic:** 1) the budget and what it buys now; 2) the four areas with home age, taxes and HOA; 3) the two measured drives; 4) what to verify before an offer; 5) the next step.
- **Firsthand input Taylor supplies:** which areas he would not recommend for which buyer and why; what he has seen go wrong at each price point; his own drive times and traffic notes.
- **Exact useful outcome for the viewer:** a written area shortlist by budget and commute they can use without calling anyone.
- **Proof / B-roll shot list:** dashcam drive clips with time overlays; walking shots at one street per area; a screenshot of the tax-rate table; a one-page shortlist graphic.
- **Reuse:** the shortlist becomes a page on templetxhomes.net; one 45-second vertical per area from the same footage; the tax-rate table reused in Brief 2.
- **Invitation:** section 7 wording; the page link and intake question in the description and pinned comment.
- **Owner / assistant work:** Taylor: MLS pulls, drives, filming, the judgment calls, final review. Assistant: research brief with sources, outline, tax-rate table draft, description and chapters, page draft, Short cut list.

### Brief 2 — The real monthly cost of a Temple home (2026): three verified examples

- **Buyer decision:** whether a given price is affordable after the property-tax reassessment, insurance and any concession.
- **Evidence required:** three de-identified active listings in different price bands; Bell CAD rates for each; the Texas rule that a buyer's taxable value resets toward purchase price without the seller's exemption; an insurance agent's quoted range for each (Taylor obtains or shows the request); a lender's current rate quote with date, or a clearly labeled illustrative rate; the typical concession seen locally, cited to the local source with its date.
- **Tentative title:** "The Real Monthly Payment on a Temple TX Home (2026): 3 Examples, Taxes Included". Alternatives: "Why Your Temple Payment Jumps After Closing (With Real Numbers)" · "$275K in Temple: What It Actually Costs Each Month".
- **Thumbnail:** one number per example in a three-column card; Taylor pointing at the middle one.
- **Hook:** "Two of these three homes cost more per month than the listing suggests, and the reason is on your tax bill."
- **Entity:** named in the first sentence after the hook.
- **Chapters:** 1) the three homes; 2) principal and interest at today's quote; 3) taxes the right way; 4) insurance; 5) the concession scenario; 6) cash to close; 7) what to ask your lender.
- **Firsthand input:** cases where a client's payment surprised them and how it was caught; which assumptions Taylor insists on checking.
- **Outcome:** a payment worksheet the viewer can replicate.
- **Proof / B-roll:** screen recordings of the CAD rate lookup; a worksheet overlay; exterior shots of similar homes (not the specific listings if clients are involved).
- **Reuse:** the worksheet page; a 60-second vertical on the tax jump; the sequel to the September 22 video gets a card and end-screen link from it.
- **Owner / assistant:** Taylor: listing selection, lender and insurer calls, filming, review. Assistant: worksheet with formulas, research notes with sources, chapters, description.
- **Provisional effort:** owner 4–5 h; assistant 3 h. **Displaces:** the monthly market-update video.

### Brief 3 — New build vs resale in Temple: a matched $300K pair walked side by side

## 6. The next step up: what actually raises quality

Quality here means more suitable conversations per video, not polish. Ranked by expected contribution:

1. **Decision demonstrations** (Briefs 1, 3, 11): show the choice being made with real constraints. Highest fit with the buyer-side transactions in the ledger.
2. **Verified cost inputs** (Brief 2): every number has a source and date on screen. This is the gap in every competitor sample found.
3. **Matched-property comparisons** (Briefs 3, 11): two homes, same money. Requires access coordination; strongest proof of process.
4. **Field tests** (Briefs 1, 5): measured drives with time overlays. Cheap, differentiating, reusable.
5. **Real client questions** (anonymized) as chapter openers; ask permission or generalize.
6. **Natural delivery and proof of process:** Taylor on site, reading the tax screen, not a studio. Cinematic production, length and staged mistakes are not quality signals and are not recommended.

## 7. From discovery to client: the path and its parts

**Path:** existing video → the relevant existing page on templetxhomes.net (the area shortlist, the cost worksheet, the new-build checklist) → one helpful first reply within the business day → a bounded consultation (30 minutes, about the viewer's own numbers) or the resource itself → an agreed next step recorded in the appointment ledger → written buyer agreement before showings → service → funded transaction → review and introduction ask.

**Public value stands on its own.** Each video ends with the artifact the viewer came for (shortlist, worksheet, checklist) available on the page without a form. The consultation is offered, not required.

**Platform rules checked October 1, 2026:** end screens need a video of at least 25 seconds; the "Link" element to an external website is available only to YouTube Partner Program members [P1]; YPP requires 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days [P2]. At 243 subscribers the channel is **not eligible** for external end-screen links, so the path uses: the page link as the first line of the description, a pinned comment with the same link and the intake question, a spoken invitation, and end-screen video/playlist elements pointing to the next decision video. Impressions, click-through, average view duration and traffic sources are available to every channel in YouTube Studio, with long-form and Shorts reported separately [P3], so measurement does not depend on YPP.

**Attribution preserved:** the intake question ("Which video or page brought you here?") is asked in every first reply and on the form; the appointment ledger's existing fields (source, exact asset, source-evidence locator) are populated at intake under the August 15 rules (L §4); assists are recorded as assists, not promoted to origin; D029 stays "owner-reported tentative" until the process accepts evidence.

**Do not:** change titles daily; alter a running experiment's packaging mid-window to earn activity credit; add a booking wall.

**Best economically justified design:** eight to ten decision videos in 90 days at the Brief 1–3 standard, each with its page and intake capture; an editor for the assembly cut once the Lanes log shows editing is the owner's largest production lane; a lav microphone and a dashcam mount if not already owned (cost variables C_mic, C_mount); no paid promotion in the window.

**Conditions to hire an editor:** two consecutive months in which (a) the Lanes log shows editing above a share of production time Taylor sets, (b) tagged video inquiries are at least two per month with at least one first consultation, and (c) cash after obligations covers the editor's monthly cost for the next three months (figures from Taylor's own records; none assumed). **Condition to stop buying polish:** two videos with upgraded production show no improvement in first-28-day CTR, average view duration or tagged inquiries against the plain versions.
