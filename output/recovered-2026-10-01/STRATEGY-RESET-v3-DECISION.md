### Keep, repair, pause, scale

- **Keep** when video-sourced first consultations run at least two a month over eight weeks and the signed field is populated, whatever the funding count; funding lags.
- **Repair** when inquiries exist but consultations do not (response or offer), when consultations exist but agreements do not (consult content, agreement presentation), or when agreements exist but funding fails at unusual rates (qualification, financing, buyer's-market fallout). Fix the one stage.
- **Pause the development priority** when, after twelve weeks of weekly publishing with the intake fields populated, video-sourced first consultations stay under one a month, or when the Lanes log shows production displacing client service. Then the challenger decision in section 3 applies.
- **Scale** (editing help, then a bounded paid pilot) only when the section 5 gate is met with real cash figures.
- Before changing anything, name the cause: no video published (execution); videos published, no inquiries (exposure); inquiries without fit (fit); consults without agreements inside 30 days (slow progression, wait); agreements and closings with thin retained payout (economics).

### The strongest case against this choice

The four YouTube rows could be the product of older videos on a channel whose continuity is unestablished (L §3), while the current channel's reported week shows four uploads for five subscribers (H3). Meanwhile the ledger shows that the most completely qualified current household came from a referral network and that two expired sellers are at or near signing. In a market with rising inventory and falling sales, listings are the controllable work, and a disciplined dial block costs nothing. If the expired rows' low retention came from the old split rather than discounted fees, post-cap listings could out-earn buyer-side video deals per transaction, with shorter attribution gaps and no platform risk. This document holds against that case only because the retained-economics question is unanswered and the compliance cost of outbound has risen; both are verifiable within 30 days, and the plan is built to switch if they resolve the other way.

### Three implications worth acting on

1. **Retention ratio, not lead count, is the biggest unexplained number in the ledger.** Buyer-side digital rows retain roughly twice the share of price that outbound seller rows do (2.33% vs 1.16% medians, Q-recalc). If that is a listing-fee pattern, fee discipline on listings is worth more than any new source; if it is a split artifact, seller work is undervalued here. Uncertainty: cause unknown until payout documents are read.
2. **The second half of the ledger was carried by a mix of owned and gatekept sources.** March–August 2026 produced 17 rows and $71,304 against 11 rows and $37,890 in the prior six months (Q-recalc), with REO and RealEstateAgents.com supplying five of those rows. Protecting gatekept flow (fast response, good standing, 35% fee accepted) matters as much in the next 90 days as developing the owned source. Uncertainty: two unknown Income rows sit in that half.
3. **Two rule changes make buyer-side compensation contractual and texting riskier at the same time.** Texas SB 1968 requires written buyer agreements with stated compensation from January 1, 2026 [P5], and SB 140 brought texts under the telephone-solicitation law from September 1, 2025 [P3]. The first protects buyer-side retention in a buyer's market; the second raises the cost of cold outreach. Both favor a source whose first contact is an inbound request and whose agreement is explained before the first showing. Uncertainty: broker policy and the exact TREC implementation of SB 1968 (the "showing-only" boundary) need broker confirmation.
