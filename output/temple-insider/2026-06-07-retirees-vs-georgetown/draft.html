If you're deciding where to spend retirement in Central Texas, the default answer everyone gives is Georgetown's Sun City. Here's the case for looking 35 minutes north first — and the one honest reason you might not.

Most "best places to retire" lists rank towns on golf courses and walkability. The retirees who actually move here optimize for two things that matter far more as you age: how fast you can see a specialist, and how much of your fixed income the tax bill quietly eats. On both, Temple wins — and almost nobody talks about it.

The healthcare almost nobody factors in

Temple is built around Baylor Scott & White — a 636-bed Level I trauma center, the only one on I-35 between Dallas and Austin, with 30+ specialties on one campus. Specialist waits run about 2–3 weeks here vs. 4–6 weeks in Austin. For veterans, the Olin E. Teague VA Medical Center is in town too. When healthcare becomes your #1 real priority — and in retirement it usually does — this is the whole ballgame.

The money math

  • No Texas state income tax on Social Security, pensions, or retirement withdrawals.
  • Over-65 school-tax freeze: once you turn 65, the school portion of your property tax is locked for life — roughly $2,400–$3,200/yr saved on a $300K home. It even transfers to your next Texas home.
  • Cost-of-living index ~82–86 (national average = 100); median home around $255K.

The honest catch

Temple does not have a large 55+ age-restricted community like Sun City — no gated, golf-cart, activities-director setup. If that resort-style community is the thing you're actually buying, Georgetown is the better call, and I'll tell you that straight. Temple is a medical and working city, not a resort town. (Two more honest notes: the summers are hot, and you'll fly out of Austin or Waco.)

Who this is really for

Retirees who want top-tier healthcare, a low fixed-cost lifestyle, and a normal neighborhood with single-story options — think Bella Terra, Lake Pointe, Three Creeks, or Canyon Creek — instead of an amenity bubble.
