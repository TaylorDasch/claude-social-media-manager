## Scope and method

This lane reviewed every currently accessible public upload in the requested two-year window, 2024-07-24 through 2026-07-24 inclusive: **62 videos** (all channel uploads available in that window). For each, the reviewer retrieved the video metadata and description with `yt-dlp`, retrieved the available automatic English captions, and scanned the description and captions for fair-housing, advertising, lending, and disclosure issues. Titles and descriptions were reviewed as advertising copy as well.

One same-day scheduled premiere (`ZHorotwPvVQ`) was returned by YouTube as “Premieres in 29 minutes,” without retrievable metadata/captions. It is not counted as a reviewed public upload and needs a post-premiere addendum.

This is an evidence and risk review, not a legal conclusion. “Potential violation” means the excerpt is the kind of public housing preference statement that merits broker/counsel review; it does not establish enforcement liability or intent. I did **not** auto-flag factual school, crime, healthcare, military, or geographic-origin discussion. The 2026 HUD-policy nuance does not erase the Fair Housing Act’s advertising prohibition or Texas Rule 531.19’s protected-class preference bar.

Primary rules used:

- Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. §3604(c): housing notices/advertisements may not indicate a preference, limitation, or discrimination based on familial status.
- TREC Rule 531.19: license holders may not make a disclosure that indicates or is intended to indicate a preference, limitation, or discrimination based on the listed protected classes, including familial status.
- TREC Rule 535.155: real-estate advertisements require the license holder/team name and broker name in a readily noticeable location; social-media account/profile linking may satisfy the placement rule.
- TREC Rules 531.18 and 531.20: CPN and completed IABS link rules for a social-media account used as a business website.

Official guidance checked: [TREC social-media rules article](https://www.trec.texas.gov/article/what-you-need-know-comply-our-social-media-rules), [TREC advertising FAQ](https://www.trec.texas.gov/node/323), and [TREC IABS guidance](https://www.trec.texas.gov/node/743).

## Evidence and verification limits

- Raw metadata and captions are retained under `raw/aundrea/` in this audit folder. Auto captions can mis-transcribe; direct video links and timestamps are provided so a human can verify audio before taking action.
- This was a transcript/description review. Thumbnails, title cards, lower-thirds, end cards, maps, on-screen listing status, and other visual-only content need a separate visual pass. No visual-only issue is alleged here.
- The live `livinginctx.com` home page was checked on 2026-07-24 and contained direct footer links labeled “TREC Consumer Protection Notice” and “TREC Information About Brokerage Services.” Both description short links checked resolved to IABS PDFs. The About page text itself did not expose the broker or notice links to this retrieval method.
- No automatic finding was created merely for school references, crime/safety topics, medical-profession relocation, military relocation, California-origin targeting, or a claim being favorable/negative. Those need false/misleading-claim evidence or a protected-class preference context before escalation.

## Practical remediation pattern

Replace audience-preference language with objective property/location facts: commute time, lot size, beds/baths, HOA terms, actual school-district boundary (with an official link), parks, and price. For example, “family-friendly neighborhood” can become “neighborhood with a community park, sidewalks, and [named] HOA amenities.” Avoid tying a home, neighborhood, or city to families, retirees, age groups, or another protected-class proxy.
