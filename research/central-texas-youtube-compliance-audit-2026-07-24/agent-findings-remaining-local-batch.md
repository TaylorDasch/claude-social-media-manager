## Scope, result, and important limitation

This is a public-content compliance **risk review**, not legal advice or a finding that anyone violated TREC rules, Fair Housing law, RESPA, FTC rules, mortgage-advertising rules, or any other law. No person should be reported, contacted, or characterized as noncompliant based solely on this document. The items below identify statements that a broker or counsel should check against the full video, the applicable agreement/disclosure package, source materials, and the facts at publication.

The complete public channel catalog was enumerated without cookies (2,860 unique historical video IDs across the sixteen named channels and their Videos/Shorts tabs). YouTube then rate-limited no-cookie individual-video and caption retrieval across this shared audit session, returning “Sign in to confirm you’re not a bot.” This prevented a truthful claim that every in-window description or caption transcript was reviewed. The no-cookie fallback used each channel’s public RSS feed (which supplies the latest 15 uploads with published dates) and the public channel descriptions contained in the channel index.

Accordingly, this is a **partial evidence audit with a complete retrieval-gap ledger**, not a completed transcript-by-transcript audit of the batch. Only title/feed/channel-description evidence that was actually retrievable is used below. No timestamp is invented where a transcript could not be retrieved.

For Fair Housing context, HUD’s April 24, 2026 public position is that discussion of school or crime information alone is not a Fair Housing Act violation; intentional discriminatory steering is required. No school/crime item below is called a Fair Housing violation. ## Review flags based on retrievable public evidence

### RL-01 — $62,000 military-buyer savings result needs source and incentive disclosure review

**Channel/video:** Living at Fort Hood / Hanna Smelser — [“PCSing to Fort Hood? Here’s How our Military Buyers Saved $62K on New Construction!”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r71Ud2xcSM4)  
**Published:** May 16, 2026 (public RSS)  
**Severity:** Moderate–High  
**Confidence:** High that the public title makes the stated $62K result claim; low on any legal conclusion because no description/caption or deal documents were retrievable.

**Evidence:** The public title says: “PCSing to Fort Hood? Here’s How our Military Buyers Saved $62K on New Construction!”

**Concern label:** outcome/consumer-savings substantiation; builder incentive / preferred-lender / FTC-material-terms review; possible RESPA/settlement-payment context review.

**Why review is appropriate:** A six-figure-adjacent savings statement targeting military PCS buyers may create an expectation of a typical or readily available buyer outcome. The title does not tell the viewer whether this is a price reduction, seller concession, builder incentive, rate buydown, lender credit, commission rebate, combination, or a comparison against an assumed alternative. It also does not identify loan, credit, price, timing, builder, or eligibility conditions.

**Missing context before any conclusion:** full transcript and on-screen disclosures; source and calculation for $62K; whether it was a one-off transaction; builder and lender terms; borrower qualification, loan type, rate/APR/points, lock period, and credit/down-payment assumptions; whether a preferred lender was required; and any settlement-payment or rebate documentation.

### RL-02 — 2.99% rate / $50 down / free appliances / closing-cost promotion needs mortgage-ad material-term review

**Channel/video:** Jessica Doree — [“#Bloop #Topwater szn & 2.99% rate won’t last! As low as $50 down FREE appliances, closing costs PAID”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=biq0haoCeiM)  
**Published:** May 13, 2025 (public RSS)  
**Severity:** High  
**Confidence:** High on the title’s finance/incentive language; low on an ultimate Truth in Lending, RESPA, TREC, or FTC conclusion because visuals and terms were unavailable.

**Evidence:** The public title says: “2.99% rate won’t last! As low as $50 down FREE appliances, closing costs PAID.”

**Concern label:** mortgage-advertising / TILA-Regulation Z review; incentive material-terms review; potentially misleading-advertising review.

**Why review is appropriate:** Rate, down-payment, and payment-of-closing-costs claims are material to a consumer’s financing decision. A title can be viewed without opening the video, while it supplies none of the information needed to understand the rate program or the availability of the incentives. The short-term urgency phrase “won’t last” further increases the need for a dated, supportable offer and clear availability conditions.

**Missing context before any conclusion:** lender/builder identity; whether this is an interest rate or APR; loan term/type, points, lock expiration, credit score, debt-to-income/down-payment assumptions, property/builder eligibility, who pays the stated costs, cash-versus-credit limits, geographic limitations, final-video visual disclosures, and the offer date/expiration.

### RL-03 — “#1 RV Resort in Texas” is an objective ranking claim needing a supportable basis

**Channel/video:** Sandee Payne — [“Invest in Tiny Homes & Lots at FireFly RV Resort, Fredericksburg, TX | #1 RV Resort in Texas”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fks5Vr6nQHo)  
**Published:** July 28, 2024 (public RSS)  
**Severity:** Moderate  
**Confidence:** High that the title contains the claimed ranking; medium on advertising risk because “#1” may have a substantiated source or may be non-actionable puffery depending on context.

**Evidence:** The public title includes “#1 RV Resort in Texas.”

**Concern label:** TREC/FTC objective-ranking and substantiation review; investment-marketing review.

**Why review is appropriate:** “#1” is more objectively testable than ordinary opinion language. If based on a third-party ranking, it should be identified, current, and accurately characterized. If it refers to occupancy, sales, amenities, reviews, revenue, or a specific publication’s ranking, that qualification matters. The word “Invest” also makes it important not to imply a return, liquidity, appreciation, or suitability without clear basis/limits.

**Missing context:** source, methodology, date, category, and geographic comparison for “#1”; whether the creator received compensation/referral consideration from the resort/developer; offering documents; risks, projected-income basis, property-management/HOA constraints, securities/real-estate-investment characterization, and the full video/disclosures.

### RL-04 — Potential stale listing-status review queue

**Channels/videos:**

- Sandee Payne — [“2250 Hilltop Loop, Killeen Texas- For SALE”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ofQdNt_BPWg), January 26, 2025 (public RSS).
- Sandee Payne — [“2536 Red Fern Drive, Harker Heights”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BO5W6XKvDUY), February 17, 2025 (public RSS).
- People 1st / Gloria Watson — [“ACTIVE LISTING - 513 E 27th St. Belton, TX - People 1st Realty”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DXNFOvcHfeI), July 1, 2026 (public RSS).

**Severity:** Moderate for the January 2025 explicit “For SALE” post; Low for the remaining examples pending factual status confirmation.  
**Confidence:** High as to the titles/publication dates; low as to any current listing status.

**Concern label:** TREC misleading/stale-advertisement review.

**Why review is appropriate:** A public video titled “For SALE” remains discoverable well after publication. The audit has no live MLS or current-listing-status verification for these properties and did not retrieve the video descriptions/pinned comments. A closed, withdrawn, expired, or materially changed listing that is still represented as presently for sale can be misleading. This is a verification queue, not a finding that any referenced property is no longer available.

**Missing context:** current MLS/listing status; all title/description/pinned-comment edits; whether the video includes a dated or status-updated disclosure; listing authorization; and live availability at the time of the audit.

### RL-05 — “Best single family deal” needs a defined comparison if used as an objective market claim

**Channel/video:** Samantha Hammonds — [“The best single family deal in Killeen | PCS to Fort Cavazos | Fully updated house for sale!”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jcX1O0LIG1k)  
**Published:** March 10, 2025 (public RSS)  
**Severity:** Low–Moderate  
**Confidence:** High on title wording; low–medium on risk because “best” may be obvious puffery rather than a factual ranking.

**Evidence:** The public title calls the property “The best single family deal in Killeen.”

**Concern label:** TREC misleading / objective-comparison substantiation review.

**Why review is appropriate:** If “best” conveys a comparative value claim rather than sales puffery, the basis needs to be defined (price, condition, location, return, size, days on market, or some other comparator) and current. The video title also states “house for sale,” which should receive the same current-status check described in RL-04.

**Missing context:** exact comparative basis, market/date boundary, property status, source data, full video qualification, and whether the statement was plainly framed as opinion.

## Items not treated as findings

- The channel/profile phrase “families,” “military families,” “first-time buyers,” or military PCS assistance was seen in several public descriptions. Standing alone, those references do not establish a Fair Housing violation. The no-cookie transcript gap prevents a defensible conclusion on any viewer-specific steering communication.
- ELITE Realty’s public profile says it helps identify “strong, cash-flowing opportunities.” That is too general, without a particular promised return, property, or transcript evidence, to support an investment-performance finding in this limited pass.
- New Builds in Central Texas has public builder-interview titles. An interview or builder relationship alone does not prove compensation, a referral arrangement, or a RESPA issue.
- No direct transcript evidence was retrieved for a school/crime/location “avoid” recommendation in this batch. None is alleged here.

## Recommended closeout actions

1. Retry the no-cookie individual metadata/caption pass after YouTube’s bot rate limit clears, using a slow serial request rate. Preserve a per-video success/unavailable ledger and read captions for each upload whose title/description contains financing, incentives, rankings, school/crime, family preference, investment return, or listing-status language.
2. Run a visual QA of each video and Short that is an advertisement: name of license holder/team and broker in a readily noticeable location; current phone/site information; lender/loan disclosure overlay; and updates to inactive property tours.
3. For RL-01/RL-02, obtain the exact builder/lender terms and the final published asset before determining the applicable mortgage-advertising, RESPA, TREC, or FTC treatment.
4. For RL-03/RL-05, retain the factual source/date/methodology for any objective ranking or comparative “best” claim, or revise it to clearly marked opinion.
5. Verify and update/remove old “For Sale,” “Active,” price, incentive, and availability claims when the underlying listing changes status.
