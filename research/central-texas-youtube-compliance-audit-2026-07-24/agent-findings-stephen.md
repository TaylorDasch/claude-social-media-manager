This is a public-content risk review, not legal advice and not a determination that any item is a legal, TREC, Fair Housing, RESPA, Truth in Lending, NMLS, or FTC violation. Video images, lower thirds, spoken qualifiers outside the quoted segment, audiences, underlying data, lender/builder agreements, and historical versions can change the analysis.

The review uses the current HUD position published April 24, 2026: discussion of school and crime information alone is not a Fair Housing Act violation; an actionable claim requires intentional discriminatory steering. That limits the conclusions here. The school/crime/family-language items below are therefore presented as elevated advertising/steering-review risk, **not** definitive Fair Housing findings. ## Review flags

### SH-01 — “Up to $5,000” builder-paid commission rebate without public material terms

**Severity:** Moderate–High  
**Confidence:** High that the quoted promise is public and lacks terms on the reviewed About page/linked site; low on any ultimate RESPA, state-law, or lender-compliance conclusion.  
**Labels:** TREC/FTC material-terms review; settlement-payment/RESPA review; consumer-disclosure review.

**Evidence:** [Real Estate Steph CTX About](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCvwxIfW2u71mpzrKastNbDg/about):

> “As a thank you, our subscribers who buy with us can receive up to a $5,000 cash rebate from our builder-paid commission. Let us pay YOU to buy a home.”

The reviewed linked site did not surface public rebate terms, eligibility rules, exclusions, financing/lender conditions, offer duration, or calculation method.

**Why this merits review:** “Up to” cash/commission-rebate offers are material incentives. The public statement does not tell a consumer who qualifies, what a buyer must do, which builders/listings are included, whether the amount is typical, how the rebate is calculated, whether a preferred lender is required, whether lender/settlement approval is needed, or which transaction constraints apply. A broker/lender/settlement-payment review should establish whether the offer is lawful, funded and disclosed correctly, and advertised consistently.

**Missing context needed before any conclusion:** written rebate policy; brokerage and builder agreements; buyer representation agreement; lender and closing-agent approval; loan-program restrictions; geographic/state restrictions; availability/caps; amount calculation; whether the payment is a credit at closing or post-closing payment; and any required finance/settlement disclosures.

### SH-03 — Seller-performance and market-statistic claims need substantiation/qualification

**Severity:** Moderate  
**Confidence:** High that the claims were spoken; medium on risk because data may be supportable.

**Evidence:** [“Central Texas Homeowners Should Watch This Closely”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5i1Q2eGhiS0) (January 14, 2026):

| Timestamp | Public statement |
|---|---|
| 00:08:45 | “right now it’s at 92.5” (discussing list-to-close percentage) |
| 00:09:08 | “little cheat code. If you get close to that 7 and a half% less than market value when you price, you will likely sell very very fast.” |
| 00:09:40 | “There is a strategy that works.” |
| 00:09:44 | “My home sellers actually get 100% on average. So the average is actually 99.7…” |
| 00:10:00 | “because we follow a strategy and a system that works.” |

The description also states that most sellers are losing 7.5% of equity and sitting 100+ days, that his sellers average 99.7 list-to-close versus a 92.5 average, and suggests a rate-forecast scenario.

**Why this merits review:** “100% on average,” “99.7,” “92.5,” and “will likely sell very very fast” can be understood as comparative performance or outcome claims. The video/description reviewed does not state the market boundary, time period, property categories, source, sample size, calculation method, or whether the client result represents all relevant listings. “Cheat code” and guaranteed-sounding system language can increase misleading-advertising risk if the data/qualifiers are unavailable.

**Missing context:** source report/MLS export; date window; list-to-sale versus list-to-close definition; geography/property-type filters; count and inclusion/exclusion rules for “my home sellers”; proof that comparisons use matching cohorts; and a clearer non-guarantee qualification.

#### B. Supporting pattern examples

[“Safest Places to Live in Fort Hood Texas”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QdF070z89d0) (January 25, 2025) says at 00:00:56–00:01:42 that the speaker is not a crime expert, does not steer people to/from neighborhoods, uses NeighborhoodScout.com, and asks viewers to cross-reference. Later it nevertheless describes areas as “the safest Killeen area neighborhoods” (00:03:19), “Harker Heights Southwest is the safest” (00:07:31), and “one of safest places you can be in the nation” (00:14:10).

[“Most People Moving to Fort Hood Pick the Wrong Neighborhood (Here’s a Better One)”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GIHqfOVHm7E) (February 21, 2026) calls the featured place a “great place for kids” with “top-rated schools” (00:00:27), says more “families” are choosing it (00:00:51), and describes an “amazing place for families” / “family-oriented community” (00:04:09).

[“I Found the PERFECT STARTER HOME for a Growing Family in Round Rock”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YVThYKqdxW8) (February 14, 2026) says “perfect for the large or growing family” (00:00:09) and “especially if you are a growing family” (00:01:05).

**Why this merits review:** Current HUD guidance means these statements do not alone establish a Fair Housing Act violation. Still, direct “avoid” instructions, repeated crime/safety and school rankings, subjective descriptors such as “ghetto,” and family-targeted housing language are a high-scrutiny pattern for a real-estate advertiser. The risk is amplified if advice varies by a viewer’s protected characteristic, if data is selectively used, or if the claims result in steering. “Family-oriented”/“for growing families” can also create an avoidable familial-status preference appearance in dwelling advertising.

**Missing context:** data sources and dates for each school/crime claim; whether recommendations are provided consistently to all consumers; viewer-specific communications; intent; full scripts/edits; any neutral resource links; and broker/counsel policy for discussing schools, crime, and protected-class-adjacent requests.

**Safer direction for counsel/broker review:** frame location choices around neutral, consistently applied objective criteria; identify source/date and invite viewers to review official/independent resources; avoid “avoid,” “safest,” “best,” “ghetto,” and family-preference phrasing; avoid tailoring geographic recommendations around protected characteristics.

## Positive/limiting observations

- The channel About pages and direct site provide substantial broker/license-holder identity information; no blanket “missing identity” finding is supportable from the public profile review.
- The crime-video speaker expressly discloses that he is not a crime expert and asks viewers to cross-reference a named source. That mitigates, but does not fully remove, the review concern created by later superlative/avoidance recommendations.
- The builder-incentive video contains a late caution not to focus only on the low rate. That mitigates a consumer-risk reading but does not replace ad-specific material-term review.
- The affordability video reviewed, [“How Much Home You Can ACTUALLY Afford In Texas (By Salary)”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1aqtBNxMid0), identifies its rate and DTI assumptions and tells viewers at 00:08:37–00:08:53 that actual results vary and that the content is a general overview. No standalone finding was made from that video; visual loan-ad disclosure review remains outside this text-only pass.

## Recommended next compliance steps

1. Verify the correct individual NMLS number and run a controlled replacement check through descriptions, channel About pages, end cards, pinned comments, sites, and templates.
2. Have broker/compliance counsel approve a written rebate policy and a concise, clear public terms block before continuing the “up to $5,000” promotion.
3. Retain source files and calculation methodology for every comparative seller-statistic or outcome claim, and add market/date/sample qualifiers near the claim.
4. Replace subjective safety/school/family directives with neutral resource-based language and a consistently applied policy for location questions.
5. Conduct a frame-level QA of each loan/rate/incentive video and paid placement for broker identity and all applicable mortgage-ad disclosures; the present review did not verify visuals.
