**Audit window:** 2024-07-24 through 2026-07-24 inclusive. Public uploads actually available in the window begin 2024-08-15 and end 2026-07-22. Taylor Dasch content was not included.

**Scope:** This is a content-risk review, not a legal determination. “Finding” means a specific statement that warrants broker/compliance-counsel review; it does not establish an FHA, TREC, lending, or consumer-protection violation. Automated captions can contain transcription errors, so the linked video is the controlling evidence.

## Rules applied

- TREC Rule 535.155 requires each advertisement to display the license holder/team name and broker name in a readily noticeable location, and bars advertising that is misleading or likely to deceive. [TREC rule text](https://www.trec.texas.gov/node/634)
- The FHA covers advertising discrimination based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, and disability. [HUD overview](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/fair_housing_equal_opp/fair_housing_act_overview)
- Current HUD guidance (April 2026) says discussing crime or school-quality information alone is not unlawful steering when it is shared consistently and without discriminatory intent. I therefore did **not** call a video a violation merely for mentioning schools, crime, or safety. [HUD announcement](https://www.hud.gov/news/hud-no-26-028)

## Findings requiring review

### BG-01 — VA-loan / wealth-building claims may be misleading without substantiation and disclosures

**Severity:** Moderate  
**Confidence:** Medium  
**Rule:** TREC Rule 535.155 misleading-or-likely-to-deceive advertising standard; confirm any lender/VA/financial-advertising requirements with counsel and a mortgage professional.  
**Missing context:** Whether the speaker has documented examples, states material assumptions/costs/risks elsewhere in the video or on screen, or has required lender/VA approval. No written “not financial/investment advice,” result-variability, or comparable disclosure was found in the public descriptions.

| Video | Exact evidence | Why it is a risk |
|---|---|---|
| [How Soldiers Build Million-Dollar Portfolios With Their VA Loan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uyDO9TayGyE) — 2025-12-06 | **00:00:40:** “soldiers at Fort Hood are using their zero down VA loan to secure six figure wealth, build million-dollar portfolios before they retire”; **00:08:46:** “That is how you have a million-dollar plus portfolio”; **00:09:39:** “more than likely over that million-dollar mark.” | A broad likely-result claim tied to a VA-loan strategy. It omits investment, vacancy, repair, financing, entitlement, and resale risk in the quoted claims. Caption later says to speak with a mortgage professional (06:37), which helps but does not itself substantiate the headline/result claim. |
| [STOP Renting at Fort Hood Texas and Know What Your BAH Really Covers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5uMDm9LyH1Q) — 2025-12-13 | **00:00:04:** “Soldiers, stop renting”; **00:00:38:** use BAH on “something that can pay itself forward … that’s going to pay your kids, that’s going to pay you”; **00:01:09:** “use your BAH to cover your mortgage.” | The video later uses a $265K example and acknowledges taxes/insurance and a payment above the cited BAH range. The categorical opening and future-payoff framing should be softened to an individualized affordability/investment analysis rather than a general recommendation. |

**Corrective direction:** Change “how soldiers build” / “that is how” / “more than likely” to conditional educational language; identify the scenario assumptions; state that VA eligibility, occupancy, residual entitlement, loan terms, taxes, insurance, reserves, rent, vacancy, repairs, and market performance vary; route financing claims to a licensed lender.

### BG-02 — School-ranking/location recommendations are an elevated steering risk, but not a confirmed FHA violation under current HUD guidance

**Severity:** Moderate  
**Confidence:** Medium  
**Rule:** FHA advertising/steering risk; apply the 2026 HUD rule above.  
**Missing context:** Whether the same data and options are shared consistently with every prospect; sources/date/method for the rankings; the full visual presentation; and whether actual services are conditioned on a household’s familial status.

### BG-03 — Familial-status / age-targeting language is a potential advertising-preference issue

**Severity:** Moderate  
**Confidence:** Low-to-medium  
**Rule:** FHA protection for familial status; age-oriented community marketing may also create an advertising-preference issue unless a lawful housing-for-older-persons exemption is actually established.  
**Missing context:** These are city/community guides rather than ads for a named dwelling; neither caption set establishes a housing exclusion. Confirm whether any referenced community is lawful age-qualified before using language that suggests it is for retirees.

| Video | Exact caption evidence | Why it is a risk / safer edit |
|---|---|---|
| [Where to Retire in Central Texas in 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1u3RA7NaVJU) — 2026-06-19 | **00:00:39:** “my favorite communities for retirees and empty nesters”; **00:02:29:** “senior citizen centers”; **00:07:13:** “[a] retirement community.” | “For retirees/empty nesters” can read as age/familial-status preference if used to market housing. Use neutral feature language (home price, access, lot size, amenities, maintenance) and, if relevant, identify a lawful 55+ status only after verification. |
| [Killeen vs Temple vs Belton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tEjSZkQ5OWk) — 2026-03-14 | **00:12:22:** “Belton, but it is a very family oriented town.” | “Family-oriented” is a preference-style descriptor. Replace with concrete, neutral facts (parks, commute, housing mix, public amenities) available to all households. |
