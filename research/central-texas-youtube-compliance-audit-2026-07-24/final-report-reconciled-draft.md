The principal review groups are:

2. quantified investment, savings, valuation, ranking, seller-performance, and outcome claims needing substantiation or material qualifications;
3. rate, down-payment, rebate, and incentive statements needing broker/lender and final-asset review;
4. safety, school, crime, and neighborhood wording needing neutral-source and consistency review; and

No public-record item in this audit establishes every element of a Fair Housing, TREC, RESPA, Regulation Z, FTC, or other legal violation. Public text or captions do not establish intent, current listing status, authorization, compensation, creditor role, licensure, the accuracy of underlying figures, or whether a disclosure appears in an unreviewed visual, linked, or platform surface.

## What the report means—and does not mean

An “apparent concern” is issue-spotting: the quoted public wording warrants review against the complete published asset and the responsible broker’s records. It is not a determination that a statement is false, a creator intended to discriminate, a consumer was excluded, or a regulator would reach a particular result.

- A title, description, or automatic caption shows that wording was publicly retrieved. Automatic captions require audio confirmation before disciplinary or enforcement reliance.
- A lender, builder, title company, mortgage program, incentive, affiliate, or product reference does not establish a RESPA §8(a) concern without evidence of a fee, kickback, or other thing of value given or accepted under an agreement or understanding for a referral of settlement-service business involving a federally related mortgage loan; it also does not by itself establish an FTC material connection.
- A rate, APR, payment, down-payment, or financing reference does not establish a Regulation Z issue without the complete final ad, creditor/advertiser role, applicable trigger terms, and visual prominence/proximity evidence.
- School, crime, safety, military, VA, PCS, and geographic content is not, by itself, a Fair Housing conclusion. The audit identifies only the wording and consistency questions described below.
- A sparse YouTube About page alone does not establish that broker identity, the Consumer Protection Notice (CPN), or Information About Brokerage Services (IABS) is unavailable. A readily noticeable direct public path, banners, video frames, end cards, and linked pages can matter.

## Defensible audit scope

The audit used a city-and-service scope, not an assumption that every person in a public search result lives in Central Texas. A creator’s public residence cannot be proven for every channel and was not required.

- Public housing-service channels credibly local to **Temple, Belton, Killeen, Harker Heights, Copperas Cove, or Nolanville**, including the immediately adjacent Fort Hood/Fort Cavazos market, received a full-channel audit where accessible inventory supported it. For those full-channel lanes, every recovered public upload in the two-year window remained in scope even when the individual video concerned another city or topic.
- Statewide, national, or out-of-market creators received direct-video-only review for the specific, locally relevant housing videos discovered in the audit. Unrelated uploads were not treated as part of the six-city creator universe.
- Current authenticated profile evidence moved Palm Harbor, rennerrealty, Lisa Copeland, Inside The World Of Real Estate, and The Myles Group to direct-video-only treatment because their broader channel identities are national, Austin, North Atlanta, or otherwise out of the target market. Their directly relevant Central Texas videos remain in scope.
- Authenticated profile review also narrowed five original-root channels—Dom McShan, ELITE Realty Georgetown, Luke Johnston, At Home in Texas, and Lovejoy Homes Realty—from provisional full-channel treatment to their directly discovered target-market videos. This prevented broad DFW, Georgetown, statewide, syndication, or Allen inventory from being counted as six-city creator coverage.
- Discovery triage identified credible local housing-service candidates from the public search harvest, while excluding news, government, schools, tourism, media vendors, generic national commentary, unrelated trades, personal military vlogs, and unidentifiable automated listing feeds unless the channel itself marketed local housing services.

This scope supports review of public housing-service communications without implying where every creator resides, whom they represent, or whether all uploads are locally marketed.

## Methodology and coverage

The audit combined public-channel inventory, profile text, titles, descriptions, current authenticated metadata where recovered, available English caption artifacts, direct-video review, and limited rendered browser/direct-link checks. It normalized source records against the review matrix and adjudication guardrails, deduplicated repeated substantive assets where appropriate, and excluded the two Taylor IDs stated above.

For every adjudicated full-channel identity, the inventory process separately probed the public **Videos, Shorts, and archived Live (`/streams`) tabs**. The archived-live pass also covered the manually audited and explicit zero-eligible identities so that a sparse Videos tab was not mistaken for an empty channel. Direct-video-only identities remained limited to the locally relevant videos that caused their inclusion.

Every available caption artifact was **machine-screened**. Humans adjudicated cue items and priority contexts, including relevant descriptions, titles, available caption excerpts/context, and selected direct-browser paths. This does **not** mean every transcript across every channel was manually read end-to-end. Captions that were inaccessible, music-only, empty, or non-substantive were treated as limits rather than support for a conclusion.

The applied standard was intentionally conservative:

1. record public wording, URL, date, and timestamp/location where available;
2. classify the record as an apparent concern, source/substantiation review, verification item, low-priority cleanup, or resolved item;
3. state the material missing facts; and
4. avoid claiming unverified visual, compensation, authorization, listing-status, lender, or private-communication facts.

## Limitations and handoff

This is not legal advice, an agency determination, an enforcement referral, or a finding of misconduct. It is a public-content issue-spotting report with material limits:

- Content can change after collection; live pages may differ from the captured evidence.
- Automatic captions may be inaccurate; audio confirmation is needed before disciplinary or enforcement reliance.
- The review did not authenticate or verify every thumbnail, title card, lower third, end card, pinned comment, map, paid placement, caption track, linked page, or current listing status.
- Visual/frame review was limited. A caption, title, or description cannot establish whether disclosures, qualifications, source citations, or broker information were visible in the video frame.
- Direct-link/browser review was limited to the rendered public paths documented in the evidence package. An unreviewed banner, end card, profile element, or linked page may provide relevant context.
- Public content cannot prove client-specific steering, discriminatory intent, protected-class preference in actual service, listing authority, privacy facts, a referral agreement, compensation, creditor/lender status, NMLS validity, rate availability, APR/loan terms, or the validity of performance data.
- A record was counted as a public upload only when retained metadata supplied a public-availability signal; blank, authentication-required, and unlisted availability states were excluded rather than presumed public.
- Unavailable, unlisted, deleted, or inaccessible uploads are not converted into adverse inferences. In particular, `rtxESd1DzFk` is not included as a report review item because it is currently unlisted.
