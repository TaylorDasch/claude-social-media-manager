# Central Texas YouTube audit — compliance review matrix

**As checked:** 2026-07-24  
**Use:** Screening standard for public YouTube titles, descriptions, captions, profile text, direct public links, and sampled visuals. This is not a legal opinion or an agency determination.

## Finding taxonomy

- **Observable conflict:** Public text or a visible element facially conflicts with a cited requirement. Report it as an *apparent concern*, not an adjudicated violation.
- **High-risk potential concern:** The public evidence creates a substantial issue, but authorization, compensation, license status, transaction documents, visual disclosures, or other material context is unavailable.
- **Broker/legal verification item:** The statement warrants review, but the available public record cannot establish the required legal elements.
- **No finding:** No discrete text-based concern in the reviewed material. This does not certify unseen visual, authorization, compensation, listing-status, or private-message facts.

Severity reflects remediation priority, not a conclusion about liability:

- **Critical:** Explicit protected-class exclusion/preference in dwelling advertising, or public evidence of all material RESPA §8(a) elements: a fee, kickback, or other thing of value given or accepted under an agreement or understanding for a referral of settlement-service business involving a federally related mortgage loan.
- **High:** Clear familial-status preference language; materially conflicting license/loan identity; specific financial, valuation, rate, or outcome claim without visible support or material terms.
- **Medium:** Comparative/ranking claim, subjective steering language, or profile/disclosure concern that depends materially on context.
- **Low:** Clarity, staleness, or disclosure-path verification with limited evidence of consumer harm.

## Texas real-estate advertising

### TREC Rules §§531.18 and 531.20 — CPN and IABS

TREC requires CPN and completed IABS links on covered business websites. For social media, each link may be on the profile or on a separate public page reached through a direct profile/platform link. Therefore, an About page that omits the notices is **not by itself** enough for a missing-disclosure finding; the direct public link path must be checked first.

Primary source: [TREC Rules §§531.18 and 531.20](https://www.trec.texas.gov/agency-information/rules-and-laws/trec-rules).

### TREC Rule §531.19 — discriminatory practices

The rule covers race, color, religion, sex, national origin, ancestry, familial status, and disability. Screen for statements that indicate or are intended to indicate a preference, limitation, or discrimination about an owner, occupant, purchaser, lessor, or lessee.

Primary source: [TREC Rule §531.19](https://www.trec.texas.gov/agency-information/rules-and-laws/trec-rules).

**Age nuance:** Age is not one of the listed federal FHA/TREC §531.19 classes. “Retirees,” “senior,” or “55+” language is not automatically an age-discrimination finding under these authorities. It still warrants review when it implies children are excluded, misstates a housing-for-older-persons qualification, or combines with familial-status preference language.

### TREC Rules §§535.154 and 535.155 — names and advertisements

- Team names must not imply that a team offers brokerage services independently of its sponsoring broker, must end in “team” or “group,” and must be registered before use.
- Covered advertisements must identify the license holder/team and broker in a readily noticeable location. The broker name must be at least half the size of the largest agent/team contact information.
- For social media, the required information may be on a separate page or profile only when directly linked and readily noticeable.
- Screen for material inaccuracies, team/broker-role confusion, stale listing status, unsupported appraisal/value or sold-role claims, rebate conditions, provider compensation, and rankings without disclosed objective criteria.

Primary source: [TREC Rules §§535.154–535.155](https://www.trec.texas.gov/agency-information/rules-and-laws/trec-rules).

## Fair Housing

The federal Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. §3604(c), and 24 C.F.R. §100.75 prohibit notices, statements, and advertisements concerning the sale or rental of a dwelling that indicate a preference, limitation, or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap/disability, familial status, or national origin. Texas Property Code §301.022 applies a similar advertising prohibition.

Primary sources:

- [42 U.S.C. §3604](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title42-section3604)
- [24 C.F.R. §100.75](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/subtitle-B/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-100/subpart-C/section-100.75)
- [Texas Property Code Chapter 301](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/docs/PR/pdf/PR.301.pdf)
- [HUD fair-housing advertising guidance](https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/FHEO/documents/BBE%20Part%20109%20Fair%20Housing%20Advertising.pdf)

### Current crime/school nuance

HUD stated on 2026-04-24 that sharing crime-rate or school-quality data is not itself an FHA violation when information is provided consistently and without discriminatory intent; HUD described intentional protected-class discrimination as necessary for unlawful steering. The audit therefore does **not** treat the mere mention of schools, ratings, crime, or safety as a violation. It escalates direct “avoid/go here” instructions, protected-class tailoring, unsupported superlatives, demographic-composition commentary, or inconsistent-looking recommendations for broker/legal context review.

Primary source: [HUD No. 26-028](https://www.hud.gov/news/hud-no-26-028).

## Settlement-service referrals and incentives

RESPA §8 and Regulation X prohibit giving or accepting a fee, kickback, or other thing of value under an agreement or understanding for the referral of settlement-service business involving a federally related mortgage loan. They also prohibit unearned fee splits. Bona fide compensation for actual services and certain cooperative-brokerage arrangements may be permitted. A consumer incentive for using the provider is generally not prohibited by §8, but an incentive in exchange for the consumer referring other business is an example of prohibited conduct.

Public mentions of a lender, title company, inspector, builder, rebate, or incentive are **not enough** to allege a RESPA violation. Escalate only when the public content supplies evidence about compensation/thing of value, an agreement or pattern, a referral, and a covered settlement service—or mark the missing elements expressly.

Primary source: [CFPB RESPA §8 FAQs](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/mortgage-resources/real-estate-settlement-procedures-act/real-estate-settlement-procedures-act-faqs/).

## Mortgage and rate advertising

Regulation Z §1026.24 requires advertised credit terms to be actually available and required disclosures to be clear and conspicuous. Advertised rates must be expressed as APR where the rule applies, and dwelling-secured rate/payment disclosures may require equal prominence and close proximity. Triggering terms can require down-payment, repayment-term, and APR information. Whether a particular real-estate video is a covered credit advertisement depends on the advertiser, offer, and format, so rate/APR/0%-down items are classified as visual/lender-counsel verification unless the public record establishes more.

Primary sources:

- [CFPB Regulation Z §1026.24](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/2026-04-08/24/)
- [Official interpretation of §1026.24](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/interp-24/)

## FTC endorsements and material connections

When a creator endorses a product or brand and has a material connection—financial, employment, personal, family, free/discounted product, or another thing of value—the FTC advises that the connection be obvious and hard to miss. For video, the disclosure should be in the video, not only in the description. Do not infer a material connection merely because a builder, lender, restaurant, or product is mentioned; log the missing compensation/relationship evidence.

Primary source: [FTC Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers).

## Evidence standard used in the final report

Each escalated row should include:

1. channel and creator;
2. public video/profile URL and upload date;
3. exact caption timestamp or “title,” “description,” or “current profile”;
4. a short public excerpt;
5. the rule/topic and why it is an apparent concern;
6. severity and evidence confidence;
7. the material facts not available publicly;
8. whether visual playback, broker records, lender terms, or legal review is still required.

Recommended formulation:

> Observable content at the cited location presents an apparent compliance concern. This audit does not determine intent, compensation, authorization, licensure, or legal liability; broker/legal review is required.
