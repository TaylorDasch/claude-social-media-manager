## Review standard

- TREC advertising rules generally require the license holder/team name and broker name in a readily noticeable location; a profile or a direct electronic link can satisfy certain social-media disclosures. A missing item is not asserted unless that channel/profile path was actually checked.
- Fair-housing advertising risk is contextual. Phrases that present a property as suited to a family/children can create a familial-status concern even without an explicit exclusion. Neutral description of bedrooms, rooms, or a school location is not treated as a finding.
- A public older listing video is not, by itself, proof that the property is still being advertised as active. It is logged as a status-verification issue only where the video lacks a date/current-status qualifier.

## Items reviewed and not elevated

- The retrieved Century 21 descriptions consistently identified the agent and “CENTURY 21 Premier Realtors,” with company/agent sites and contact information. On that evidence, I did **not** allege a missing-broker-name issue for those videos. I did not verify every listed external site for IABS/Consumer Protection Notice placement.
- “Family room,” “guest room,” bedroom count, and proximity to schools/base were not elevated when presented as property/location facts rather than a preferred resident group.
- Expert Rentals’ “Pets accepted upon Owner approval,” voucher acceptance, and “May be Available Sooner!” statements were preserved as policy/status language; this pass did not establish that they were false or discriminatory. The no-cat/pet policy was not treated as a Fair Housing finding.
- The review found no retrieved evidence of race, color, religion, sex, disability, national-origin, or explicit age/child exclusion wording. There was no retrieved automatic-caption evidence of tenant privacy disclosure or visual disclosure; the unreviewed visual layer remains open.
