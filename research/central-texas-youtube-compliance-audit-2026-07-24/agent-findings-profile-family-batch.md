# Profile / family channel batch — evidence log

Audit date: 2026-07-24 (Central). Scope: public uploads dated 2024-07-24 through 2026-07-24, inclusive. This is an evidence-led screening log, **not** a legal conclusion, TREC determination, or HUD enforcement finding. “High” means the quoted language is plainly present; it does not mean that an agency has adjudicated a violation.

## Method and limits

- Cataloged every public upload in the date window on the five assigned channels (62 total), then reviewed every title and available description. English auto-captions were reviewed where available (51 of 62).
- Caption availability is not a completed visual review. Thumbnails, on-screen overlays, spoken material omitted or mistranscribed by auto-captions, end cards, pinned comments, linked forms, and current listing status need separate manual confirmation. An unavailable caption is **not** a violation.
- Current About text and public destination paths were checked before logging an identity/disclosure concern. Home in Texas Team’s linked site identifies **Real Brokerage** and publicly links the TREC Consumer Protection Notice (CPN) and Information About Brokerage Services (IABS). Lisa Kamprath’s site, linked from a reviewed video description, identifies **White Label Realty** and displays the TREC notices. Frank Adams’s email-domain site contains CPN/IABS links, but brokerage identity is dynamically injected and was not exposed in the static capture. Elizabeth Thomas’s linked site identifies **Ace Real Estate Group** in its current site metadata; its CPN/IABS path was not established in this pass.
- Rule labels: Fair Housing Act §804(c) / 24 C.F.R. §100.75 (advertising that expresses a preference based on a protected class, including familial status); fair-housing steering risk; Texas real-estate advertising identity/broker-disclosure risk (including TREC Rule 535.155); and Texas misleading-advertising/substantiation risk. The HUD school/crime nuance is applied: objective, sourced information is not itself prohibited. The concern below is subjective school/neighborhood advice, protected-class preference language, or an unsupported outcome claim.

## Findings

### Home in Texas Team

1. **Neighborhood selection framed around age and familial status** — high severity; high confidence in the quoted language.
   - Video: [Everything You Need to Know About Belton Texas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=15TJl0reFxQ) (`15TJl0reFxQ`), 2025-02-21.
   - Evidence: 00:00:48–00:00:53, downtown Belton is “best for retirees and young families”; 00:02:02–00:02:09, North Belton is “great for Suburban families just looking to be close to a school”; 00:02:19–00:02:23, it is “great for families that are seeking easy access to a school.”
   - Rule/risk: FHA §804(c) familial-status and age-preference advertising risk, plus fair-housing steering risk. Describing location, distance, or official district boundaries is different from labeling an area as best for age/family groups. “Retirees” is especially risky unless the housing qualifies for a Housing for Older Persons Act exemption.
   - Missing context: HOPA qualification (not stated); neutral property and distance criteria; official school sources; on-screen qualifications.

2. **School-driven resale/value claim** — moderate severity; high confidence.
   - Video: [Neighborhoods in Belton ISD Tour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=stbP8X-u4-8) (`stbP8X-u4-8`), 2025-09-02.
   - Evidence: 00:02:12–00:02:20, buyers are “investing in a really great neighborhood that has great resale because of the elementary school that it goes to.”
   - Rule/risk: fair-housing steering and misleading-advertising/substantiation risk. The statement connects a school assignment to expected resale performance without a defined data source, period, or methodology.
   - Missing context: official boundary source, sales dataset/time window, property type, whether the statement is opinion, and any current assignment-change caveat.

3. **Specific appreciation prediction and investment assurance** — high severity; high confidence.
   - Video: [This Temple Texas Landmark Could Change the Local Real Estate Market](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4TbjiKdk0A) (`u4TbjiKdk0A`), 2025-06-26.
   - Evidence: 00:02:45–00:03:08 says nearby redevelopment “almost always raises property values,” predicts a “10 to 20% increase”; 00:03:40–00:03:48 calls it a “great time to invest” and says buyers are “sure to make a really good investment”; 00:04:40–00:04:42 urges getting in “before the prices go up.”
   - Rule/risk: Texas misleading-advertising / investment-performance substantiation risk. The claim is a projected price outcome, not merely a neutral market observation; “sure” heightens the risk.
   - Missing context: data source, defined neighborhoods/properties, baseline and forecast period, development certainty, methodology, and prominent no-guarantee qualification.

4. **Family/school placement messaging in a relocation guide** — moderate severity; high confidence.
   - Video: [Everything You Need to Know Moving to This County in Texas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AtLtAFk59pw) (`AtLtAFk59pw`), 2025-03-10.
   - Evidence: 00:00:14–00:00:23 says the team helps families find their “perfect neighborhood” and “perfect schools”; 00:04:42–00:04:53 says many families move to Salado for smaller schools/classes and that this is “a plus for any families”; 00:09:01–00:09:14 calls Belton ISD known for “academic excellence.”
   - Rule/risk: fair-housing steering risk and unsourced subjective school comparison. Official, cited accountability information and district-boundary links are safer than “perfect,” “plus,” or excellence judgments tied to household type.
   - Missing context: TEA or other official sources/date; neutral written criteria; whether the team assists all buyers under the same criteria; on-screen citations.
