# Claude Design Brief: Premium Beehiiv Market Newsletter

Use this brief when asking Claude Design to create a high-fidelity monthly newsletter for Taylor Dasch / Temple TX Homes.

## Core Instruction

Create a high-fidelity Beehiiv newsletter design that feels like a premium editorial market report, not a normal realtor email.

The desired quality bar: this should feel like a serious magazine/art director was paid to make one issue feel collectible, polished, local, and data-forward.

Use the structure below as a starting point, not a cage. If a stronger visual idea appears, take it. Creativity is encouraged as long as the final design stays readable, mobile-safe, Beehiiv-friendly, and useful to real clients.

## Recommended Claude Design Option

Use: **High Fidelity**

Do not choose a wide layout for the email itself. The newsletter should be a narrow, mobile-first email experience.

Ideal canvas:
- Max width: 600 to 640px
- Mobile-first
- Email-safe structure
- Beehiiv-compatible
- No JavaScript
- No fragile web-app interactions
- No tiny desktop-only layouts

## Creative Permission

You may depart from the wireframe when it improves the design.

You are allowed to:
- Create a magazine-style opening cover.
- Use dramatic editorial hierarchy.
- Use a surprising but tasteful section opener.
- Create custom visual treatments for numbers, market signals, and local context.
- Add subtle motifs inspired by maps, MLS sheets, field notes, appraisal reports, street grids, courthouse filings, or market ledgers.
- Reorder sections if the story becomes stronger.
- Use asymmetry, oversized numbers, premium spacing, and editorial rhythm.
- Add one memorable "signature" visual moment that makes the issue feel designed, not assembled.

You may not:
- Sacrifice readability for visual flair.
- Invent data, rent numbers, sales prices, charts, photos, or claims.
- Make the email feel like a generic luxury real estate flyer.
- Use a wide desktop web page layout.
- Use decorative gradients as the main visual idea.
- Create horizontal overflow on mobile.
- Bury the CTA.
- Use more than 3 to 5 links.
- Overload the issue with cards.

## Audience

Primary audience:
- Normal Temple/Belton buyers
- Sellers deciding whether to list
- Relocating families
- BSW medical professionals
- Fort Hood / Central Texas movers
- Local homeowners who want market clarity

Secondary audience:
- Referral partners
- Past clients
- Warm prospects

This is not an investor memo unless the issue explicitly says it is. For normal clients, translate data into plain decisions:
- Should I buy now?
- Should I wait?
- Is my home still worth what I think?
- What price range is moving?
- Where do buyers have leverage?
- What should sellers do before listing?

## Brand Position

Taylor Dasch should come across as:
- Local operator
- Calm market guide
- Data-forward but human
- Blunt when needed
- Sophisticated without being stiff
- Trustworthy, not salesy
- A practitioner, not a content marketer

Avoid:
- Generic realtor glamour
- Mansion brochure energy
- Fake luxury
- Stock-photo filler
- Confetti design
- "Market is hot!" hype
- Corporate newsletter blandness

## Visual Direction

Overall feel:
- Premium local market magazine
- Editorial briefing
- High-trust real estate intelligence
- Clean, quiet, expensive
- Designed but not over-designed

Possible issue names:
- Temple Market Intel
- The Temple Field Report
- Central Texas Market Brief
- Temple/Belton Monthly Market Report

Preferred palette:
- Ink: #111827 or #171717
- Soft black: #0f172a
- Warm white: #fbfaf7
- Paper: #f4f1ea
- Light gray: #e5e7eb
- Muted gray text: #6b7280
- Taylor emerald: #059669
- Caution amber: #b7791f
- Deep blue accent: #1e293b

Use color sparingly. The email should not become a one-note green or blue design.

Typography:
- Use email-safe fallbacks.
- Headlines can use Georgia or a refined serif fallback.
- Body should use system sans or Arial/Helvetica for readability.
- Avoid negative letter spacing.
- Do not scale font size with viewport width.
- Keep body copy comfortable: 15 to 17px, line-height 1.5 to 1.7.

Layout style:
- 600 to 640px central column.
- Magazine cover intro.
- Strong section openers.
- Alternating text and data modules.
- Use hairline dividers.
- Use compact data tables only when they help.
- Use no more than one major card-like section at a time.
- Prefer editorial bands, rule lines, and number blocks over generic rounded cards.

Component style:
- Square or lightly rounded corners only, 0 to 8px.
- No heavy shadows.
- No glassmorphism.
- No decorative orb/blob backgrounds.
- No loud gradients.
- CTA should be obvious but restrained.

## Required Email Structure

Use this as the base flow. Improve it if a better editorial rhythm emerges.

1. Masthead
   - Newsletter name
   - Month and year
   - "Temple + Belton, Texas"
   - Small author line: Taylor Dasch, EG Realty

2. Cover / Lead Story
   - One powerful headline
   - One short subhead
   - One sentence that tells readers what changed this month

3. The 5-Number Market Snapshot
   - Active listings
   - Closed sales
   - Median closed price
   - Median days on market
   - Price cuts or months of supply

4. What It Means In Plain English
   - 3 to 5 short bullets or a short paragraph
   - Buyer implication
   - Seller implication
   - Overall market read

5. Buyer Read
   - What buyers can negotiate
   - Where urgency still matters
   - What price ranges are most competitive

6. Seller Read
   - Pricing guidance
   - Prep/listing guidance
   - Warning about chasing the market down

7. Neighborhood / Price Band Focus
   - One table, chart-like module, or editorial block
   - Show where the market is strongest or weakest

8. Taylor's Take
   - A blunt, human, first-person note
   - Should feel like something only a local agent who watches the data would say

9. CTA
   - One clear action
   - Examples:
     - "Reply MARKET and I will send you the full Temple/Belton snapshot."
     - "Thinking about selling this summer? Reply PRICE and I will send you a realistic range."
     - "Buying in the next 90 days? Reply BUYER and I will send the current leverage map."

10. Footer
   - Taylor Dasch
   - EG Realty
   - Phone/email/site if provided
   - Short disclaimer
   - Unsubscribe handled by Beehiiv

## Data Display Rules

Use market numbers as design anchors, not filler.

Good data treatments:
- Oversized single-number blocks
- "Signal / meaning" pairs
- Compact comparison table
- One "buyer leverage meter"
- One "seller reality check"
- One "price band heat map"

Avoid:
- Too many charts
- Tiny labels
- Long tables that break on mobile
- Decorative charts with no decision value
- Exact-looking graphs if the data is not provided

## Copy Style

Write like a premium market brief:
- Short sentences
- Clear claims
- No hype
- No fake urgency
- No generic "now is the best time" language
- Every section should answer: "So what should the reader do?"

Preferred phrases:
- "The market is normalizing, not collapsing."
- "Buyers have leverage again, but not everywhere."
- "Sellers can still win, but the pricing window is narrower."
- "The best homes are still moving. The lazy listings are not."
- "Do not use 2021 expectations in a 2026 market."

Avoid phrases:
- "Dream home"
- "Hot market"
- "Act fast before it is gone"
- "Luxury lifestyle"
- "Hidden gem" unless specifically justified

## Beehiiv Compatibility

Design for Beehiiv/email reality:
- Keep layout single-column or simple stacked modules.
- Use inline CSS if producing HTML.
- Keep images optional, not required for comprehension.
- All text must still work if images are blocked.
- Tables must collapse or remain readable on mobile.
- Keep the total design lightweight.
- Use buttons as simple links styled like buttons.
- Avoid forms, scripts, sticky nav, hover-only states, and interactive widgets.

## Image Direction

Images are optional. If used, they should feel real and local.

Best image types:
- Actual Temple/Belton neighborhood or street photo
- Listing/detail photo if permission exists
- Cropped map-style header
- Field-note style market image
- Subtle local texture: curb, street sign, front porch, downtown, school zone, construction, hospital district

Avoid:
- Generic stock family in front of a house
- Luxury mansion stock photo
- Dark blurry cityscape
- AI-looking real estate fantasy renders
- Any image that makes the market report less credible

## Signature Creative Moment Ideas

Pick one if it strengthens the issue:
- "The Market Gauge": a horizontal signal strip showing Buyer Leverage / Balanced / Seller Leverage.
- "The Price Cut Ledger": a refined table showing how many listings cut price and the median cut.
- "The 30-Second Read": a narrow editorial box at the top with the whole market in 3 lines.
- "The Street-Level Truth": a field-note block where Taylor gives the honest local read.
- "The Price Band Heat Map": simple stacked bands showing where inventory is tight or loose.
- "The Seller Reality Check": a clean before/after module showing list price vs price-reduced behavior.

## Final Output Requirements

Return a complete high-fidelity newsletter design.

If HTML is requested:
- Provide Beehiiv-friendly HTML.
- Use inline styles where practical.
- Keep the email width 600 to 640px.
- Include fallback fonts.
- Do not include JavaScript.

If a visual design is requested:
- Make it high fidelity.
- Make it narrow/mobile-first.
- Show the whole newsletter flow, not just the hero.
- Include realistic spacing and final-looking typography.

Before finalizing, check:
- Does it feel like a premium market magazine?
- Can a normal client understand it in 60 seconds?
- Is the CTA visible?
- Does it work on mobile?
- Are the numbers treated as insight, not decoration?
- Did the creative idea improve the report, or just decorate it?
