The warranty. This is where the three-and-a-half stars comes from, and it's the single most important thing I'll tell you today.

Two more traps before we compare. [B-ROLL: community signage — public street side]
First, the price on the sign isn't the payment. On new construction, the county often taxes you the first year on the lot or a partially-built value — then reassesses the finished home, and in year two the tax portion of your escrow jumps. One: there's no option period — that strips out the standard inspect-and-walk protection Texas buyers are used to. Two: a material-substitution clause lets them swap specified materials without your sign-off. Three: no guaranteed closing date — they can move it, and your rate lock and your lease timing are your problem, not theirs. Four: the earnest-money terms lean the builder's way, so your deposit is more forfeitable than you think. And five: that preferred-lender lock — the incentive only exists through Kangaroo.
So who should skip Stylecraft entirely? If you need to modify floor plans, they're rigid — walk away. If you have a low tolerance for chasing post-closing repairs, this isn't your builder. If you refuse to touch a captive lender on principle, you'll never see the incentive that makes the deal worth it. And honestly, once you're pushing past the low four-hundreds — their range runs up to about four-eighty on the top plans — you have stronger craftsmanship options. Which brings me to the field.

Stylecraft's own footprint, so you know the range: eight communities across Temple, Belton, Killeen, and Nolanville, feeding four school districts — Belton, Temple, Academy, and Killeen ISD.
