## ROUND 3 — DEMAND (~5:25 - 7:35)

**[ON CAMERA, then cut to demand-driver card]**

"Round three. Forget yield for a second — who actually fills the unit, and do they keep showing up?

Temple's demand is boring. I mean that as a compliment. Baylor Scott & White is a regional medical hub — that's doctors, nurses, traveling medical staff, year-round, recession-resistant. The Fort Hood region pumps military and contractor demand into the whole corridor. And the logistics-manufacturing spine — McLane, Caterpillar, SeAH, the data-center corridor coming up the I-35 / Highway 190 axis — that's workweek tenants who need a roof near a job. None of that is flashy. All of it is durable. Boring workweek demand is exactly what you want behind a long-term rental.

**[Cut to Waco demand card]**

Waco's demand is *visible* — and more cyclical. Baylor University brings a student-rental engine and a college-town rhythm. Magnolia and the downtown tourism wave — that's a real, national visitor draw, and it's the foundation of something Temple flat-out does not have: a genuine short-term-rental economy. TSTC adds more student and trade demand.

**[ON CAMERA]**

"So read the difference. Temple is durable, employer-anchored, workweek demand. Waco is bigger and more visible — students and tourists — but more sensitive to enrollment cycles and travel cycles. Waco gives you a tourism lever Temple can't. Temple gives you a tenant who's still there when the tourism cycle cools. Different demand, different risk profile — and that drives which *strategy* fits where."

## ROUND 4 — STRATEGY FIT (~7:35 - 11:05)

**[ON CAMERA, then strategy matrix builds row by row]**

"Round four. Same dollar, four strategies. They do not land the same in these two markets.

**[Row 1 — Buy-and-Hold]**

**Buy-and-hold.** Waco takes a slight edge on raw yield — that 10.8 versus 8.1. But Temple takes the edge on durability and on getting the property *below* market in the first place, because there's more inventory and more seller fatigue to negotiate against. Yield buyer who operates well: lean Waco. Set-it, hold-it, sleep-at-night buyer: lean Temple.

**[Row 2 — BRRRR]**

**BRRRR** — buy, rehab, rent, refinance, repeat. This one's Temple. More inventory means more reps. More seller fatigue means more below-retail entries. And there are older pockets in Temple with real forced-equity upside — you create the value, you don't pay for it. More at-bats is the whole game in BRRRR, and Temple has more at-bats.

**[Row 3 — Mid-Term Rental]**

**Mid-term rental** — 30-day-plus furnished. Temple, and it's not close. Baylor Scott & White means a steady stream of traveling medical professionals — travel nurses, locum physicians, staff on 13-week contracts — who need furnished housing for a few months at a time. That's a premium over long-term rent without short-term-rental headaches. That medical mid-term thesis is Temple's quiet edge.

**[Row 4 — Short-Term Rental]**

**Short-term rental** — this is where Waco actually pulls ahead. Magnolia, Baylor games, downtown — Waco has the visitor economy to fill a nightly rental. Temple does not, and I'm not going to force a Temple STR that won't book. But underwrite Waco's STR with your eyes open: the City of Waco requires a special permit for some short-term rentals — budget a roughly 60-to-90-day approval process — there are parking rules, and you're paying around a 15 percent combined hotel occupancy tax. Verify the current rules before you close; this was last checked May 2026 and city ordinances move.

**[ON CAMERA]**

"So the dollar isn't market-agnostic. The strategy you're running should pick your city — not the other way around."

## ROUND 5 — RISK (~11:05 - 12:15)

**[ON CAMERA, then risk-ledger card — two columns]**

"Round five. What blows the deal up. Both markets have real downside — here it is, no spin.

**Temple risk.** Slower exits. Higher inventory and longer days-on-market mean if you need to *sell*, you wait. And there's a new-construction overhang — builders in the Temple corridor are still delivering, and that competes with your resale on the way out. You buy easier in Temple; you sometimes sell slower.

**Waco risk.** The Brazos River. Flood exposure is real on the wrong parcel — pull the flood map before you fall in love, because flood insurance can quietly eat that yield edge. Older housing stock means more capex surprises — the stuff you didn't budget for. Demand is more cyclical and more strategy-specific — lean on students or tourists and you're exposed when those cycles cool. And the STR rule risk — a city permit you didn't get, or an ordinance change, can switch off your business model overnight.

**[ON CAMERA]**

"Neither market is risk-free. They're just different risks. Temple's risk is *patience* on the exit. Waco's risk is *water, age, and rules.*"

**[ON CAMERA — direct, decisive]**

"So who wins? Honest answer, because that's the whole point of this channel.

If you are a pure gross-yield buyer and you actually operate older homes well — tight management, tight maintenance, you underwrite the flood map — Waco edges it. I'll say that plainly. That 10.8 versus 8.1 is real money if you run a tight ship.

But here's why it's closer than the sticker price pretends. Price per square foot is *identical*, and the gross gap is only about 2.7 points. Once you net out older-stock capex, flood insurance, and management drag on a cheaper home, that edge shrinks fast.

My personal lean, with my own capital, is Temple first. Newer stock, more forced-equity and BRRRR reps, a real medical mid-term-rental thesis next to Baylor Scott & White, and durable workweek demand that doesn't blink when a tourism cycle cools. Plus — and this matters — I'm boots-on-ground here. I see the off-market and below-retail Temple deals before they hit the portals.

When is Waco the right call? When you have a specific short-term-rental thesis near Magnolia or downtown, or when you find a clearly-discounted long-term hold in the right Waco pocket, outside the flood zone, and you're going to operate it well. In those cases, Waco's the move — and I'll tell you that straight."
