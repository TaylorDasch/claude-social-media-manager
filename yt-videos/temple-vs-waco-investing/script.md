## Round 1 - Price And Negotiation Room

**[1:10-1:35 | Desk A-roll. Right-half Temple vs Waco table.]**

Round one: price and negotiation room.

Temple's local April 2026 market page shows a $265,000 median sold price, 6.9 months of inventory, 69 median days on market, and a 96.7% sale-to-list ratio.

Redfin's March 2026 Temple page shows a similar direction: median sale price about $263,000, down 4.4% year over year, with homes taking 111 days on market.

That is not a hot market. That is a negotiation market.

**[1:35-2:10 | Desk A-roll. Right-half Waco data cards.]**

Waco is messier because the public data sources disagree more.

Redfin's March 2026 Waco page shows a $275,000 median sale price, down 5.1% year over year, and 95 days on market.

Zillow's Waco page, updated April 30, 2026, shows an average home value around $196,000, a March median sale price around $231,550, a $250,000 median list price, and 73.8% of sales under list.

So the exact number depends on source, but the pattern is clear: Waco is not a crazy bidding-war market either. Buyers are getting discounts there too.

**[2:10-2:55 | Desk A-roll. Right-third winner card: "Round 1: Temple by a little." Keep card small while Taylor explains nuance.]**

Here is my investor read.

If you are just looking at headline price, Waco looks cheaper.

If you are looking at negotiation room, Temple has a cleaner buyer-market story right now: more inventory, softer pricing, and more obvious seller motivation.

That matters because investors do not make money by buying a median house at a median price and hoping the spreadsheet feels better next year.

You make money on basis.

If I can buy a Temple house for $235K that should be worth $260K after cleanup, that beats buying a Waco house at $220K with no discount and no forced-equity path.

**[2:55-3:05 | Score graphic]**

Round one: Waco wins lower entry price. Temple wins negotiation setup. I call the round a tie, with Temple stronger for deal-finding.

## Round 2 - Rent And Day-One Math

**[3:05-3:30 | Desk A-roll. Right-third rent note card: "Do not compare average rent to your exact deal."]**

Round two: rent.

The March research pack put a typical three-bedroom single-family rent around $1,600 in Temple and around $1,550 in Waco. RentCafe's April 2026 apartment data shows Temple three-bedroom apartments around $1,533, and it lists Waco's average apartment rent higher than Temple's city average.

That means you need to be careful with rent comps. Apartment data, house data, student housing, medical housing, and short-term rentals are different markets.

Do not mix them in one spreadsheet.

**[3:30-4:25 | Desk A-roll. Right-half conservative buy-and-hold calculator.]**

Let's use a conservative investor example.

Temple purchase: $255,000.

Waco purchase: $231,550, using Zillow's March median sale price.

Twenty-five percent down. DSCR-style loan. Investor insurance. Property taxes. Vacancy. Maintenance. Property management.

At current-rate investor debt, neither deal is exciting if you buy it clean at median price and rent it long term on day one.

That is the truth.

This is where YouTube investing content gets people in trouble. They use gross rent, ignore taxes, use owner-occupant insurance, forget vacancy, and then act shocked when the deal is negative after closing.

**[4:25-5:15 | Desk A-roll. Right-third card cycles: Purchase price / rent / taxes / insurance / vacancy / repairs / management.]**

So if your strategy is "buy a clean median house, put 25% down, rent it long term, and expect day-one cash flow," I would not be excited about either market.

The better question is: where can you change the math?

Temple gives you three cleaner paths:

Buy below market because inventory is higher.

Force equity through a rehab or BRRRR.

Or furnish near BSW and go after the medical mid-term rental lane.

Waco gives you two cleaner paths:

Buy closer to the lower end of the basis range.

Or use the tourism draw with a short-term rental, but only if the property and permit path actually work.

**[5:15-5:25 | Score graphic]**

Round two: Waco has the lower basis edge. Temple has the better ways to change the math. Slight edge Temple if you are active, slight edge Waco if you are purely basis-driven.

## Round 3 - Demand Drivers

**[5:25-5:55 | Desk A-roll with optional B-roll punch-ins. Right-half Temple demand-driver board.]**

Round three is demand.

Temple's demand is boring in the best way.

Healthcare. Military. Logistics. Manufacturing.

Baylor Scott & White is the daily anchor. Fort Hood is the regional military anchor. McLane, Caterpillar, SeAH, and the data-center corridor add employment layers.

None of that is sexy, but investors should like boring demand.

**[5:55-6:45 | Desk A-roll with optional B-roll punch-ins. Right-half Waco demand-driver board.]**

Waco's demand is more visible.

Baylor University. Magnolia. Downtown tourism. Manufacturing. TSTC and the broader McLennan County job base.

That gives Waco a much stronger short-term rental and visitor economy than Temple.

If your whole strategy is built around weekend tourism, Temple is not Waco. Do not force a Temple STR just because the purchase price looks good.

But Waco's visible demand comes with more sensitivity. Student cycles matter. Tourism cycles matter. STR rules matter. The property has to be in the right pocket, with the right design, and the right permit path.

**[6:45-7:25 | Desk A-roll. Right-third comparison card: "Temple = workweek demand / Waco = visitor + student demand."]**

Here is the difference.

Temple is a "people live here because they have to be here" market.

Waco is partly that, but it is also a "people visit here because they want to be here" market.

That is not good or bad. It just tells you which strategy belongs where.

If you want boring long-term demand with medical and military anchors, Temple gets the edge.

If you want visitor-driven upside, Waco gets the edge.

**[7:25-7:35 | Score graphic]**

Round three: Temple for durable rental demand. Waco for tourism demand.

## Round 4 - Strategy Fit

**[7:35-7:50 | Desk A-roll. Right-half four-strategy matrix.]**

Round four: strategy fit.

This is the most important part of the video.

Do not ask, "Which city is better?"

Ask, "Which city fits my strategy?"

**[7:50-8:35 | Card 1: Buy-and-hold]**

For normal buy-and-hold, I prefer Temple if I can buy below market or near BSW.

Why? Because the tenant demand is easier to understand. Hospital, military, local workforce, growing families.

But I would not buy a random clean Temple house at full retail and call it a deal. I want a discount, a repair angle, or a better rent path.

Waco buy-and-hold can work too, especially near employment, Baylor-adjacent demand, or voucher demand, but I want to be careful about flood zones, older housing stock, and tenant income.

**[8:35-9:20 | Card 2: BRRRR]**

For BRRRR, Temple is my pick.

More inventory, more seller fatigue, older housing pockets, and a buyer-market setup give you more chances to create equity.

Not every old Temple house is a deal. Some are cheap because they should be cheap: foundation movement, old plumbing, bad layout, train noise, drainage, or a block that will make resale harder.

But if you know how to underwrite repairs, Temple gives you more reps.

**[9:20-10:05 | Card 3: Mid-term rental]**

For mid-term rental, Temple wins for me.

BSW is the anchor. Medical travelers, residents, fellows, and hospital-adjacent demand give Temple a clearer furnished-rental thesis than people realize.

The right play is not a random big house across town.

It is a clean two- or three-bedroom near the hospital, with durable furniture, strong Wi-Fi, easy parking, and a lease structure that matches medical contracts.

**[10:05-10:55 | Card 4: Short-term rental]**

For short-term rental, Waco wins.

I would not fight that.

Magnolia and downtown Waco give you a visitor economy Temple simply does not have.

But Waco STRs are not "buy it and list it" anymore. The City of Waco says some STRs require a special permit before a license is issued, the special-permit process takes about 60 to 90 days, parking rules apply, and the combined hotel occupancy tax is 15%.

So Waco wins STR, but only if you underwrite the permit, the zoning, the parking, the competition, and the design. A boring house with random furniture is not a strategy.

**[10:55-11:05 | Score graphic]**

Round four: Temple for BRRRR and MTR. Waco for STR. Buy-and-hold depends on basis.

## Round 5 - Risks

**[11:05-11:30 | Desk A-roll. Right-third Temple risk card.]**

Round five: risk.

Temple's risk is liquidity and overbuilding.

Redfin's March 2026 page had Temple homes taking 111 days on market. My local April market page had 6.9 months of inventory. That gives buyers room, but it also means if you need to sell fast, Temple may punish you.

New construction inventory can also drag on resale if you buy the wrong product at the wrong price.

**[11:30-12:05 | Desk A-roll. Right-third Waco risk cards.]**

Waco's risk is more strategy-specific.

Flood exposure matters. Student and tourism demand can move differently than medical/military demand. STR rules matter. And if you are buying near tourist zones, you may be competing against investors who already understand the design and permit game.

The honest read is this:

Temple is easier to underwrite for boring rental demand.

Waco is easier to pitch, but harder to operate well if you are leaning on tourism.

**[12:05-12:15 | Score graphic]**

Round five: Temple has slower-sale risk. Waco has more strategy and location risk. I give Temple the risk-adjusted edge for long-term rentals.

## Final Verdict

**[12:15-13:05 | Final scorecard]**

Here is my verdict.

If you are a BRRRR investor, I would look at Temple first.

If you are a medical mid-term rental investor, I would look at Temple first.

If you are a boring buy-and-hold investor, I would look at both, but I would not buy either at full retail without a clear edge.

If you are a short-term rental investor, I would look at Waco first, but only if the permit path, zoning, parking, and furnishing plan all pencil.

So which market would I buy?

I would buy Temple first for forced equity and medical rental demand.

I would buy Waco only for a very specific short-term rental or a clearly discounted long-term rental in the right pocket.

And I would skip both if the deal only works because I used optimistic rent, ignored repairs, or assumed rates are going to save me.

## Specific Plays

**[13:05-13:45 | Desk A-roll. Right-half card: "What I would actually buy."]**

In Temple, I would be looking for a three-bedroom near the BSW side of town, preferably with some repair friction that scares retail buyers but does not scare an investor.

I want to buy below the clean retail comp, improve the property, and then choose between long-term rent and furnished medical rent.

In Waco, I would be looking for a property with a real visitor-use thesis, outside the wrong flood exposure, with a clear STR permit path, off-street parking, and a design angle that can compete.

If it is just "near Magnolia" and the numbers only work at perfect occupancy, I am out.
