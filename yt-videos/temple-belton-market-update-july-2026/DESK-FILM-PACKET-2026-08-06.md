### 0:28–0:55 — Answer first

> Alright, so here’s the simple version. A fresh, well-priced home can still
> move. A listing at 60 or 90 days deserves more questions, but that doesn’t
> automatically make it a deal. Age tells me where to look. Price history and
> the homes it actually competes with tell me whether to push.

### 0:55–1:25 — Build the staircase

`[GRAPHIC 1: 13.7 → 51.2 → 66.0 → 79.8]`

> The full staircase matters. It was 13.7% in the first 30 days. From day 31 to
> 60, it was 51.2%. From day 61 to 90, it was 66.0%. After day 90, it was 79.8%.
> Now listen, that’s a snapshot of different active listings. It doesn’t prove
> that crossing day 60 caused a price cut.

Short landing line: `Age is the question trigger. It isn’t the answer.`

### 1:25–2:20 — Taylor’s showing observation

`[ON SCREEN: TAYLOR’S SHOWING OBSERVATION · NOT MLS-MEASURED]`

> Here’s something I see in person—not something this export measures.
> Sometimes the photos are weak, or the price started ahead of the condition.
> Then I walk the house and notice a small clue: a settling crack I want an
> inspector to look at, or an older roof where I want the age and insurability
> verified.
>
> A crack isn’t a foundation diagnosis. An older roof isn’t automatically a bad
> roof. And this data doesn’t prove any one of those made the house sit. It tells
> me what I need to investigate before I call it negotiating room.

Short landing line: `A price cut shows a correction. It doesn’t promise another one.`

Editor note: the current 61+ DOM remarks scan contains examples of roof,
foundation, settling, repair, and as-is disclosures. It cannot verify weak
photography or prove why a listing remained active. Do not show identifiable MLS
photos or remarks.

### 2:20–3:05 — The closing guardrail

`[GRAPHIC 2: 95.73% ORIGINAL / 99.48% FINAL]`

> Now here’s the number that keeps this from turning into a lowball video. In
> the 168 qualifying Temple and Belton sales that closed from July 7 through
> August 5, the median close-to-original-list relationship was 95.73%. The
> median close-to-final-list relationship was 99.48%. And 53.0% closed below
> final list.
>
> So some negotiation was there, but a lot of the public correction happened
> before the offer. That’s the honest deal.

### 3:05–4:35 — The three-number test

`[GRAPHIC 3: RELATIVE DOM / PRICE HISTORY / SUBSTITUTE PACE]`

#### One: relative DOM

> Compare the home with the same city, price range, property type, condition,
> and closest reasonable alternatives. The 60- and 90-day marks tell me when to
> investigate. They don’t set the offer.

#### Two: current price versus original price

> How far has the seller already moved? How recently? Did the new price put the
> home beside better choices? The public price history can carry more signal
> than the red badge beside the listing.

#### Three: substitute pace

> What happened to the homes it actually competes with? Did they sit, reduce,
> go pending quickly, withdraw, or close near the final ask? Does the buyer have
> three real alternatives, or none?

Landing line:

> I want all three pointing the same way before I tell a buyer to push harder on
> price or terms. The numbers don’t tell us what the seller will take. They tell
> us whether the facts support pushing—or moving.

### 4:35–5:15 — Buy now or wait

> Alright, so should you buy now or wait? If your move date is fixed, the payment
> is comfortable, and the comps support the house, don’t freeze because of a
> headline. But if the payment feels stretched or the move isn’t firm, wait. I’d
> rather tell you that now than force a house into a timeline that doesn’t fit.

For an out-of-town buyer: financing ready, ranked backups, and an in-person or
video condition check before treating an older listing as an opportunity.

### 5:15–5:40 — Exact limitation

`[CLEAN DESK SHOT · NO MUSIC]`

> Here’s the limitation. This is an August 5 snapshot of active listing records,
> not the same houses followed for 90 days. It doesn’t measure photography,
> interior condition, seller credits, cumulative market time, or motivation. So
> use the data to pick the question. Use the exact house to answer it.

### 5:40–6:05 — CTA and handoff
