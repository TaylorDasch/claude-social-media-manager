You just matched, or you signed, or you accepted the offer for Baylor Scott and White — and now you've got about six weeks to figure out where you're going to live in a town you've maybe driven through once.

**[DECISION 1 — RENT FIRST OR BUY NOW]**

Decision one, and it has to come first: rent first, or buy now. Because if the answer is rent, the other two can wait.

Here's the honest default, and there's a specific BSW reason behind it. If you're a resident or a fellow on a three-to-five-year track, rent for the first year — because until you're actually working, you can't predict your rotation. You don't know if you'll be at the main campus, the clinic, or driving to a satellite site half the week, and that's the thing that actually decides your commute. You also don't know which side of town fits how you live. A twelve-month lease buys you all of that information cheaply.

This isn't about "renting is throwing money away." It's about whether you have the information yet. At month two in a new town, you don't.

The exception is real: if you're an attending, you're staying, and you know your service line, buying early can absolutely be the better move. And if you're a thirteen-week traveler, just rent furnished and keep your money mobile.

So where do you actually rent? Aim for a twelve-month lease in that ring around campus — as of filming, a solid one-bedroom in that range tends to run somewhere in the twelve-to-eighteen-hundred-a-month neighborhood, but check current listings, because that moves. I'm not going to redo the rent-versus-buy math here, because I built a whole video on it — the real numbers, the break-even point, what it costs to sell in two years if your plans change. It's called "Is Buying in Temple a Mistake for BSW Residents? The Real Math." If decision one is your decision, watch that one next.

**[DECISION 2 — WHICH SIDE OF TOWN]**

Decision two, and here's what surprises almost everyone: Temple is small. As of filming, most of the areas BSW people actually choose sit about ten to twenty minutes from the hospital, depending on where you land. You're not picking between a fifteen-minute commute and an hour. You're picking between fifteen and twenty-five — and that gap buys very different homes.

So the honest default here isn't a neighborhood name, it's a method: decide your commute ceiling first, then look at what your money buys inside it. The closest-in option is the Hospital District itself — the cheapest entry point near campus, but the tradeoff is real: those homes are older and you're buying condition, not new finishes. Push the ring out and you trade a few minutes for newer construction and bigger lots. Check current prices before you decide off any of that.

One gotcha that gets people on day one: don't assume the school district from the city name. Wyndham Hill, in South Temple, is Academy ISD — not Temple ISD. Bella Terra and Sage Meadows are City of Temple. If you've got kids, verify the district at the specific address, never by the area.

I'm not going to walk you through every neighborhood, because that's its own video — and it's the one to watch the moment you've set your ring. It's called "Temple TX for Medical Professionals: Neighborhoods Within 15 Minutes of BSW." That's your block-by-block tour. Two companions when you're ready: there's a deeper one on the area right around campus, and an honest review of Bella Terra, one of the most common picks for medical staff. I'll drop all three in the description and pin them.

**[DECISION 3 — NEW CONSTRUCTION OR RESALE]** *(route beat — its own video now)*

Decision three: new construction or resale. Once you know you're buying and you know your ring, this is the last fork — and it's where that mistake I promised you lives.

I'm not going to settle this one on camera, because it earned its own video — I just filmed the full new-build-versus-resale breakdown for Temple, and it's the next one to watch the second this becomes your live decision. But here's the headline and the trap, so you don't get burned before you get there.

The headline: new construction is genuinely appealing when you're working eighty-hour weeks — a brand-new house with no projects is worth real money to your sanity, and as of filming, builders here have been running aggressive incentives. Resale, on the other hand, tends to get you established trees, a real read on the neighborhood, and often more home for your money in the closer rings — at the cost of a house that's lived a life. There's no universal right answer, just the right one for your timeline, and that full video walks it.

Now the trap, and this is the one that costs people: be careful using a physician loan on new construction specifically. The timing, the appraisal, and the way the loan interacts with a build-in-progress can bite you — it's a different animal than using that same loan on a resale home. On physician loans in general: they can be a real tool, terms vary, and I'm not going to send you to one specific lender — talk to two or three and compare.

So two next videos for this fork: the full new-build-versus-resale breakdown for the decision itself, and "Don't Use Your Physician Loan on New Construction, BSW Temple TX" before you finance it. Both are linked below.

**[HONEST CAVEATS]**

Two quick caveats. Every number I touched — prices, taxes, the commute ring — that's all as of when I'm filming. On taxes specifically: effective property tax around here runs roughly two-point-two to two-point-seven percent depending on your MUD or PID district and your exemptions, and a new subdivision can sit at the top of that range. This market moves — check the current figures, and don't treat anything in any video, mine included, as a timeless fact. And the library keeps growing, so grab the most recent one.
