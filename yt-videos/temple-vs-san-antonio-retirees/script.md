**[1:05–2:30 — SECTION 1: WHERE SAN ANTONIO WINS (give it fully — this is the trust beat). LOWER-THIRD: "San Antonio wins: 3 things."]**

Number one — military medicine. San Antonio is "Military City USA," and it's not marketing. Brooke Army Medical Center is the largest hospital in the entire Department of Defense and the only Level I trauma center the military has — plus the DoD's burn center. Three bases in one city. If your retirement is going to revolve around active-duty military medicine and the biggest veteran community in the country, San Antonio wins, full stop. Write it down.

**[beat — B-roll: San Antonio cultural shots, market square, festival]** Number two — diversity and culture, and I won't pretend otherwise. San Antonio is a majority-Hispanic city, around 65 percent, with deep Mexican-American roots — the food, the festivals, the history. You said diversity matters to you. On Hispanic culture specifically, Temple can't match that. That's a real, honest point for San Antonio.

**[beat — B-roll: theme park / zoo stock or tasteful cutaway]** Number three — your nine-year-old. San Antonio is a kid's playground: SeaWorld, Six Flags, the zoo, Morgan's Wonderland, real museums, the River Walk. Temple doesn't have that in town — for the big day-out stuff, you'd drive to Austin or Waco. Another fair one for San Antonio. And I'll add a quick fourth — the airport. If your family's overseas or scattered, San Antonio has its own international airport in the city; Temple's nearest big airport is Austin, about 75 minutes south. So that's the San Antonio column, and it's a strong one. Now let me flip it.

**[2:30–4:00 — SECTION 2: THE FLIP — NURSING. GRAPHIC: "Same pay. ~4× the competition."]**

Here's the number I promised. Your spouse is a nurse — so nursing isn't a side detail, it's the household's career. And the average nurse in San Antonio and the average nurse in the Temple area make almost exactly the same money — roughly ninety thousand a year, mean, in both. Verify the current Bureau of Labor Statistics figure when you're ready, but the two metros are within a couple hundred dollars of each other.

**[beat — graphic: 22,000 vs 5,000 nurse icons]** Except San Antonio has about four times as many nurses competing for that paycheck — around twenty-two thousand of them, versus about five thousand in the Temple-Killeen area. More nursing schools, more new grads, same or lower pay. You called San Antonio nursing jobs "low paying" — the data says your instinct is right. That's what an oversaturated market does to wages.

**[beat — B-roll: BSW tower, then McLane Children's, then VA]** Now flip to Temple. You were told Temple is "short on nursing options." It's the opposite. Temple is the town built around a hospital — literally. Baylor Scott & White here is the original, flagship hospital of the whole system, founded in 1897. It's a Level I trauma center, the only one on I-35 between Dallas and Austin, more than 600 beds, a Texas A&M teaching hospital with dozens of residency programs. Add the only children's hospital between Dallas and Austin, a full VA hospital, and Fort Hood's healthcare demand up the road. For a nurse, Temple isn't fewer options — it's the same pay, a quarter of the competition, and a hospital system that anchors the entire town. That's the flip.

**[4:00–4:22 — PULL-QUOTE. Talking head, direct to camera, slower.]**

So here's the honest way to hold it: San Antonio is where you retire if a military hospital is the center of your life. Temple is where you retire if your family's career and your budget are. For most of the families who write me, it's the second one. Hold onto that sentence — it's basically the whole decision.

**[4:22–5:35 — SECTION 3: MONEY (cost + taxes). GRAPHIC: comparison card "verify medians the month you shop."]**

Now the money, and this is closer than you'd think on the sticker — but it separates underneath. The median home price in Temple and in San Antonio right now is basically a tie — both hovering around 260 thousand — and the rent's a tie too, about seventeen hundred a month either way. Verify those the month you actually shop, because they move. But that tie is good news for you, because it means renting first costs you nothing on either side — you're not "losing" by testing Temple for two years.

**[beat — LOWER-THIRD: "Where Temple separates."]** Where Temple separates is the cost of everything else and the tax bill. Day-to-day cost of living runs roughly eight percent lower in Temple. And property taxes — Bell County's effective rate runs lower than San Antonio's Bexar County, something like 1.6 to 2 percent versus about 2.3. On a 260-thousand-dollar home, that's hundreds to well over a thousand dollars a year, every year — verify your exact parcel, but the direction is clear.

**[beat — GRAPHIC: "No TX income tax · military retirement untaxed · over-65 freeze."]** And both are in Texas, which for a military retiree is the real prize: no state income tax, and Texas does not tax your military retirement pay or your pension at all. The year you turn 65 you can freeze your school taxes and stack extra exemptions — and if either of you carries a 100-percent service-connected disability rating, your homestead property tax can go to zero. Run your real numbers with the appraisal district and your tax advisor — I'm not putting an exact figure on screen because the rules change — but for a military family, Texas is hard to beat, and that part applies to both cities.

**[5:35–6:35 — SECTION 4: SAFETY, SCHOOLS, CONSERVATIVE (her stated values). LOWER-THIRD: "Safety · schools · community."]**

Three things you told me matter — let's do them fast, because the data's clean. Safety: Temple's crime rate runs about half San Antonio's — roughly 27 per thousand versus 52, with violent crime about half as well. Same source, NeighborhoodScout, same method — so it's apples to apples.

**[beat — GRAPHIC: "Belton ISD: B · Temple ISD: C · SA big-3: C."]** Schools, for your nine-year-old: the state just graded Texas districts, and Belton ISD — right next to Temple — pulled a B. That beats all three of San Antonio's biggest districts, which all landed at a C. Temple ISD's a C, same as San Antonio's. So on district ratings, the Temple area edges it.

**[beat]** And community fit: you said you're conservative. Bell County voted Trump by about 16 points in 2024. Bexar County — San Antonio — was one of only twelve counties in all of Texas that went the other way. San Antonio's trending more conservative, to be fair, but as it stands, if a conservative community matters to you, that's a real difference.

**[beat — the honest diversity correction]** One more, because it's the one you were worried about. "Temple has no diversity" — that's not accurate. Temple is about half non-white, and Bell County, because of Fort Hood, is one of the more diverse and one of the more Black communities in Texas. It's a military-melting-pot kind of diversity instead of San Antonio's Hispanic-majority kind. Different flavor — but Temple is not the homogeneous small town you were picturing.

**[6:35–7:45 — SECTION 5: THE HOME ITSELF (Perry + the simple-home truth). B-roll: newer single-story streets, larger lots.]**

Now the homes — and I have to be straight with you, because you mentioned you love Perry Homes. Perry doesn't build in Temple. They build Houston, Dallas, San Antonio, and Austin — the closest Perry community to Temple is in Georgetown, about 45 minutes south. And Temple has fewer of those big, master-planned luxury communities in general; our builders are D.R. Horton, Lennar, Stylecraft, Carothers — solid new construction, but not Perry's amenity package. If a glossy master-planned community is what you're picturing, that's a genuine point for San Antonio or Georgetown.

**[beat — talking head, tie to her own words]** But here's the thing you actually told me: you're getting older, you've got one kid left at home, and a simple home sounds appealing — you said you're a little over the materialistic life. Read that back. For a simple, right-sized, single-story new home, your money goes further in Temple. In the 350-to-450 range, you're looking at roughly 1,800 to 2,500 square feet here, often on a quarter-acre to most of an acre — versus a smaller home on a smaller lot for that same money on a new build in San Antonio. The "Temple homes aren't as pretty" feeling is mostly about luxury *volume* — not whether you can find a beautiful, simple home to grow old in. You can.

**[7:45–8:35 — SECTION 6: THE "UNDERDEVELOPED / ISOLATED" MYTH. B-roll: I-35, downtown revitalization, Pepper Creek trail.]**

Last one before the verdict: "underdeveloped and isolated." I'll give you the honest version. Temple sits right on Interstate 35 — an hour to Austin, 40 minutes to Waco. It's at about 102,000 people and growing roughly four percent a year. Baylor Scott & White alone is a two-and-a-half-billion-dollar economic engine; there's a five-and-a-half-mile trail system, a downtown that's actively being redeveloped, and over a hundred million dollars of new investment last year. "Underdeveloped" isn't the right word. "Smaller and quieter" is — and at your stage, with the traffic and the crowds being exactly what you didn't like about San Antonio, that's the point, not the problem.

**[8:35–9:20 — WHO SHOULD NOT PICK TEMPLE (the second honest concession — keep it prominent). Talking head, serious. LOWER-THIRD: "Pick San Antonio if…"]**

So — my second promise to say it out loud. Who should *not* retire in Temple? If your top priorities are big-city culture, a Hispanic-majority community, the deepest military-medical network in the country, and theme-park-level things for your kid in your own town — then go to San Antonio, and don't second-guess it. That's the honest call for that person.

**[beat — number card: "even if Temple wins for you…"]** And even if Temple is your pick, go in clear-eyed: there's no Sun City-style 55-plus resort community here. The summers are brutal — upper 90s, a stretch over a hundred. It's car-dependent. And the nearest international airport is over an hour away. None of that is a dealbreaker for most people — but you should hear it from me now, not discover it after you've signed.

**[9:20–10:05 — TAYLOR'S TAKE. Talking head, warm, personal.]**

Here's my honest read on what you wrote. The things you didn't like about San Antonio were the traffic and the crowds. The things you said you wanted were simple, smaller, conservative, family-first — and you've got a nurse's career anchoring it all. That profile isn't a San Antonio profile. That's Temple. And renting first is exactly the right move — the prices are a tie, so you risk nothing testing it for a year or two while you're finishing up overseas. I help military and nurse families make this exact move to Baylor Scott & White all the time — it tends to land well for the people who came for the career and the cost, not the nightlife.

**[10:05–10:50 — VERDICT + CTA (closes the hook loop). Talking head, confident. LOWER-THIRD: page URL + phone.]**

So the scorecard, honest version. San Antonio wins military medicine, big-city culture and diversity, and your kid's attractions — three real wins. Temple wins nursing for the cost, day-to-day affordability, property tax, safety, school ratings, conservative community, and a simpler home for your money — with a VA hospital right in town. If your top three are that San Antonio list, go to San Antonio with my blessing. But for a military family with a nurse, a kid, a budget, and a preference for the simple life — I think Temple's the better fit, and most families like yours that I meet end up agreeing.
