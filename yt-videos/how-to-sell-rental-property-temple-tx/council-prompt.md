## The one-line thesis
You can sell a tenant-occupied rental in Temple — but the data shows it sits ~3 weeks
longer because of restricted showings. The fix is occupancy strategy + pricing, and most
investors leave money on the table by ignoring the first-time-buyer pool.

## Hard facts to anchor the script (all from Central Texas MLS, trailing 12 mo, June 2026)
- Temple closed sales by occupancy — median days on market: **Owner 69 · Vacant 72 · Tenant-occupied 95** (n≈996; tenant subset ≈10, directional).
- Sold-to-list ratio held ~**99–100%** across all occupancy types → the penalty is the *calendar*, not the ratio.
- Temple median sold ≈ **$274K** at **99.7%** of list.
- Under-$250K band (where most rentals sit) sells fastest: **57-day** median (first-time + investor demand overlap).
- **22** tenant-occupied listings under $250K on the Temple market right now.
- Light-reno lever: a targeted **$2,000–$5,000** can move a property from investor-only to first-time-buyer eligible.

## Taylor's real takes (use his voice — direct, analyst, honest, no fluff)
1. **Occupancy first.** Vacant = better price, more showings, more end-buyer options. That's what he recommends when the choice is open. Sell occupied only when the next deal is already found and its income offsets the meat left on the bone.
2. **Pricing is everything**, whoever owns it. Price to live comps, re-check weekly. Leaving a little on the bone isn't a loss when it's a reallocation.
3. **Present every listing like it's worth $1M** — pro photos/video on an $80K house and a $1M house alike. Won't put his name on a listing without them.
4. **Answer the phone.** The investor-price-point agent gap is real — he's put offers on investment properties where the agent never replied; doesn't even know if the offer was presented. Costs the *seller* a buyer they never knew existed.
5. **Market to first-time buyers**, not only investors — the pools overlap under $250K, and that's where the strongest prices come from.
