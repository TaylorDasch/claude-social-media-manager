## Core thesis

Morgan's Point Resort contains materially different housing stock inside one small city: active homes span 1,001–2,804 square feet and 1966–2026 build years, with newer developments at the top of the current range. A buyer should compare homes that compete for the same buyer, price the ownership items that sit behind the list price, and verify what any lake language legally provides.

The city field is not a value driver. It is removed from the public thesis.

## The three checks

### 1. Compare the right housing stock

- Start with nearby closed sales from the same market area when possible.
- Match the subject on site, size, age, condition, style, legal characteristics, and property rights.
- Do not treat the 13 active listings or the $330,000 active median as one comp set.
- Do not compare a 1970s original-section home as though it were a 2019 or 2026 home in a newer development merely because both sit inside the city limits.

**Signpost:** `Here's one: compare the house to the version of Morgan's Point it actually competes with.`

### 2. Price the second ownership cost

- Active build years span 1966–2026.
- For older homes, check roof, foundation, HVAC, electrical panel and brand, supply lines, and insurability.
- Four of the 26 listing-remark records mention septic; verify the system, service history, and any aerobic maintenance requirements for the exact property.
- Get the bound insurance quote during the option period.

**Signpost:** `Here's two—and this is where the list price stops helping.`

### 3. Separate a view from property rights

- `WaterfrontYN` is False on all 26 July 30 records.
- `WaterAccessYN` is True on 2, False on 4, and blank on 20.
- Four records mention a Belton Lake view.
- USACE prohibits private docks and piers lake-wide.
- Federal land generally sits between many lots and the water; verify the exact boundary with a survey or Corps record.

**Signpost:** `And here's three—the lake. A view is not ownership, and it is not a private dock.`

## Closing argument

The same flexibility that lets buyers find mixed ages, shops, RV accommodation, and fewer HOA constraints can also produce block-by-block variability. The buyer action is neutral and specific: drive the exact street at the hour they would normally come home and verify the recorded restrictions for the property.

If a buyer wants uniform newer construction, tighter community controls, or a private dock on their own lot, Morgan's Point Resort may not fit. Taylor should say that plainly.
