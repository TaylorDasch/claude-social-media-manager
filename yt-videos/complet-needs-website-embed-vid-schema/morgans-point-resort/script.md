## 3. Beat outline

### B1 · 0:00–0:18 — Hook

Use the verbatim hook in §1. One continuous shot. Let the two prices appear as spoken; do not show a median.

### B2 · 0:18–0:45 — Set the decision

**Riff:**

- Morgan's Point Resort is its own incorporated city on Belton Lake.
- The July 30 active set contains 13 homes.
- The median list price is $330,000, but a median across mixed housing stock doesn't price the home a buyer is standing in.
- The video will show the comparison procedure, not declare a cheap or expensive side of town.

**Land on:**

> "The question isn't whether Morgan's Point is cheap or expensive. The question is what this specific house competes with, what it needs, and what you actually own."

### B3 · 0:45–2:35 — Check 1: compare the right housing stock

**Open:**

> "Alright, so here's one: compare the house to the version of Morgan's Point it actually competes with."

**Riff:**

- The 13 actives run from 1,001 to 2,804 square feet and from 1966 to 2026.
- Price per square foot spans $152 to $310.
- The $869,000 active is a 2026 build in Rancho Del Lago.
- The $665,000 active is a 2019 build in Campus at Lakewood Ranch.
- Other actives sit in the original Morgan's Point Resort sections and date back to 1966.
- Same city limits doesn't mean same buyer, same condition, same site, or same legal characteristics.

🔒 **SAY EXACTLY — the corrected comp rule:**

> "When I run comps here, I start with nearby closed sales that compete for the same buyer—similar site, size, age, condition, style, and property rights. If I need to expand outside that immediate set, I explain why and adjust for the real differences."

**Add in Taylor's voice:**

> "If the best comp is older but actually similar, I would rather explain that than force a newer sale into the set because it looks cleaner on a spreadsheet."

**Land on:**

> "The range describes the town. The comp set prices the house."

### B4 · 2:35–4:20 — Check 2: price the second ownership cost

**Open:**

> "Alright, here's two—and this is where the list price stops helping."

**Riff the inspection and ownership list:**

- Roof age and remaining life.
- Evidence of foundation movement.
- HVAC age.
- Electrical panel and brand.
- Supply-line material.
- A bound insurance quote before the option period ends.
- Septic type, service history, and maintenance contract where applicable.

**Verified local proof:**

- Four of the 26 July 30 listing-remark records mention septic.
- Some descriptions pair city water with aerobic septic.
- The active stock spans 60 build years, so one checklist cannot be assumed to fit every house.

🔒 **SAY EXACTLY — property-specific boundary:**

> "I'm not telling you any specific home here has any of those issues. I'm telling you the active inventory runs from 1966 to 2026, so the due diligence can't be one-size-fits-all."

**Taylor practice line:**

> "When I show a home out here, I want that insurance quote and the system ages in front of us while we can still make a decision—not after closing."

**Land on:**

> "A lower list price can still be the right buy. Just price what comes next before you call it the deal."

### B5 · 4:20–6:20 — Check 3: view, access, and ownership are different

**Open:**

> "And here's three—the lake. A view isn't ownership, and it isn't a private dock."

**Verified MLS facts, July 30:**

- `WaterfrontYN` is False on all 26 Morgan's Point records.
- `WaterAccessYN` is True on 2, False on 4, and blank on 20.
- Four records mention a Belton Lake view.

🔒 **SAY EXACTLY — USACE rule and boundary:**

> "The MLS does have a waterfront field, and on all 26 of these records it says no. That lines up with how Belton Lake works. The Corps of Engineers' master plan says private shoreline uses aren't allowed here, and it specifically includes private floating docks and fixed or movable piers. Private docks and piers are prohibited lake-wide."

> "On many Morgan's Point lots, federal land generally sits between the residential property and the water. But a GIS map isn't a survey, so verify the exact boundary for the house you're buying with a survey or a Corps boundary record."

**Resolve the hook:**

> "So if a listing shows the lake, that may be a real view. It isn't proof that you own the shoreline, and it isn't a private dock. That's what 'lake home' changes here."

**Land on:**

> "The picture tells you what you can see. The survey and the Corps tell you what you own."
