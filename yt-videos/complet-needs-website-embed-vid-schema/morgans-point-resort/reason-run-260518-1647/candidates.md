## Author A — Round 1 (cold-start, baseline that would have shipped from a one-pass draft)

**Concept:** "Three $250K Homes in Morgan's Point Resort — Only One Is Lakefront"
**Frame:** Walkthrough of three currently-listed ACTIVES, one per tier.
**Title (recommended):** "Morgan's Point Resort: $250K Buys 3 Very Different Homes — Lakefront vs Lake-Access vs Inland" (90 chars — over the 60-char preference)
**Hook (verbatim, baseline):**
> "Three homes. Same price. Same Morgan's Point Resort. But only one is actually on the water. I walked all three this week and the difference between them costs buyers thousands. Let me show you."

**Tier 3 buyer-fit framing (baseline — Fair Housing edge):**
> "Tier 3 inland is perfect for retirees who want the lake address without the maintenance, and great for families who want the Belton ISD assignment without paying lakefront prices."

**Filming location (baseline):** "Belton Lake shoreline" — no public-access qualifier
**Pinned comment (baseline):** Simple "DM for the shortlist" — no lead-magnet mechanic

→ Would have shipped with: ACTIVES dependency, 65-90 char title, Fair Housing language in tier 3, filming-location compliance risk, weak conversion mechanic.

## Author B — Round 1 (refactor after critique)

**Concept:** Same 3-tier frame, but refactored to use recent SOLDS (comp-walk frame) — survives any filming week.
**Title (recommended):** "Three $250K Homes in Morgan's Point Resort, TX" (49 chars)
**Hook (verbatim, refactored):**
> "If you bought a Morgan's Point Resort home in the last twelve months at the median sold price — about $249,500 — there's roughly a one-in-three chance you bought something that isn't actually on the lake. The MLS doesn't tell you which tier of waterfront you bought. The listing photos don't tell you. And most buyers find out at appraisal or at resale, not before. Three homes, same price, very different products. Here's the difference."
