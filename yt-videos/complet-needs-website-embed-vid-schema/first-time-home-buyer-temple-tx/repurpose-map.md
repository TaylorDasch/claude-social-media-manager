## The 28/36 rule

Lenders will approve you for more than you should spend. The rule that survives every cycle: housing payment under 28% of gross monthly income, total debt under 36%. A household earning $7,000/month gross has a target max PITI around $1,960 — which maps to roughly a $260K to $280K house at current rates.

If your housing payment hits 32%+ of gross, you're house-poor before you move in.

## The 5 loans

- **FHA** — 3.5% down, 580 minimum credit. MIP for life of loan.
- **VA** — 0% down, no PMI. Veterans, active-duty, qualifying spouses.
- **Conventional** — 5% standard, 3% with first-time buyer programs. Best for 740+ credit.
- **USDA** — 0% down. Rural Bell County. Income-capped.
- **TSAHC + MCC stack** — 3-5% down payment grant + $2K/year federal tax credit, stacked on top of any of the above.

The decisive question: do you qualify for VA, USDA, or TSAHC? If yes, run those first. FHA is the fallback, not the default.

## The TSAHC + MCC stack

Most Bell County first-time buyers don't know these exist. Income-qualified buyers can stack a 3-5% down payment grant (never repaid) with a federal tax credit worth up to $2,000/year for the life of the loan. Some buyers close on a $280K home with under $3,000 out of pocket.

Not every lender writes these programs — the per-loan margin is thinner. If your lender says "we don't do those," call a second lender. Verify current 2026 limits at tsahc.org.

## The 5 entry-level neighborhoods (May 2026 MLS)

- **Lake Pointe** — median ~$255K · ~$155/sqft · Belton ISD
- **Carriage House Trails** — median ~$235K · ~$148/sqft · Temple ISD
- **Prairie Ridge** — median ~$290K · ~$165/sqft · Temple ISD
- **Three Creeks** — median ~$320K · ~$170/sqft · Belton ISD (top)
- **Hubbard Branch** — median ~$310K · ~$160/sqft · Belton ISD

About 1,840 active listings sit in the $200K–$350K band in Temple right now, 2,525 corridor-wide. Temple is one of the deepest first-time buyer markets in Central Texas.

## The 7 traps

1. **The builder upgrade trap** — $300K model signs at $385K. Stick to structural upgrades only.
2. **Foundation issues** in pre-2000 Temple/Belton homes (Central Texas clay). Get a structural engineer.
3. **MUD/PID tax surprise** — $1.5K-$4K/year extra in some master-planned communities. Pull the tax detail before you offer.
4. **HOA fee creep + special assessments** — read the last 3 years of board minutes.
5. **Texas insurance crisis** — premiums up 30-60%. Quote during option period.
6. **Job change in the 60 days before closing** — don't.
7. **Buying without a buyer's agent** — seller pays both commissions in most Texas transactions. Buying represented costs you nothing extra.

## The 90-day timeline

Pre-approval before tours. Loan choice before house hunting. Program stack before the contract. Then: tour 6-12 homes, option-period inspection + insurance, underwriting, close. File your homestead exemption with Bell County before April 30 of the year following purchase — most first-time buyers forget.
