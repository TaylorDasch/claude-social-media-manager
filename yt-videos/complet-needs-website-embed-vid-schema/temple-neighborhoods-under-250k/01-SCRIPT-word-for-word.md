## #5 — ALTA VISTA (~90s)

`[B-ROLL: Alta Vista entrance, Bethann/Huntington streetscape, two-story 2010s homes]`
`[ON-SCREEN CARD: ALTA VISTA · Median $228,500 · $213K–$244K · ~1,600 sqft · built ~2017 · Academy ISD · No HOA]`

> Number five is Alta Vista, on the south side. This is one of the newer pockets on the list — most of these homes went up around 2017, built by D.R. Horton and Ashford — and at the under-two-fifty tier you're looking at a median right around two hundred twenty-eight thousand for about sixteen hundred square feet.
>
> Here's the part people miss: Alta Vista is in **Academy ISD** — not Temple ISD.

`[LOWER-THIRD: Academy ISD — smaller district, strong reputation]`

> If schools are driving your move, that's a real plus. Academy is a smaller, well-regarded district, and a lot of buyers don't realize you can get it *inside* Temple's city limits. And it's about a seven-minute drive to Baylor Scott & White.

`[MAP: Alta Vista → BSW · 7 min · 3.9 mi]`

> Now the honest part. At this price, Alta Vista is thin — when I pulled the numbers, only three homes were actually active under two-fifty. Most of the neighborhood now trades between two-fifty and the mid-three-hundreds. So this one's about timing: when the right one comes up, you move on it. **Best fit:** a family that wants something newer and wants Academy schools.

`[B-ROLL: Oak Ridge new construction, spice-named street signs — Peppermint, Coriander — framing/finished homes]`
`[ON-SCREEN CARD: OAK RIDGE · Median $236,675 · $219K–$250K · ~1,415 sqft · built 2025 · NEW construction · Temple ISD · Mandatory HOA]`

> Number four — Oak Ridge. This is the one for you if the words "fixer-upper" make you break out in a cold sweat. Oak Ridge is brand-new: homes built 2022 through 2026, mostly D.R. Horton and Flintrock Builders. Under two-fifty you're at a median around two hundred thirty-seven thousand for roughly fourteen hundred square feet — and you get the new-build stuff people love: warranties, modern open layouts, nothing to repair on day one.
>
> Fun detail you'll notice driving in — every street is named after a spice. Cilantro, Saffron, Turmeric, Peppermint.

`[LOWER-THIRD: Streets: Cilantro · Saffron · Turmeric · Oregano]`

> It's about eleven minutes to Baylor Scott & White — the farthest on this list, but still nothing.

`[MAP: Oak Ridge → BSW · 11 min · 5.1 mi]`

> Schools are Temple ISD — the community zones to Garcia Elementary, then Lamar Middle and Temple High.

`[LOWER-THIRD: Temple ISD — Garcia Elem → Lamar MS → Temple High]`

## #2 — WESTERN HILLS (~90s)

`[B-ROLL: Western Hills, big lots, mature trees, Apache/Chisholm street signs, range of home styles]`
`[ON-SCREEN CARD: WESTERN HILLS · Median $210,000 · $100K–$250K · ~1,734 sqft · built ~1985 · No HOA · Temple ISD]`

> Number two — Western Hills. If your priority is space and a yard, this is your neighborhood. It's established west Temple — homes mostly from the 1960s through the 90s, no HOA, big lots, mature trees. And it's got the biggest square footage on this whole list: a median around seventeen hundred thirty square feet, plenty of homes pushing past two thousand, at a median right around two hundred ten thousand.
>
> The streets are named for Native American nations and Texas rivers — Apache, Arapaho, Comanche, Brazos.

`[LOWER-THIRD: Streets: Apache · Comanche · Brazos · Chisholm]`
`[MAP: Western Hills → BSW · 8 min · 3.5 mi]`

> Eight minutes to Baylor Scott & White. Now here's the honest one, and it matters: condition in Western Hills is all over the map. That hundred-thousand-dollar bottom of the range? That's a fixer with foundation problems. There are also beautifully, fully remodeled homes up at the top. So in Western Hills you've got to inspect carefully and know exactly what you're buying — but if you do, it's the most home and land per dollar in Temple. **Best fit:** a buyer who wants room to spread out, no HOA, and isn't afraid to vet a home or do a few updates.
