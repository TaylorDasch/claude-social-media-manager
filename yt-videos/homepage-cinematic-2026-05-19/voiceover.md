# Voiceover Read-Script — Homepage Cinematic Reel
**For:** Taylor Dasch · studio read · single session
**Recording target:** 96kHz / 24-bit, lav + cardioid boom doubled, room treated
**Tone:** Calm, low register, even pace. Podcast-intro, not commercial. Think *Decoder* opening, not network promo.
**Pace note:** Slower than feels natural. ~155 words per minute, NOT 175. The cinema does the lifting.
**Reset between sections:** Full breath. The silence between beats is part of the script.

Notation:
- **bold** = emphasis (very light — these aren't punches, they're weight shifts)
- *italic* = soften, drop volume ~10%
- `/` = micro-pause (~0.3 sec)
- `//` = full pause (~0.8 sec)
- `(breath)` = audible inhale, leave it in the take
- `___` = elongated vowel, hold the word slightly

---

## SECTION A — Cold open (over drone push, 0:00-0:06)

> Most agents open a video like this with a **skyline**.
>
> // (breath)
>
> I'd rather open with the **receipts**.

**Direction:** First line should land slightly under the music — almost confidential. Second line is the thesis — let it sit a beat longer than feels comfortable.

---

## SECTION B — The Desk (over close interior, 0:06-0:12)

> *Thirty million* in closings.
>
> / A hundred transactions.
>
> / Ranked **twenty-eight** of two thousand thirteen agents / in Bell County.

**Direction:** This is the receipts. Don't sell it. Read it the way a CFO reads a P&L on a board call — flat, factual, with the numbers themselves carrying weight. Pause after "transactions" so the rank lands as a separate beat.

## SECTION C — Taylor on Camera, Bookend #1 (0:12-0:22)

> I'm **Taylor Dasch**.
>
> / I work **five lanes** / and I never **blur** them.
>
> // (breath)
>
> Investor math / isn't BSW math.
>
> Military PCS math / isn't first-time-buyer math.
>
> // (breath, slight)
>
> The playbook **bends** to match the audience.

**Direction:** Eye contact is just past the camera — podcast posture, not pitch posture. The word "blur" gets the most weight in the whole script. Hold on "blur" for an extra micro-beat before the breath.

**Alternate take to capture (insurance):** If "five lanes" reads stilted on camera, swap to "five different audiences." Capture both versions in studio so the edit has options.

## SECTION D — Geographic Bridge (over montage, 0:22-0:28)

> **Temple, Texas.**
>
> / Three highways. / Two hospitals.
>
> A military base. / A university.
>
> // (breath)
>
> **Six markets** / running at different speeds.

**Direction:** The list is a rhythm — short, declarative, period after each beat. NOT a single breath. Don't rush. Each item gets its own moment so the visual can land alongside it.

## SECTION E — The Five Lanes (over signature shots, 0:28-0:48)

### Lane 1 — BSW (0:28-0:32)
> *BSW relocation* —
>
> physician mortgages, / Match Day timelines, / commutes that survive a resident schedule.

### Lane 2 — Investor (0:32-0:36)
> *Investor* —
>
> cap rate, / rent comps, / cash-on-cash.

> / The math we run **before** the showing.

### Lane 3 — Military (0:36-0:40)
> *Military PCS* —
>
> BAH against PITI, / gate commute, / what to do with the house / when orders move you again.

### Lane 4 — First-Time Buyer (0:40-0:44)
> *First-time buyer* —
>
> FHA, VA, USDA, / the closing-cost stack nobody hands you upfront.

### Lane 5 — Seller (0:44-0:48)
> *Seller* —
>
> pricing in tight inventory, / marketing that isn't syndication theater, / **net-sheet honesty** up front.

**Direction:** Each lane is a five-second editorial. The lane name (italics in the script) drops slightly in volume — it's a label, not a punch. The descriptor list that follows is the substance. Punch only the final word of each lane: *schedule, showing, again, upfront, up front.*

## SECTION G — Taylor on Camera, Bookend #2 (0:50-1:00)

> One agent / running **five playbooks** / beats five agents running one.
>
> // (breath)
>
> The playbooks only stay **sharp** / if the lanes / never **blur**.

**Direction:** This is the verdict. Pace is slower than Bookend #1 — measured, almost like you're closing an argument. "Sharp" and the second "blur" carry the weight. The callback to "blur" from Bookend #1 is intentional — the reel opens and closes on the same word.

---

## SECTION H — End Card (1:00-1:05)

> Pick your lane.
>
> / I'll send back the right pillar, / the right data, / the right short list.

**Direction:** Almost spoken, not read. Drop volume by ~15% from the bookend. This is the invitation, not the pitch. Last word ("list") trails into the music fade.

**Alternate take to capture:** "Pick your lane. I'll send back the right pillar, right data, right short list — drafts only." (cuts "the" three times for tighter cadence — capture both, edit picks)
