## Pass 1 — Mission framing

### What is this video actually doing?
Most "top neighborhoods" videos on YouTube are agent-personal-preference lists dressed as analysis. The audience knows it. The category has accumulated skepticism. The first job of this video is to defeat that skepticism in 30 seconds — by making the **picks visibly come from the MLS data, not from the agent's listings or sponsorships.**

### What is the audience trying to do?
Three distinct sub-audiences arrive at this video:
- **BSW medical relocator** with a sub-$250K target — likely a resident, fellow, or new-staff buyer; needs commute to BSW Temple.
- **First-time buyer (Temple-area renter)** trying to step out of a $1,800/mo rental into a $1,650/mo mortgage. Wants newest possible build under $250K.
- **Lake/lifestyle relocator** — out-of-state or out-of-region buyer who chose Temple/Belton for lake access + low cost. Wants water proximity.

A bad video tries to serve all three with the **same picks**. A good video uses **three distinct picks** that each map to one sub-audience cleanly, so every viewer self-identifies at least once.

### What does "best" mean here?
"Best under $250K" cannot mean "highest median sale" — that just picks the most expensive. It cannot mean "lowest median sale" — that picks distressed pockets. It cannot mean "highest inventory count" — that just picks the biggest builder development.

Definition the video should defend:
> **"Best" = a neighborhood that (1) has buyer-actionable activity under $250K right now or in the past closing window, (2) maps to a real buyer archetype that isn't the same as the other two picks, and (3) holds up when you read the MLS data, not the marketing copy.**

That definition justifies why Westfield (only 2 active) and Morgans Point Resort (only 2 active) survive — both have proven sold-velocity even though their active inventory is thin. The thinness is part of the story.

## Pass 2 — Hook deliberation

### Hook candidates tested mentally
1. "Temple has 358 subdivisions under $250K. Three of them are different enough that they actually mean something."
2. "I'm not going to show you the same neighborhood three times pretending it's three different neighborhoods."
3. "Most 'top neighborhoods under $250K' lists are just the agent's listings. This one is just the MLS data."
4. "Under $250K in Temple right now, your three best picks aren't three versions of the same house. They're three different buyer journeys."
5. "Pull the May 15 MLS. Filter under $250K. Sort by what the data actually says. You get Pecan Creek, Westfield, and Morgans Point Resort — and they couldn't be more different."

**Hook 3** is the strongest opener — it pre-empts the genre's biggest weakness (agent-bias clickbait) before the viewer can deploy it as an excuse to bounce. But it depends on **delivering data immediately** to back the claim — otherwise it's hypocrisy.

**Hook 5** is the strongest data anchor — but the runtime cost of three neighborhood names at the very top is a retention risk (viewer files the names, leaves to Google).

**Decision:** Open with Hook 3, immediately follow with the data-validation moment from Hook 5 in the first 30 seconds. The names land at 0:18 — not so early they leave, not so late they bounce thinking it's vague.

## Pass 3 — Structural choices

### Three-segment vs. integrated
Option A — Three discrete neighborhood segments (3:00 each) with intro + outro.
Option B — Integrated comparison weaving the three throughout each topic (price, velocity, lifestyle).

Option A wins because:
- Each segment can be cut to a Short later (clean repurpose).
- B-roll planning is simpler (one location per segment).
- Viewers who only care about one neighborhood can scrub to that chapter.
- The video has chapter markers, which YouTube favors for retention.

### Segment order: which neighborhood first?
- Pecan Creek first — most viewers, most actionable inventory, biggest data story (33 records). Earns the early retention.
- Westfield second — short segment (less data to mine), placed in the middle so the runtime doesn't dip there.
- Morgans Point Resort third — strongest velocity story (9-day contract, 20-day median DOM), emotionally most aspirational (lake), and naturally sets up the closing "who buys what" decision frame.

### Should the video rank them 1/2/3?
No. The thesis ("three different buyer journeys") is broken if the video then ranks them — that contradicts the framing. Instead, end with a **decision matrix**: "If you want X, go to Y." Different from a ranking.

## Pass 4 — Voice anchors and risk passes

### Voice anchors (lifted from prior `temple-market-read` channel voice)
- "I'm Taylor Dasch with EG Realty in Temple…" (identity stamp early).
- "Pulled May 15." (data freshness stamp).
- "Here's what the data actually says." (analyst tone marker).
- "Honest version." (signal a contrarian or hedge is coming).
- Inclusive of negatives — never sanitize the data.

### Risk pass — what could the video say wrong?
- Calling Pecan Creek "the best" — must not. It's the best **for one buyer profile**.
- Claiming school district without verification — must be flagged on-camera.
- Predicting appreciation — out of scope.
- Saying "broker" — banned word. Must say "agent."
- Using "hidden gem," "nestled," "perfect," etc. — banned.
- Forgetting to caveat the $89,900 Westfield outlier — viewer will see it on Zillow and the credibility is dead.
- Mixing in investor framing for Morgans Point Resort (lake = STR potential temptation) — banned for this channel lane.
- Showing addresses on-camera that belong to currently-occupied owners — use established streets, not house numbers.

### Forced-distinction pass — what makes each segment NOT sound like the others?
- **Pecan Creek**: builder voice — talk about phase, plan, incentive, base price, options, warranty.
- **Westfield**: scarcity voice — talk about closings vs. active, what waiting looks like, how to write a quick offer.
- **Morgans Point Resort**: lifestyle voice — talk about lake, drive time, lot character, why DOM is 20 not 60.

Each segment uses a **different verb vocabulary** so the viewer's ear doesn't blur them.

## Pass 5 — Endgame & retention engineering

### The closing decision matrix
Last 60–90 seconds = "If you're [X], here's which one." Three rows. Three viewer profiles. Three picks. This is the line viewers screenshot and send to their spouse — the natural shareable moment.

### Anti-bounce techniques used
- Names announced in first 30 sec (no "stay tuned" trick).
- Specific dollar figures at minute 1, 4, 7, 10 (each segment's hook).
- Chapter markers labeled with neighborhood name (anchor scrubbing).
- A negative for each pick (no segment is pure cheerleading) — credibility insurance.

### CTA
Single CTA only. Lead-capture link in description + a "send me your situation" pinned comment offer. Do not pile dual CTAs.
