### Body (3–45s)
**[GRAPHIC: three stacking tax layers build under VO — exemptions reset · MUD · PID.]**
> "Three things blow up the payment. One — exemptions reset. The current owner's been there 20 years with every exemption; your tax bill starts WITHOUT those. Your rate is higher than the listing implies.
>
> Two — MUD districts. A Municipal Utility District adds a tax. Three — PID districts. A Public Improvement District adds another. Same-looking houses, very different payments, depending on the line on the map you're standing on.

**Length:** 45–55s · **Source:** long-form 3:28–4:43 (Mistake #3 + the confession)

### Hook (first 3s)
**[SHOT: tight on Taylor, slightly lower energy / candid. Caption: "AGENT CONFESSION."]**
> "I'll admit something most agents won't: I used to list homes way over what they were worth — because the seller wanted to. A lot of agents still do."

### Body (3–38s)
**[B-ROLL: MLS listing, "asking price" callout, comps grid with one outlier circled.]**
> "So here's the mistake buyers make: they treat the asking price like it's the market value. It's not. A seller can list at whatever they want, and plenty of agents will let them.
>
> A cheap price doesn't mean a deal. An accepted offer under asking doesn't mean a deal. The only way to know is the data PLUS someone who knows the area — because sometimes one weird comp drags the whole number, and a local will throw it out.

> A well-priced home usually sits 30, 60, 90 days. The ones that move in days are usually distressed and priced to sell — that's the real deal, and that's what I watch for."
