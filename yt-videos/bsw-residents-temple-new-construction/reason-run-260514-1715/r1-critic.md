# Critic — Round 1 (cold context, sees only Candidate A)

## Adversarial attack on Candidate A

**WEAKNESS-1 [FATAL]: 60-second-promise vs 60-second-delivery gap.**
The hook at 0:00–0:15 closes with "this is the 60-second decision that changes which side of those numbers you land on." But the direct answer ("get your buyer representation documented via TREC 1502") doesn't arrive until the 1:00 timestamp — after a 45-second identity declaration block (0:15–1:00). A BSW resident scrolling at 11pm clicks off before the payoff because the hook promised fast and the script delivered slow. The candidate even violates its own implicit structural promise. The identity block buys back retention from the wrong viewer — the one looking for credentials before the payoff — at the expense of the much larger cohort looking for the move first.

**WEAKNESS-2 [FATAL]: Missing literal deflection script for "we can't pay your agent."**
The candidate's middle act (3:00–5:00) promises the centerpiece deliverable: "how to handle the builder-rep deflection." It then NARRATES three deflections ("Oh, we don't need an agent here…" etc.) but gives the viewer only ONE verbatim line back ("My agent is representing me. Please make sure that's noted on the visitor card and in your CRM."). The middle deflection — "we can't pay your agent's commission" — has no written rebuttal. The candidate's reasoning paragraph ("The builder pays the buyer's agent at closing as part of the listing terms — same as any other sale") is an explanation, not a script. A resident under social pressure at a sign-in desk cannot recite a 45-word explanation; they need a 10-word rebuttal they can memorize. The walkthrough demo shot list stages this in shot 7 but the script never writes the actual lines. The video's core promised deliverable — a usable script for the sign-in desk — fails on its centerpiece beat.

**WEAKNESS-3 [MAJOR]: Lender Path 1 structurally reads as a pitch despite the disclosure.**
Path 1 names Matt Levant at Acre Mortgage with a personal CTA ("Worth a twenty-minute call to know what he's seeing"). Paths 2 and 3 are unnamed: "a VA lender who has actually closed VA on new construction in Bell County" and "Builder-preferred lenders." The asymmetry — one named person with a CTA, two unnamed categories — signals preference. A PGY-2 watching for trust signals will read this as a referral funnel disguised as education. The brief explicitly required "Three lender paths explained (NOT pitched)." The disclosure ("no lender is required") arrives later but doesn't repair the structural imbalance. Either name a specific VA specialist and a specific conventional broker (parallel structure), or name no one and describe categories only.

**WEAKNESS-4 [MAJOR]: "87% leak rate in County View" used unqualified in the hook is sample-size dishonest.**
County View had 15 sales total, 13 leaked. The 87% is mathematically correct but a viewer who Googles the neighborhood will see a small subdivision and dismiss the headline as cherry-picked. The defensible version anchors on the larger sample: "Even in Parks at Westfield, where 29 new homes sold, 55% of buyers walked in alone — sixteen of them." The Analyst persona's job is to catch exactly this — a stat that's accurate in narrow terms but designed to feel larger than the underlying data supports. Replacing the County View anchor with a larger-sample anchor preserves the impact and survives skeptical fact-check.

**WEAKNESS-5 [MAJOR]: Title #3 "Lost $40M to Temple Builders" is misleading framing.**
Buyers did not lose $40M to builders. About $40M in GMV transacted with no buyer-side representation present. The actual cost to the buyer cohort is foregone leverage (contract review, warranty advocate, upgrade negotiation, inspection coordination) — not a $40M wealth transfer. The phrase "lost to builders" implies the builders captured something the buyers should have kept. Comment-section will pushback: "Show me the math on what was actually lost." A defensible reframe: "$40M in Temple new construction closed with no buyer agent — here's what those buyers gave up."

**WEAKNESS-6 [MAJOR]: No mid-roll CTAs at minute 2, 4, 6.**
A 9:45 video carries exactly one close (9:00). YouTube retention curves drop predictably at 30s, 2min, 5min for educational content. The candidate has all its CTA energy stacked at the back. Viewers who bounce in the 4–6 minute cohort (the largest bounce cluster for explainer videos) never hear about the Builder Scorecard or Calendly. Insert short mid-rolls: at 3:00 after the 48-hour window beat, at 6:00 after the lender paths. Each one ~10 seconds, lower-third graphic for the link, no full sales pitch.

**WEAKNESS-7 [MAJOR]: Residency-to-closing-timing gap is unaddressed.**
A PGY-1 starting in late June or July with their first W-2 income arriving then, considering Temple new construction with typical 6–9 month build timelines, needs the timeline math: lock in May → close September → start payments before W-2. The Acre Mortgage mention gestures at this ("conventional underwriters will deny that loan") but doesn't show the actual decision tree: when to sign with the builder, when to lock rate, when income recognition kicks in. The Scout persona's brief explicitly flagged this gap. Without it, the video answers "should I get an agent?" but not "when do I do this relative to my residency contract?"

**WEAKNESS-8 [MAJOR]: Walkthrough demo at 3:00–5:00 is narrated, not visually cued.**
The script for the centerpiece beat (the 2-minute model-home walkthrough) reads as continuous narration. It describes the visitor card, the deflections, the response — but the script has zero cut markers ("[CUT to visitor card]", "[CUT to pen filling Agent line]", "[CUT to deflection-1 take]"). The B-roll list and walkthrough shot list have the assets staged separately. The script and shot list don't talk to each other. An editor following this script will produce 2 minutes of talking head with no visual variety — which is exactly when retention drops on a 9-min video. The script must cue when to cut, not leave it to the edit room.

**WEAKNESS-9 [MAJOR]: CTA path is circular and missing the Calendly URL.**
The description text says "If you're a BSW resident planning to look in the next 90 days, book a 20-min Calendly call. Link pinned in the comments." The pinned comment says "book a 20-min Calendly call (link in the description)." Neither location actually contains a Calendly URL. Viewer reaches a dead end. The correct setup: put one canonical Calendly link in the description, second copy of the same link in the pinned comment. Specify the URL or note it as a placeholder for Taylor to fill at upload.

**WEAKNESS-10 [MINOR]: Title #1 is generic.**
"BSW Residents: What to Do Before a Temple Model Home" is vague — "what to do" is the weakest possible verb construction. Compare to Title #2's "Playbook" — concrete, denotes a packaged answer. A PGY-2 scrolling YouTube has 1.2 seconds per title scan; high-density verbs win. "BSW Residents: The 60-Second Move Before a Temple Model Home" carries more information per scan-tick.

**WEAKNESS-11 [MINOR]: Stark Law reference in pinned comment lacks setup.**
First-time commenters see "Real estate brokerage and Stark Law are separate worlds; if anyone told you otherwise, they were wrong on the law." Without watching the video first, this reads as a non-sequitur — Stark Law has not been introduced. The pinned comment is the highest-leverage comment slot and should focus on the Builder Scorecard CTA. Move the Stark Law line into the description body where the legal frameworks are already established context.

**WEAKNESS-12 [MINOR]: "Builder pays the buyer's agent at closing as part of the listing terms — same as any other sale" overstates the parallel.**
New-construction buyer-agent compensation is sometimes a builder-published rate, sometimes structured into the home price, occasionally negotiated transaction by transaction. Saying it's "the same as any other sale" invites Reddit-style correction from a buyer who closed at a builder where the structure was different. Safer framing: "The builder has buyer-agent compensation built into the deal — you don't pay it out of pocket." This is true across structures and avoids overstating.

**VERDICT:** Hook over-promises a 60-second answer that arrives at 1:00, and the centerpiece walkthrough demo never writes the verbatim line for "we can't pay your agent" — these two FATAL gaps mean the candidate fails on its core retention promise and its core promised deliverable.
