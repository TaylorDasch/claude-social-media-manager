### [4:50–5:25] REGISTRATION HANDCUFF + PROCURING-CAUSE NAMING

[CUT to CU on visitor card with a highlighted "Register for future incentives" checkbox]

"One more thing at the sign-in desk — the registration handcuff. Some builders offer to 'register you for future incentives' or 'add you to a priority list' on the visitor card. If you're already represented and your agent is named on the card, registration is fine. If you check that box stand-alone before you've signed a 1502, it can be used later as a procuring-cause claim — the builder asserting they were the first contact and therefore earned the buyer-side compensation, not your agent. ### [7:55–8:55] LEGAL FRAMEWORKS + STARK LAW CORRECTION

[CUT to Taylor on-camera, plain background, slightly tighter framing]

"Last piece. Four real frameworks govern what builders and lenders can and cannot do with you.

- **RESPA Section 8** — federal Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act. Prohibits kickbacks between builders and lenders for steering buyers to a specific lender.
- **TREC Section 535.148** — Texas Real Estate Commission rule on broker conduct and undisclosed agency.
- **TREC Section 535.155** — advertising and disclosure requirements.
- **Texas DTPA Section 17.46** — Deceptive Trade Practices Act, false or misleading practices.

If a builder lies about whether you can be represented, or whether the price changes with a different lender, those are the laws that apply. Not Stark Law.

Here is the correction. Some people will tell you that Stark Law restricts you, as a physician or resident, from getting buyer representation or from receiving certain incentives. That is wrong. **Stark Law — 42 USC Section 1395nn — is a Medicare and Medicaid self-referral statute for healthcare services.** It governs what physicians can refer to entities they have a financial interest in within the healthcare system. It has zero application to real estate brokerage. It has zero application to a builder paying your buyer's agent at closing. If anyone — a sales rep, a colleague, a hospital finance officer — tells you Stark Law applies to your home purchase, they are wrong on the law. You can repeat that. You should repeat that.

I'm not your attorney. None of this is legal advice. I'm naming the actual frameworks. Stark Law is not one of them."
