## The angle (locked)

"Is Temple safe?" is the question every relocating buyer asks Taylor — and the honest answer is that no agent can responsibly answer it with a citywide score or a neighborhood ranking. The video's hook IS the refusal: *"I'm a Temple agent, I get asked this every week, and I'm not going to answer it the way you want — here's why, and here's the process that actually protects you."* Taylor then walks the same official-source process the page teaches: what Temple Police actually reported for 2024, why crime-rate websites disagree with each other, and the address-level checks (records, repeat visits, FEMA flood map, insurance/roof pricing) a buyer can run themselves.

## COMPLIANCE GUARDRAILS (NON-NEGOTIABLE — every draft is checked against these)

1. **Never label any city, neighborhood, street, school zone, or area "safe," "unsafe," "good," "bad," "sketchy," or "up and coming."** No rankings, no "areas I'd avoid," no steering language of any kind. This is fair-housing exposure for a licensed agent.
2. **No demographic references or proxies** — nothing about who lives anywhere, school "quality" as a safety proxy, or coded language.
3. **Official sources only, numbers verbatim from the page:** Temple Police 2024 Annual Report (crimes against persons 1,488; crimes against property 2,891; NIBRS transition), Texas DPS ORI TX0140700, FBI Crime Data Explorer, FEMA Map Service Center, Texas Department of Insurance. If a stat isn't on the page, it doesn't go in the script.
4. **The buyer decides.** Every segment ends with "here's how you check it for your address," never "here's what I'd choose."
5. **Disclaimer beat near the end** (mirror the page): information only; crime reports get revised; no prediction of future events; Equal Housing Opportunity.
6. Standard voice rules: no "dream home," "charming," "nestled," "hidden gem," "vibrant." Analyst tone, calm, direct.
