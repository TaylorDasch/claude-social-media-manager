What that percentage actually means for you, the buyer.

When more than half of sellers in a market have already cut, the negotiating posture flips. Three years ago, sellers held firm and buyers had to compete with five other offers. Today, the seller is the one watching the calendar. Two weeks, three weeks, no offers — they're talking to their agent about another cut. They know it. You know it.

[CUT to graphic: 'The signal in the cut']

Two — listed 30+ days, one cut already. This is your sweet spot. The seller has admitted the original number was wrong. Your offer should start below the NEW ask, not at it. They've signaled they'll move.

Three — listed 60+ days, zero cuts. This is the dangerous one. Either the seller doesn't actually need to sell — in which case they won't negotiate — or they're underwater and can't cut without coming to closing with money. Walk unless your inspection turns up a reason they HAVE to deal."

[CUT to graphic: median DOM bar chart, 30 days (2022) → 65 days (now), with "median sold DOM: 74 days" overlay]

"Median days on market for active Temple listings on May 14: 65 and a half days. Median for homes that closed in the last 30 days: 74. The slow listings drag the mean up to over 100, because the luxury segment is genuinely stuck — but the typical resale is selling in roughly two and a half months.

[CUT to Taylor on-camera]

Compare that to two years ago: 30 days. The 2021 'see it Friday, offer Saturday, beat by an out-of-town cash offer Sunday' market is done. For a seller, this is painful. For a buyer at today's interest rate, it's the only leverage you have to work with. The market hasn't crashed — it slowed down enough that you can negotiate. That's the trade.

Here's what 65-day DOM actually buys you:

You can see a house twice. You can sleep on it. You can pull comps. You can write an inspection contingency that lets you back out. You can ask for repairs without losing the deal to backup offers. None of that was available in 2021. All of it's available now. If your agent is telling you to waive contingencies and offer above ask — get a different agent. That advice was true in 2021 and it's wrong today."

### [5:45–7:45] CHANGE 3 — BUILDERS ARE NOW YOUR REAL COMPETITION FOR RESALE

[CUT to graphic: 1,218 active split into resale 870 + new construction 348, with 'New construction = 29% of inventory, 28% of sales']

"Twenty-nine percent of active Temple-Belton inventory is brand-new construction — homes built in 2024 or later. 348 of 1,218 listings. Of the 165 homes that closed in the last 30 days, 46 were new construction. Roughly the same 28%.

What that means: builders are absorbing about the same share of buyers as they have of inventory. They're not winning the market, but they're not losing either. They're holding par with rate buydowns, closing-cost credits, and the fact that the house has zero repair history. That's their lever.

Resale sellers cannot match a builder's incentive. They can only match it with a price cut — which is exactly what 52.6% of them are doing. The median cut is $15,000. The math is not a coincidence.

[CUT to graphic: side-by-side payment math example]

That saves a buyer roughly $300 to $400 per month for two years — call it $9,000. A resale seller in the same price band has to cut roughly $15,000 to bring monthly payments to the same place over a five-year hold. That's not theory — that's the median cut you're seeing in the data.

[CUT to Taylor on-camera]

Builder-specific incentives change month to month. I'm not going to quote rates that'll be wrong by next week. Tour one new construction and one resale in your band, same square footage. Run the actual monthly payment with the builder's incentive AND its expiration AND the HOA-PID-MUD math. Often the resale wins on long-term hold. But you cannot know without running the numbers."
