## DELIVERABLE 5 — THE TWO MARKETS INSIDE ONE CITY (1:30–4:30)

### 1:30–2:30 — Why "53% have price drops" misleads when read flat

**(A) [Back to kitchen-table wide. One physical artifact in shot: printed list of 8 sample listings, three highlighted.]**

"Here's the headline mistake. Someone reads 'fifty-three percent of listings have price cuts' and concludes the market is broken. Or that every Temple listing is open to a lowball. Both wrong.

The fifty-three percent number is true. But it averages two completely different inventory pools that have to be read separately. Let me show you.

Of those 459 listings with price drops, the median drop is fifteen thousand dollars on a $287K median. That's four-and-a-half percent. That's not panic. That's a seller who priced ambitiously, watched their listing sit thirty days, and corrected. That's a working market doing what it's supposed to do.

Then there's the other tail. Eighty-six listings have dropped ten percent or more from the original ask. That's where the real reductions are. And those are not distributed evenly. They're concentrated in two places — and once you know which two places, the fifty-three percent number tells a much more useful story."

**Lower-third 1:30–2:30:** "Pool A: median drop $15K (4.5%) — pricing correction. Pool B: ≥10% drops — concentrated in two segments."

### 2:30–3:30 — Where the ten-percent drops actually are

**(A) [Same set. Taylor reads from the printed list, points to highlighted rows.]**

"Pool B — the ten-percent-plus drops — clusters in two places. I pulled the eight largest drops in Temple's current active inventory. Here's what they share.

First cluster: **older luxury, north of $800K, that was priced into a 2022 market and is now finding 2026 air.** Lakeaire, Cliffs of Canyon Creek, Wildflower, Crooked Creek. Custom homes from the 1990s and early 2000s. Sellers anchored their list price to peak comp. The market moved. The ones still on the board today are the listings that haven't moved enough.

Second cluster: **new construction over $700K with builder spec inventory.** When a builder is sitting on a finished spec home past their internal carry-cost threshold, the price moves. Whatley Acres is one example I pulled. There are more.

Notice what's NOT on the heavily-discounted list. The sub-$350K move-in-ready three-bed two-bath in a 76502 commute corridor. That inventory pool — the one most first-time buyers and most physician hires are actually shopping — is closing within four percent of original ask. The median there is much tighter. Which brings me to..."

### 3:30–4:30 — The priced-right move-in-ready pool

**(A) [Same set. Taylor closes the printed list, faces camera.]**

"The priced-right move-in-ready three-bed two-bath in a desired Temple corridor — that's a different market entirely. That inventory still moves in under thirty days. Multiple offers happen there. The buyer who walks in expecting to lowball on every house is going to get outmaneuvered by a better-prepared buyer paying close to ask.

Here's the rule I'd hand a first-time buyer in Temple right now: **the price-cut leverage is real on listings that have aged past forty days, on listings outside the median size and price band, and on luxury or large new-construction spec where the carry cost is high.** It is NOT real on a clean 1,800-to-2,200 square foot home in a desired school feeder zone in the median price band. That house is going to do what a well-priced house has always done. Show, close, move on.

If you take one thing from this video, take that. Fifty-three percent of listings have price drops. The leverage from those drops is unevenly distributed. Know which pool you're shopping before you write."

**Lower-third 3:30–4:30:** "Buyer leverage by inventory pool — uneven distribution."

## DELIVERABLE 7 — HONEST COUNTERS: WHAT COULD CHANGE THE READ (8:00–9:30)

**(A) [Back to kitchen-table wide. Laptop visible to side, closed.]**

"This is the part most market-update videos skip. I'm going to tell you the three things that could change the read I just gave you, on a thirty-to-sixty-day window.

One. **Rates.** Every published number in this video assumes mortgage rates roughly where they sit at the date stamp on screen. If rates move fifty basis points either direction in the next thirty days, the priced-right move-in-ready pool either tightens further — rates down, more buyers in — or the aged inventory pool grows — rates up, more sellers stuck. I have no edge on what the Fed does. Neither does anyone on YouTube. Track the actual rate, not the YouTube predictions about the rate.

Two. **New construction supply.** Temple's months-of-supply number — about five point eight months on the active-to-30-day-sold ratio — gets pushed by builder release schedules. If three or four big builders push large spec inventory into the back half of May into June, the months-of-supply number rises, the price-cut percentage rises, and the leverage shifts further toward buyers. I have a separate tracker on builder incentives — linked below — and I'm watching that as my early warning.

Three. **The eighty-four-day median DOM is a backwards-looking number.** It's the median DOM on the listings that closed in the last thirty days. The listings going under contract THIS WEEK will close in May and June and become future DOM data. If those contract-to-close cycles are running shorter than what's already in the data — and there are some signs they are — the DOM number drops in the June and July pulls. If they're running longer, it rises. I'll re-pull this in thirty days and post an update.

If any of these three things flips materially, I'll do a follow-up. Subscribe if you want it. If none of them flip, the read I gave you holds through June."
