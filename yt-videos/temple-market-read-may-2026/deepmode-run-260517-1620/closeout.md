## 3. Top 3 Catches the Protocol Caught (vs. single-pass draft)

These are things a single-pass draft would have shipped that the protocol prevented:

### Catch 1 — Identity placement format violation
A single-pass draft would have spent 30 seconds building the hook before introducing Taylor (sentence 4 minimum). Brief's format rule demanded identity in first 3 sentences but not first 15s — paradoxical-looking, and easy to miss. Phase 4 self-critique caught it; Phase 5 collapsed two opening sentences so identity lands as sentence 3 at 0:18-0:24. Without the protocol, the video would have technically failed the format brief on its own opening.

### Catch 2 — Military PCS calendar contradiction
A single-pass draft (or the council-run alternate) might have plausibly told military buyers "you have time, let listings sit" — the same advice given to general relocators — because the price-drop pool intuitively reads as a buyer's-leverage market. Gemini's external check caught the contradiction with the school-year deadline and VA close-cycle math. Without that catch, the video would have given the most-deadline-pressured cohort dangerously wrong tactical advice — and a military spouse watching it would have caught it five seconds before walking away.

### Catch 3 — BSW timing-cycle honesty
A single-pass draft would have spoken to "BSW residents arriving for June start" as if the audience were uniformly early-cycle Match Day buyers. Gemini's external check forced the question: "what about the viewer watching this in May who already locked housing?" v3 explicitly addresses both audiences in the same section — tells already-locked viewers to skip ahead, tells late-deciders/fellows/dual-physician households this is the section that serves them. That honesty is what differentiates a working-agent video from a generic "BSW residents — buy now!" piece.
