## TOP 3 WINS THE COUNCIL LOCKED IN

These are the things the adversarial loop CAUGHT that a one-pass draft would have shipped.

### 1. Killed the disparate-impact landmine
**What the one-pass draft would have shipped:** A "Temple neighborhoods to avoid" video naming Western Hills (A- crime grade), Deerfield Estates (no data supports caution), South Temple (high steering risk), and a Hospital District (pin distortion not explained). Selection methodology silent. Both Fair Housing Lawyer and Contrarian judges flagged this as FATAL — the selection of named areas would have correlated with protected classes even with neutral language, creating disparate-impact exposure that's still operative federally regardless of HUD's recent guidance.

**What the council shipped instead:** A selection methodology declared on-camera at 0:24 AND restated at 9:06–9:18, with three explicit criteria (build-era + soil, data-aggregation artifact, tracked price movement). Crestview District named-and-folded on-camera as a flagged-but-folded corridor under the same rule (defensive against "you skipped the majority-minority corridor" challenges). Western Hills explicitly reframed as engineering-only with crime grade A- stated UP FRONT.

### 2. Killed the NAR Article 15 disparagement trap
**What the one-pass draft would have shipped:** Titles, hooks, and B-roll built around "most agents won't tell you this" / "your agent won't talk about this" / "agents working off old rules." It tested well on retention but creates real Article 15 (false/misleading statements about competing licensees) + DTPA exposure. Both Fair Housing Lawyer and Contrarian judges flagged the Title #1 disparagement framing as FATAL.

**What the council shipped instead:** Zero competence comparison anywhere in script, titles, thumbnails, or descriptions. Description disclaimer explicitly adds: "Statements are not commentary on any other licensee." The hook still has competitive punch via counter-intuitive contradiction ("violent crime every other week. It's a hospital.") — the energy is preserved without the legal landmine.

### 3. Reduced the HUD frame from load-bearing-but-unverified to verified-quote-only
**What the one-pass draft would have shipped:** Recitation of "HUD-26-028 case number, issued April 26th, 2026" in the cold open and throughout — a load-bearing legal premise that the Critic flagged as FATAL because the case number wasn't actually visible on the hud.gov news page itself (only the Secretary Turner quote was). Three of five judges (Fair Housing, Contrarian, Data) caught this same issue independently.

**What the council shipped instead:** Author B used WebFetch to actually verify the hud.gov page. The Turner quote was confirmed live. The case number and date specificity were dropped from on-camera language. The HUD frame still anchors the video's legal posture but it now load-bears on a quote you can re-verify the morning of filming. Inman + HousingWire URLs (which were 403'ing) removed from description; hud.gov is the sole HUD citation.
