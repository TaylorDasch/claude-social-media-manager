## CRESTVIEW DECISION + VOTE REASONING

**Recommendation: DO NOT NAME CRESTVIEW AS A CHAPTER. NAME IT AT 9:06–9:18 AS A FLAGGED-BUT-FOLDED CORRIDOR.**

**Reasoning:**

1. **Selection-rule discipline, not geography avoidance.** The methodology declared at the open (Criteria 1, 2, 3) restates at 9:06–9:18. Crestview qualifies on Criterion 1 (1913–1940 build era + Houston Black soil). S Streets (Area #2) qualify on Criterion 1 PLUS active-infill block-variance. **Inclusion goes to the corridor pairing the most independent triggers.** Crestview's distinctive editorial layer (pre-WWII + reinvestment + East Heights Temple infill) is already in Area #2 — same buying problem, same inspection prescription. Adding a separate chapter duplicates analysis without adding a new inspection action.

2. **Fair-housing posture is STRONGER by naming-and-folding than by silent omission.** Crestview overlaps the historically Black East Temple corridor (8th St / MLK Jr Drive — never named by those street names in the video). NAR Article 10 + disparate-impact theory remain operative federally. **Naming Crestview on-camera as a flagged-but-folded corridor under a consistent selection rule applied citywide is the strongest available posture against a "video singles out a majority-minority corridor" challenge.** Silent omission looks like geography avoidance and is harder to defend if questioned.

3. **Data quality on Crestview is mixed + creates a pacing problem.** Median up 8% YoY at $164,950, compressed by older smaller stock — mix-shift caveat needs 30+ seconds to explain. Bellaire gives a cleaner defensible data point: down 12% YoY at $199K, same engineering category. More analytical weight in less airtime with less legal surface.

**Bellaire wins fourth slot:** Tracked-price-movement at highest data quality; mix-shift caveat in-beat preserves analytical posture; north Temple coverage broadens master-map spread.
