#### ACT 1: The Three Projects — What Is Actually Being Built (1:15–4:00)

Talking points:
- **Meta (Polmer LLC)** is the flagship. $800M minimum capital. 393 acres at NW H K Dodgen Loop / Eberhardt Road / Industrial Blvd. About 900,000 square feet across three buildings.
- Originally broke ground 2022 → paused for AI cooling redesign → resumed under JE Dunn October 2023. Phased completion late 2026 into 2027.
- Permanent jobs: 40 minimum, scaling to ~100. Average $90K/year.
- 100% renewable energy commitment.
- **Rowan Digital Infrastructure** is the heavyweight by capacity. 707 acres at 1707 and 2351 Bob White Road. 300 MW. Capital $700M–$1.2B.
- City of Temple approved Tax Abatement Reinvestment Zone #47 — covers 554 acres. $15.8M incentive package. 10-year, 50% partial abatement on the improvement value.
- Power capacity secured through Oncor. Permanent staffing: 30 Phase 1, scaling to 40 total by end of 2029. Average $80K/year.
- **Oppidan** — the satellite. 10 acres at 2325 Eberhardt Road, directly across from Meta. 5MW. 61,554 sq ft. $27–31M. Fewer than 5 permanent jobs. Largely unstaffed satellite node.

**Visual:** Desk + screen-share showing a simple 3-column comparison table (Meta / Rowan / Oppidan) with the key data points. B-roll: drive-by of the Meta site (NW H K Dodgen Loop) inserted around the Meta section. Map graphic showing the two corridors (Industrial Park north + Bob White south).

#### ACT 2: The Capital-to-Jobs Ratio (4:00–5:30)

Talking points:
- Headline math: $1.5 billion of capital coming to Bell County. Fewer than 150 permanent jobs combined across all three projects when fully stabilized.
- That's about **$10 million of capital per permanent job**. Manufacturing for comparison is ~$260K per permanent job.
- So when an agent or YouTube channel tells you "data centers create jobs that boost home prices" — point them at this number. The jobs aren't coming. The capital is. Those are completely different stories for your home value.
- The construction-phase headcount is real — about 2,000 workers concurrently through 2026/2027. But those are transient. They live in furnished mid-term rentals, RVs, or commute from outside the area. They aren't buying single-family homes in West Temple.

**Visual:** Screen-share Taylor's published "Capital-to-Jobs Ratio Meter" graphic (already on /data-center-impact/ page). B-roll: cut to construction site walk-up (use Tuesday on-location capture).

#### ACT 3: Tax Abatements + What the Commercial Tax Base Actually Does (5:30–7:00)

Talking points:
- Both Meta and Rowan secured 10-year, ~50% partial property tax abatements.
- Meaning: the City of Temple's general fund does NOT see undiluted commercial property tax revenue from these facilities until the **mid-2030s**.
- This kills the "data center = lower residential property taxes next year" narrative. That's not the deal that was negotiated.
- The honest 10-year story: as these projects roll off abatement starting around 2034, Temple's commercial-to-residential tax base ratio shifts meaningfully. The structural pressure on residential tax rates is downward over a 10-year horizon.
- Hedge clearly: I am NOT predicting Temple's tax rate drops next year. I'm saying the long-term forces favor Temple homeowners who plan to hold past 2030.
- If you're buying a home you'll sell in 3 years, this story doesn't apply to you yet.

**Visual:** Simple timeline graphic — 2026 (now) → 2034 (abatement rolls off) → 2036+ (full tax base effect). B-roll: Bell CAD building exterior.

#### ACT 4: Where the Impact Actually Hits (Geography) (7:00–8:30)

Talking points:
- Only one specific area is in the actual impact zone for relocators: **South Temple along the Bob White Road corridor.**
- Rowan's 707 acres going in there. South Pointe and the I-35 / outer loop adjacency. Industrial buildout.
- Belton secondary radius — picks up some of the construction-phase rental demand.
- **West Temple is NOT in the data-center impact zone.** Don't let any agent tell you "buy in West Temple before Meta drives prices up" — Meta's site is in the industrial park, not next to a residential subdivision. The impact is industrial, not residential.
- If you're a relocator looking at neighborhoods like Lake Pointe, Canyon Ridge, Wyndham Hill, The Groves at Lakewood Ranch — these are NOT data-center plays. Buy them for their actual merits.
