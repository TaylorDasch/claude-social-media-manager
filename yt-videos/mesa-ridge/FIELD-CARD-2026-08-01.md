## The one on-site test that matters most

At 2032, stand:

1. Under the awning at normal eye level.
2. Where a patio table would sit.
3. Beside the pavers.
4. At the rear fence, looking back toward the house.

Count only what you can actually see:

- Second-story rear/side windows
- Grade differences
- Directly aligned rear lots
- Tree/fence screening
- Open lots that may be built later

Safe line:

> “What I notice on the ground is a real mix of one- and two-story homes. On some lots, a privacy fence does not answer the whole sight-line question.”

Do not call it a defect or a neighborhood-wide problem.

## Open-house brain sparks

Use these to sharpen the video while you are there. They are prompts, not facts.

- From a seated patio position, which upper windows are visible?
- Does the sight-line concern change from one block to another?
- Does the shed solve a bigger storage problem than the lawn area it uses?
- Does the retractable awning materially change the afternoon shade?
- Does 1,849 square feet feel efficient as a one-story 4/3, or do rooms feel tight?
- Which addition would be most annoying for a new-build buyer to add later: awning, shed, pavers, fence/gate, landscaping, or shelving?
- Where does the streetscape visibly shift from smaller production homes to larger product?
- Is construction traffic, dust, or noise present today?
- Are sidewalks continuous on your route?
- What happens to street flow when cars are parked on both sides?
- Can SH 317 road noise be heard from the subject front yard or backyard?
- Are there visible drainage slopes, curb inlets, erosion, or low spots that deserve an inspection question?
- What are visitors cross-shopping against Mesa Ridge?
- Are they choosing one story, more square footage, newness, or completed improvements?
- Who would you actively tell to skip this neighborhood?

Best additional observations, if proven today:

1. Privacy changes lot by lot.
2. Product and price change within a few blocks.
3. A finished resale can solve the year-one project list.
4. Four bedrooms/three baths can be efficient without being a large house.
5. Construction and streetscape maturity change by phase.
