## #3 - Valor Estates + Bella Terra Executive

**[1:15-1:35 | B-roll: slow walk past Valor Estates street sign]**

Number three is really two neighborhoods across the same South Temple lane: Valor Estates and Bella Terra Executive.

Valor is the bigger-lot, build-path option. Bella Terra Executive is the more practical move-in-sooner option.

And the reason I pair them is because a lot of $500K-plus buyers end up comparing both in the same weekend.

**[1:35-2:15 | On camera at Valor street sign, Taylor left. Right-third Valor identity card.]**

Valor has the strongest emotional hook of any neighborhood in this video.

Every street is named after a Bell County service member killed in action.

So before we even talk about square footage or builder specs, understand what the neighborhood is trying to be. It is not just another subdivision with big lots. It is built around a military-honor story, and for the right buyer, that matters.

**[2:15-2:55 | Drone or wide lot footage]**

The real estate hook is the lot size.

Valor is the place you look when you want estate-lot breathing room without leaving South Temple. In the May 23 MLS pull, the active Valor home I found was listed at $709,900, and recent solds ranged from $589,000 to $738,000.

So this is not entry-level Temple new construction. This is a buyer saying, "I want room for a pool, a larger backyard, a side-entry garage, and a house that feels more permanent."

**[2:55-3:25 | Cut to Bella Terra exterior / Carothers style homes]**

Bella Terra is the practical counterpoint.

In the same May 23 pull, Bella Terra had active homes from the high $300Ks up to $569,000, with six active homes at or above $500K. That gives you a wider entry point than Valor, and you can still get the newer-home feel, the South Temple location, and the Carothers Executive tier if you are in that upper band.

**[3:25-4:10 | On camera, Taylor left when stopped. Right-third "Who fits / who skips" card.]**

Who fits here?

This is for the buyer who wants new construction and more room outside than a normal production subdivision gives them.

It's for the young attending, the military-connected buyer, the BSW professional, or the Austin seller who does not need an old estate, but also does not want a tiny lot.

Who should skip it?

If you want mature trees, established landscaping, and a neighborhood that already feels settled, Valor may feel too early-stage. Bella Terra is more built out, but it is still a newer-subdivision environment.

And the honest downside is timing. Newer premium neighborhoods can look incredible on paper, but the finished feel depends on construction pace, HOA execution, neighboring lots, and what is actually available when you are ready to buy.

**[4:10-4:25 | Right-third graphic: "Best fit: estate-lot new construction / Skip if: you want mature, established feel"]**

So Valor and Bella Terra are number three: strongest future-lane story, best larger-lot new-construction angle, but not the most proven luxury resale lane yet.

## #2 - Cliffs of Canyon Creek

**[4:25-4:50 | B-roll: Canyon Creek drive-in, trees, curved streets, custom exteriors]**

Number two is Cliffs of Canyon Creek.

This is the opposite of Valor.

Valor says new, open, build your thing. Cliffs says established, private, custom, and a little harder to replace.

**[4:50-5:35 | On camera near public street, Taylor left. Right-half Cliffs data card. No private-property close filming.]**

When people from Austin or Dallas hear "luxury," they usually picture brand-new. In Temple, that is not always the best answer.

Sometimes the better answer is an older custom home on a bigger lot, with mature trees, a real street canopy, and a setting you cannot mass-produce.

That is the Cliffs of Canyon Creek story.

The May 23 MLS pull showed twelve active homes in the Cliffs sections I tracked. The active median was about $374,450, but the top active listing was $529,000, and the trailing sold data included a $925,000 sale.

That tells you something important: this area is not one price band. It is a mixed estate lane. You need to evaluate the specific house, not just the subdivision name.

**[5:35-6:15 | B-roll: slow drive-by of exterior variety, driveway scale, trees, creek-area shots if public/legal]**

What you are buying here is not just a house.

You are buying setting.

More mature landscaping. More architectural variety. More one-off homes. Less of the "same elevation, same lot, same finish package" feel.

And if you are coming from Austin, the shock is not just price. It is land.

In Austin-area estate neighborhoods, the dirt itself can eat your budget before you even talk about the house. In the Cliffs, you can still find custom-home settings at a number that would be a teardown conversation in parts of Austin.

**[6:15-7:05 | On camera, Taylor left. Right-third due-diligence stack: roof / HVAC / drainage / foundation / trees.]**

Who fits here?

The buyer who wants privacy, mature trees, a less repetitive street, and a home that does not feel like everyone else's.

This can be a strong fit for an established BSW professional, a buyer who has already owned before, or someone who wants a house with character and is not afraid of inspection details.

Who should skip it?

If you want builder warranties, brand-new systems, and zero old-house questions, this is probably not where I start you.

Custom and established can be beautiful, but it can also mean roof age, drainage, HVAC age, pool condition, and renovation taste from a previous owner.

**[7:05-7:35 | On-screen checklist: roof / HVAC / drainage / foundation / trees / insurance]**

The honest downside is due diligence.

At this price point, people get impressed by the setting and stop looking at the boring stuff. That is backwards.

In the Cliffs, the boring stuff is the deal: roof, HVAC, drainage, foundation movement, tree maintenance, insurance, and whether the floor plan still matches how people live now.

**[7:35-7:55 | Right-third graphic: "#2 Cliffs of Canyon Creek - best established estate lane"]**

So Cliffs of Canyon Creek is number two because it gives you something new construction cannot: maturity, custom variety, and a true estate feel close to BSW.

But number one is the area I would usually start with for the cleanest BSW relocation decision.

## #1 - Legacy Ranch

**[7:55-8:20 | B-roll: BSW exit, commute timer, drive to Legacy Ranch entrance]**

Number one is Legacy Ranch.

Not because it is the most expensive.

Not because every house there is better than every house in the Cliffs.

It is number one because it solves the cleanest buyer problem: I want a newer premium home, close to BSW, in a neighborhood that is easy to understand before I move here.

**[8:20-9:05 | On camera at Legacy Ranch entrance, Taylor left. Right-half Legacy Ranch card.]**

The commute is the headline.

From Legacy Ranch to Baylor Scott & White, you are talking roughly a few minutes down South 31st, depending on traffic and which part of the neighborhood you are leaving from.

For a normal buyer, that is convenient.

For somebody working hospital hours, that can be the difference between "this house works" and "this house is annoying every week."

**[9:05-9:45 | B-roll: model exterior, entry, kitchen, ceiling height, backyard, community pool/trails if filmed]**

The second reason is product clarity.

Legacy Ranch gives you newer construction, a more premium streetscape, and builders like Arnold Design + Build and John Houston Homes in the conversation.

You get the kind of things buyers actually care about at this level: floor plan, ceiling height, insulation, garage space, outdoor space, and finish quality.

And because it is more legible than a one-off custom estate, it is easier for an out-of-town buyer to compare options.

**[9:45-10:30 | Taylor left or voiceover. Right-half screen graphic: May 23 MLS snapshot.]**

From the May 23 MLS pull, Legacy Ranch Phase II had active homes at $450,000 and $510,000, and Phase I had a recent $715,000 close in the data.

That is important because Legacy Ranch is not only a "$700K-plus or nothing" conversation.

It is a premium-lane conversation. Depending on the home, you may be comparing a high-$400K or low-$500K new-build option against an older custom home, or a larger executive build against Austin-area pricing.

That is where a lot of buyers get lost.

They see one price online and assume the whole neighborhood is that price. It is not.

**[10:30-11:15 | On camera, front of model or clean street view, Taylor left. Right-third "Who fits / who skips" card.]**

Who fits here?

This is where I usually start a BSW relocation buyer who wants the cleanest version of Temple premium housing.

You want newer construction. You want a straightforward commute. You want a neighborhood that makes sense quickly. You do not want to spend three months learning every custom pocket in Temple before you can make a decision.

Who should skip it?

If you want acreage, older trees, a one-of-one estate, or the lowest price per square foot possible, Legacy Ranch may not be your best fit.

The honest downside is that you are paying for convenience, newness, and location. Those are real values, but they are still premiums.

**[11:15-11:45 | B-roll: Legacy Ranch recap montage]**

That is why Legacy Ranch is number one.

It is not the flashiest story in this video.

It is the cleanest decision for the most common premium Temple buyer: someone who wants a very nice house, close to the hospital, without turning the search into a research project.

## The Austin Comparison

**[11:45-12:35 | Taylor left. Right-half Temple vs Austin-style comparison, no exact Austin claim unless listing is verified day-of.]**

Now, let's talk about Austin, because this is where people either get too salesy or too simplistic.

The point is not "Temple is cheap."

The point is that the same housing dollar buys a different life.

In Temple, $500K to $750K can put you in a newer premium home, a larger-lot build path, or an established custom setting close to the hospital.

In Austin or the closer Austin suburbs, that same dollar often turns into a smaller house, an older house, a longer commute, or a much more competitive search.

Do not take my word for it. Before you film this, pull three live Austin-area listings and put them next to three Temple options. Same budget. Same day. Let the viewer see the gap.

**[12:35-13:15 | On camera, Taylor left. Right-third honesty card: "Still verify taxes, schools, HOA, commute."]**

But here is the part most agents will not say.

You are buying a premium house in a mid-size Texas city.

If you need high-end retail ten minutes away, a deep private-school ecosystem, a large country-club scene, and Austin-level restaurant density, Temple is not Austin with a discount sticker.

It is a different life.

But if what you actually want is space, a shorter work routine, a strong house, and real money left over compared with the bigger metros, Temple makes a lot of sense.

That is the honest trade.
