## THE AFFORDABILITY BRIDGE — the unlock, not a second topic (10:15–12:45)

> "Now — the part that turns this list from interesting into *yours.* Which of these five is actually in reach comes down to two numbers nobody puts in a relocation video. Here they are.

> First — and I'm not going to insult you by guessing your salary. You know your number; it's on your offer letter, and a resident's number and an attending's number are different planets. So instead of guessing, here's the math you can do tonight: take your gross monthly pay and multiply by twenty-eight percent. That's the house payment that keeps you sleeping at night. Then anchor it to real Temple money — a two-hundred-fifty-thousand-dollar home in Western Hills or Wyndham Hill runs roughly twenty-four to twenty-six hundred a month all-in, with our Texas property taxes baked in. Step up to The Grove around four-thirty and you're roughly four thousand a month. Bring your letter to a lender and they'll place you on that ladder in ten minutes — but now you can ballpark your own rung tonight. These five neighborhoods aren't random. They're a budget ladder, and you already know which rung you're standing on.
>
> Second — the one that changes everything for medical buyers — the **physician loan.** If you're an MD, DO, or dentist, many of these programs let you put little or nothing down, skip PMI, and — this is the big one — treat your student debt far more gently than a normal mortgage. Some ignore deferred loans entirely; some count only your income-based payment. It varies by lender, which is exactly why you talk to a physician-loan specialist, not a retail loan officer. And most of these programs let residents qualify on a signed offer letter — often before your first shift. That one tool is frequently the whole difference between 'I'll rent for three years' and 'I'll buy in Wyndham Hill the month I move' — and it's what gives an attending more room than the sticker price suggests. The ladder's a floor, not a cage.
>
> Two honest cautions, because I'm not a brochure. One: if you're a resident here three years and then matching out, buying is *not* automatic — sometimes renting genuinely wins, and I did that exact math in a separate video, it's linked. Two: nurses, most physician loans are doctor-and-dentist only — but you've got your own path, down-payment help that can cover most of what you'd put down, and I made you your own video too. Watch the right one for you. *This* video is the map. Those two numbers are the money."
