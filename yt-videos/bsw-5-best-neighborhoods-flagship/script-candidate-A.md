## #1 + #2 — THE TWO UNDER-TEN-MINUTE NEIGHBORHOODS (1:40–5:30)

### Western Hills — established, closest to the district (1:40–3:40)
> "Number one and number two are basically tied, so I'm giving them to you together — because they answer the same question two different ways. The question is: *do you want to be there in under ten minutes, established or brand-new?*
>
> First, established: **Western Hills.** Seventy-six-five-oh-four, right up against the hospital district. From here I clocked **ten minutes to BSW, seven minutes to McLane** — McLane is the closest in the whole video — and about thirteen to the VA. The median price right now is **two hundred forty-nine, nine hundred.** Range runs from about a hundred-fifty up to the low four-sixties for the big remodels.
>
> Here's the honest read on Western Hills. The homes are older — sixties, seventies, eighties. Mature trees, big lots, real character, and walkable in a way newer Temple isn't. But you're buying *established*, which means some of these need work. The flip side is that's where your upside is — a tired three-bedroom you renovate over a couple years, eight minutes from your unit, in a neighborhood that isn't making any more land. If you're a resident who wants the shortest possible drive and you're handy, or you want a project with mature bones — this is your block. I had one at thirty-four-oh-one Buffalo Trail, eighteen hundred square feet, new roof, redone primary bath, two-seventy-five. That's the Western Hills play."

### Wyndham Hill — new construction, value, Academy ISD (3:40–5:30)
> "Now, same under-ten-minute commute, but you want *new* instead of a remodel project. That's **Wyndham Hill**, and it's number two. South Temple. I clocked **nine minutes to BSW — the fastest in this whole video — and nine to McLane.** Median price: **two hundred eighty thousand,** and the range is tight, two-twenty to three-forty-five, so you know what you're getting.
>
> Two things make Wyndham Hill the value pick. One: it's newer construction — a lot of it built in the last ten, twelve years, Omega Builders — so you get the open floor plan, the low-maintenance, the no-surprises home a resident or a nurse working twelve-hour shifts actually wants. Two — and write this down because I got it wrong on camera once and I'm not doing it again — **Wyndham Hill is Academy ISD. Not Temple ISD. Academy.** That's a separate, smaller, well-regarded district, and if you've got kids it changes your whole calculation, so confirm the school by the exact address before you fall in love with a house.
>
> Who's it for? The nurse who wants a brand-new three-bed without a project. The two-income couple who wants nine minutes to work and a yard. The resident who'd rather buy new than renovate. These move fast — median's under fifty days on market — so when one fits, you move."

**Why it works:** the "tied, two answers to one question" framing turns the 1-min commute ambiguity into a clean feature; honest negatives (older stock) with the upside reframed (renovation = equity); the Academy ISD correction is delivered as earned credibility ("I got it wrong once"); real addresses = receipts; each closes with a crisp "who it's for."

## #3 — HARTRICK RANCH: the range play + the leverage (5:30–8:00)

> "Number three is where the budget opens up, and it's the one I'd watch most closely if you're buying in the next ninety days. **Hartrick Ranch.** West Temple. **Thirteen minutes to BSW, twelve to McLane.** Temple ISD.
>
> What makes Hartrick different is the *range.* In one neighborhood you've got a brand-new builder three-bedroom around two-sixty-five — I had a Stylecraft and a Kiella both right there — and you've got move-up homes pushing seven-hundred-thousand-plus. The median's about **three-oh-six.** So this is the neighborhood that grows with you: buy the starter as a resident, move up to the bigger house as an attending, never leave the street.
>
> But here's the real reason I'm flagging it. The median home in Hartrick Ranch has been sitting on the market about **a hundred and twenty-three days.** Four months. In real estate, that is a *lot* of time — and time on market is leverage. For *you.* It means sellers and builders out there are negotiable right now in a way they are not in a hot neighborhood. Price cuts, closing-cost help, rate buy-downs. If you're relocating and you hate to negotiate, this is exactly the kind of neighborhood where having someone do it for you pays for itself. That's not a pitch — that's just what a hundred-and-twenty-three-day market is."

**Why it works:** "watch most closely / next ninety days" creates urgency; the range = "grows with you" is a real benefit for the career arc; the DOM-123 leverage callout is a genuine, data-grounded insight competitors don't give; "that's not a pitch — that's what the market is" pre-empts the salesy read (analyst voice).

This is where the two-physician household lands. The median home in The Grove has been on the market about **a hundred thirty-eight days.** Even in the attending tier — *especially* in the attending tier, because there are fewer buyers up here — builders are dealing. So if you're an attending walking in with a physician loan thinking you've got no negotiating room because it's new construction? You've got more than you think. We'll come back to that loan in a second."

## #5 — THREE CREEKS, BELTON: the trade (10:30–12:30)

> "Number five is the trade. And it's the one that breaks the pattern, so pay attention. **Three Creeks** — and notice, this one's in *Belton,* not Temple. **Twenty minutes to BSW, eighteen to McLane.** So yeah — you're adding maybe ten minutes over the close-in neighborhoods. Here's what you get for those ten minutes.
>
> One: it's **Belton ISD** — a different, highly-rated district, and for a lot of relocating medical families that alone is the reason. Two: it's the big amenity community — Three Creeks is built around a resort-style lagoon and lazy river, parks, trails. If you've got kids and you're moving them away from everything they know, that amenity center is the thing that makes the new place feel like a win instead of a loss. And three — this is the part that surprises people — it's *not* expensive for what it is. The median is about **three-twenty,** with brand-new D.R. Horton and Stylecraft homes from the two-forties up to about five-twenty. The furthest neighborhood in this video is one of the best values in it.
>
> So who picks Three Creeks? The family that decides Belton schools and a lazy river beat ten minutes of drive time. The nurse with two kids who wants new construction and a community. If that's you — and you'd be surprised how many medical families land here — those ten extra minutes buy a lot.
>
> Quick honorable mention while we're out here: if what you actually want is land and small-town quiet and top-rated schools, look at **Salado** — about the same drive, median around five-thirty, and you're buying acreage. Different video, but I didn't want to pretend it doesn't exist."

**Why it works:** "breaks the pattern" earns attention for the furthest/weakest-commute beat; the value subversion (furthest ≠ priciest) is a genuine surprise; the lazy-river/kids angle hits the relocating-family emotion (moving kids away from everything); Belton ISD introduces the third district; the Salado one-liner shows honesty/breadth without bloating the runtime. Plants the flag in the competitor's Belton fortress.

## THE AFFORDABILITY BRIDGE — the differentiator (12:30–15:00)

> "Okay. Commutes and neighborhoods — that's half the question. Here's the other half, the one nobody answers honestly: *what can you actually afford?*
>
> I'm not going to insult you by guessing your salary. You know your number — it's on your offer letter, and a resident's number and an attending's number are different planets. So instead of guessing, let me give you the two things that actually decide what you can buy.
>
> First, the rule of thumb: keep your total house payment around a third of your take-home, and you'll sleep at night. Match that to the neighborhoods — a third of a resident's take-home tends to land you in Western Hills or Wyndham Hill. A third of an attending's lands you in The Grove. The neighborhoods in this video aren't random — they're a budget ladder, and you already know which rung you're on.
>
> Second — and this is the one that changes everything for medical buyers — **the physician loan.** If you're an MD, a DO, or a dentist, there's a mortgage built for you: zero percent down, no PMI, and — this is the big one — it ignores your student loans. Two hundred grand in medical-school debt that would sink a normal mortgage application? The physician lender either ignores it or counts the tiny income-based payment. And residents qualify on a *signed offer letter* — you can buy before your first shift. That one loan is often the entire difference between 'I'll rent for three years' and 'I'll buy in Wyndham Hill the month I move.' It's also frequently what moves an attending from a three-hundred-thousand-dollar house to The Grove.
>
> Now — two honest cautions, because I'm not a brochure. One: if you're a resident here for three years and then matching out, buying isn't automatic — sometimes renting genuinely wins, and I made a whole separate video doing that exact math, it's linked. Two: nurses, most physician loans are doctor-and-dentist only — but you've got your *own* path, down-payment assistance that can cover almost your entire down payment, and I made you your own video too. Watch the right one for you. The point of *this* video is the map. Those two are the money."

**Why it works:** "I'm not going to insult you by guessing your salary" flips the salary-compliance constraint into a trust move and directly answers the #1 comment (pain point #3); the rule-of-thumb ties affordability back to the neighborhood ladder (makes the list actionable); the physician loan is the high-value hook for attendings without becoming the P3 video; the two cautions (rent-vs-buy → P2, nurses → P4) cross-link the playlist honestly and keep lane discipline; "no invented salary" rule honored throughout.
