> Off-post wins if your tour is 3 years or longer. If you'd take a Belton ISD or Temple ISD over base proximity. If you'd rather build equity than carry BAH equals rent forever. If the on-post waitlist for your tier is 60-plus days. And — this is the big one — if you're 70% or higher disabled.

## CHAPTER VI — THE 100% DISABLED VET LEVER (7:45–9:00)

**[VISUAL: Bold callout. Heading: "TX TAX CODE 11.131"]**

**ON CAMERA:**

> This is the single most under-utilized affordability lever in Central Texas military buying.
>
> Texas grants a total residence homestead property tax exemption to veterans rated 100% service-connected disabled, or with individual unemployability determination. Total. Exemption. The property tax line of PITI becomes zero.

**[VISUAL: Whiteboard math.
Line 1: $300K Belton home · standard PITI ≈ $X
Line 2: Same home · 100% disabled exemption · PITI ≈ $X minus $500–$600
Line 3: Effective rate reduction ≈ 1.5–2 percentage points]**

> A 100%-rated buyer in a $300K Belton home saves roughly $500 to $600 a month versus the same buyer without the exemption. That's the equivalent of buying down your interest rate by 1.5 to 2 percentage points. It also raises your affordability ceiling 15 to 25%.
>
> Two things most agents miss. **One** — the exemption applies to your residence homestead only. The day this property converts to a rental on PCS-out, the exemption ends. Plan the exit timing.
>
> **Two** — it isn't automatic. You file with the Bell County Appraisal District — BCAD — after deed transfer. Miss the deadline and you pay full tax for that year.

**[CAPTION CALLOUT: "70–99% rated: $12,000 assessed-value reduction · saves ~$240/year"]**

**ON CAMERA:**

> If you're rated 70 to 99%, you get a $12,000 assessed-value reduction. Modest — about $240 a year on a $300K home.
>
> If you're 100% rated and your lender pre-approves you to the full-tax PITI instead of the exempted PITI, that lender just knocked $40 to $60K off your purchase ceiling. Get the exempted underwrite in writing before you tour homes.

## CHAPTER VII — EXIT STRATEGY (9:00–10:00)

**[VISUAL: Whiteboard math.
"CLEAN RENTAL CONVERSION:
- Fair market rent ≥ 1.05 × PITI
- + 8–10% property management
- + 5% vacancy reserve
- + $100/mo maintenance reserve"]**

**ON CAMERA:**

> The Fort Hood buying decision usually pivots on the exit. If you can convert this property to a clean rental on PCS-out — and it covers PITI plus property management plus vacancy plus maintenance reserve — the long-tour buy math wins clearly.
>
> If it can't, you're playing a 7 to 8% transaction-cost coin flip on appreciation. And you'd better win the flip.
>
> The clean math: fair market rent covers 1.05 times PITI, plus 8 to 10% property management, plus 5% vacancy reserve, plus $100 a month maintenance reserve. In Temple and Belton, that math typically works on a $260K to $320K home in BSW-corridor or Belton ISD zones. In Killeen, the math gets brittle in heavily-military subdivisions where rental supply dumps during brigade cycles.
>
> Three operational notes. Tenant pool — Temple and Belton have BSW medical, UMHB, civilian, and military all competing. Killeen leans heavily military in many subdivisions. Property management — budget 8 to 12% for a competent local PM. Self-managing from your next duty station is a fool's errand for most. Insurance — Texas insurance has hardened since 2022. A converted rental needs landlord-policy underwriting, not homeowner. The premium delta is real.

**[LITTLE GEM CALLOUT: "VA loan + PCS inside 12 months — talk to your loan officer BEFORE listing as a rental. There's a clean path with orders, but it needs documentation."]**
