## 3. WATER — RECONCILED NUMBERS (city FAQ is the authority)
- All projects under the framework: **closed-loop cooling required**; Project Temple's system described as closed-loop **dry** cooling.
- Initial fill: **"approximately 2 million gallons total"** (city FAQ — supersedes the package's 1–1.5M; script's "fill it once and recirculate for over a decade" phrasing stays safe).

## 4. SETBACKS / BUFFERS / NOISE — WHAT THE CITY'S FRAMEWORK ACTUALLY SAYS
- Mitigation the city may require per project: **"berms, walls, barriers, equipment enclosures, setbacks, screening, or operational controls."** No citywide fixed distances published on the hub — commitments are per-project (zoning conditions + agreements).
- Noise: city code decibel limits apply; **"if a validated noise issue occurs after operation begins, the City may require additional mitigation and may use available enforcement tools."**
- The city's own CAN/CANNOT list (great neutral graphic): CAN — annex, zone, require utility agreements, meter water, mandate closed-loop, require traffic/noise studies, enforce ordinances. CANNOT — control ERCOT, regulate Oncor, rewrite state electric policy, eliminate state tax exemptions, **take private property, or ban lawful development**.

## 5. THE GILBERT HOUSE — "IS IT COMPLIANT?" ANSWER
- Yes — and the reason is the story: **2917 Eberhardt sits on land platted as Temple Industrial Park Section VI**, and Meta's campus is the Enterprise Industrial Park. This corridor has been platted industrial for decades; the farmhouse is the legacy use that stayed while the industrial park grew around it. Nobody rezoned a neighborhood around a house — the house is the last non-industrial holdout inside an industrial park.
- The city can require screening/berms per project but cannot take the property or block lawful industrial development across the road (its own FAQ language above).
