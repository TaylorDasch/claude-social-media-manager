### BEAT 3 — THE PROGRAM (THIS IS WHERE IT LANDS) (~1:45–3:00)

**[TALKING HEAD → cut to full-frame graphic for the myth-bust]**
> "First, let me kill the thing you've probably already been told. Somebody — a coworker, a Facebook group, a lender's email — is going to tell you nurses are 'Texas Heroes,' so you get the heroes home program. Here's the honest part: as a working hospital RN, you usually don't.

**ON-SCREEN (full-frame, two-column):**
> **MYTH:** "Nurses = Texas Heroes"
> **REALITY:** Homes for Texas Heroes nurses = K–12 PUBLIC SCHOOL nurses + nursing/allied-health COLLEGE faculty. A working hospital RN is *not* a "Texas Hero" under it.

> "That program — Homes for Texas Heroes — counts public school nurses and nursing-school faculty. The other groups on it are teachers, EMS, peace officers, firefighters, veterans, corrections officers. A floor RN at Baylor Scott & White is not on that list. So if someone steers you there, that's the wrong door."

**[CUT BACK TO TALKING HEAD]**
> "Here's the door that actually fits you. It's called TSAHC 'Home Sweet Texas.' And the key difference: it is *income-based*, not job-based. There's no 'are you a hero' checkbox. If your household income is under the limit, you qualify — nurse, barista, anybody.

**ON-SCREEN (full-frame card — "THE ONE THAT FITS YOU"):**
> **TSAHC — Home Sweet Texas**
> • Income-based — no job requirement
> • 3–5% of price toward down payment / closing
> • As a grant OR a 3-year forgivable second lien
> • 620 minimum credit score
> • Bell County cap: ~$153,180 HOUSEHOLD income (eff. 6/13/26)
> • Optional MCC = yearly federal tax credit on mortgage interest

> "What it gives you: three to five percent of the purchase price toward your down payment and closing costs. That comes either as a flat-out grant, or as a second lien that forgives itself over three years — you stay in the home three years, it's gone. Minimum credit score is six-twenty. And the income cap in Bell County is about a hundred fifty-three thousand a year for the household, effective this June. A single new-grad nurse clears that easily — you're well under it. If you're buying with a partner and both of you work, two incomes can push you over, so that's the one number you run with a lender before you count on it.

> There's also an optional piece called an MCC — a mortgage credit certificate. Plain English: a yearly federal tax credit on the mortgage interest you're already paying. Free money back at tax time if you set it up at closing.

> One more thing so you're not confused later: the state housing department, TDHCA, runs a nearly identical program. You don't have to pick — a good lender looks at both and points you to whichever one gives you the better deal."

**ON-SCREEN (lower third):** TDHCA runs a near-identical program — lender picks the better one

### BEAT 6 — HONEST TRADE-OFFS (~5:50–6:50)

**[TALKING HEAD — slower, direct eye contact. Tone shift to "real talk."]**
> "I'm not going to pretend this is free money with no strings. Three honest trade-offs, because you deserve the whole picture:

**ON-SCREEN (full-frame, numbered list builds one at a time):**
> **1. Rate bump.** Assistance often comes with a slightly higher interest rate.
> **2. Income cap.** Single — you clear it. Two incomes — run the household number with a lender first.
> **3. FHA MIP sticks.** Mortgage insurance stays on the loan; you refinance out of it later.

> "One: the assistance usually comes with a slightly higher interest rate than the absolute lowest you'd see otherwise. You're trading a little bit of rate for not having to come up with the cash. For most new nurses, that trade is worth it.

> Two: the income cap. As a single new grad, you're under it, no problem. But if you and a partner both work, double-check that household number with a lender before you bank on the program — two incomes can put you over.

> Three: FHA mortgage insurance — MIP — stays on the loan. It's part of what makes that low down payment possible, but it doesn't fall off automatically the way it can on other loans. The normal move is to refinance out of it down the road once you've got equity. None of these are dealbreakers. They're just the fine print I'd want you to know before you signed anything."
