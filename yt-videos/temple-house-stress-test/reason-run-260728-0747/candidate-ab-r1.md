# TEMPLE HOUSE STRESS TEST — Pilot + Series Architecture (Candidate AB, Round 1)

**Thesis.** The pilot is a retrospective consent build: Taylor re-walks a house his buyer client already owns, re-runs the exact stress test he performed on tour day, stamps every finding onto a running scorecard, and pays the video off with the deal's real documents — his tour-day calls graded on camera against the licensed inspection report and the written quotes in the file. The fact of the purchase is disclosed inside the first minute; the verdict is computed from the documents, never chosen in advance; what stays withheld is only what the viewer genuinely cannot know — the exposure total, the verdict zone the engine outputs, and what the negotiation actually extracted. This frame removes every lever the last takedown used: no active listing, no third-party target, an owner who signed a written release, and sourced numbers instead of planning ranges. The house was bought; the current owner is the client who benefited; **there is nobody left to be angry.** The one videographer day goes here — **GO**, conditional on a signed release and a broker-confirmed date — because both queued alternatives are self-shootable by the task's own description, and the pro day's non-recurring value is the series-lifetime asset bank.

**Claim discipline, stated once.** Where a claim is load-bearing, it carries a label: **(fact)** — from the task's ground truth or verifiable in the deal file; **(decision)** — a design choice Taylor can overrule; **(hypothesis)** — a bet with a named test. Any on-screen dollar figure without a source tag is a defect; the QA gate sweeps for exactly that.

## 1. Locked pilot frame + property sourcing rule

### 1.1 The frame analysis (the fatal-error surface)

**Active-listing build — rejected permanently.** An identifiable active listing, publicly analyzed for defects before the buyer's offer, is the takedown vector restated (fact: the last video was forced down over exactly this structure). It targets a third party's transaction, hands the co-op agent a grievance, and puts the broker relationship on the line at the exact moment the broker is doing Taylor a favor. It also has a game-theory defect independent of compliance: publishing the stress test of a house Taylor's buyer is about to offer on hands the seller's side the entire negotiation file for free. No wording fixes this — the target is the problem. Banned for the pilot and the series, including "positive verdict" versions: praise of an active listing is still public commentary on someone else's transaction.

**Live option-period build — rejected in its filmed form, for one controlling reason: the seller owns the house.** During a Texas option period the property still belongs to — and is usually still occupied and controlled by — the seller. A buyer's access exists for transaction purposes: tours, inspections, measuring. It is not a license to produce media inside someone else's home, the buyer cannot grant one, and closing later does not retroactively authorize footage shot while the seller owned the place. Three more structural failures: timing is uncontrollable against a one-day videographer commitment (option periods run days, closings run weeks, deals die — you cannot book the single non-recurring resource against a window a lender controls); masking cannot cure it (see 1.2 — masking barely works at all, and a co-op agent can recognize a house from its kitchen); and if the deal terminates, defect footage of a re-listed active listing exists in an edit bay — the takedown vector rebuilt from parts. So the bright line: **no media-production filming inside or over a property the client does not yet own. Ever. Pilot or series.** The live-deal energy this frame was reaching for is captured safely in 1.5.

**Retrospective consent build — selected.** The buyer client now holds title, which makes them the one party who can actually consent, and the resolved outcome means every number on screen comes from an inspection report, written quotes, an amendment, and a closing statement rather than estimates (fact: the task confirms such closed deals exist). The frame carries a built-in non-disparagement shield: the video's thesis is not "this house is bad" — it is "here is what a trained eye caught, what the documents priced it at, and how that set the final deal," told with the current owner's written permission.

What this frame is **not** is risk-free, and this document does not claim it is. The residual risks are named, mitigated, and accepted in 1.6 — that is what "cleared" means here: not "nobody could object," but "every foreseeable objector identified and structurally answered before publish."

### 1.2 The identifiability doctrine: consent is the shield, masking is a courtesy

A recently sold house has a public photographic history — listing photos persist on portals, caches, and screenshots, and anyone motivated can match a kitchen or a facade. This plan therefore does not promise anonymity, because that promise cannot be kept, and a broken promise to the owner is worse than no promise (decision).

1. **Consent carries the weight.** The release is written on the assumption the house may be recognized. The owner signs knowing that.
2. **Masking is a stated courtesy with disclosed limits.** No street address, no street signage, no house numbers, no geotag, no names unless elected — and the release says in plain English: "People who know the home, or who compare it to its old listing photos, may recognize it. We reduce casual discoverability; we cannot make a house anonymous."
3. **The courtesy is still real.** Avoid recreating the old listing's hero angle; frame tight; keep neighboring properties out of frame; aerial stills (if the 5.2 gate clears) cropped to the subject property.
4. **Recognition by the seller side is planned for, not wished away.** The people most likely to recognize the house are the former seller and the co-op agent. The counterparty hygiene rule (1.6, artifact 6) exists for exactly them: nothing in the script characterizes any person's intent or conduct, the findings shown are the buyer's own licensed inspection results, and the broker reviews the cut before publish. If they recognize the house, what they find is a factual, consented account of a closed transaction that is hostile to no one.

### 1.3 The staging problem: the verdict is computed, not cast

Frame B's outcome is known in advance, so the design must never pretend otherwise (inverse-fail #4). Two rules and one honesty layer:

**Rule 1 — disclose the retrospective early; withhold only what the viewer can't know.** At ~0:32–0:45, on camera, Taylor states that his buyer owns the house and consented (verbatim line, Section 6). Nobody can "discover" in the comments what the video announced in its first minute. The withheld payoffs are legitimate unknowns: the exposure total, the verdict zone it lands in, which finding moved the price, and what the negotiation produced in documents.

**Rule 2 — the verdict is whatever the file says.** The engine (Section 3) runs on the chosen deal's documents during prep, and the episode reports its output — BUY, NEGOTIATE, or WALK-that-became-a-cure, whatever the math yields. No verdict is selected before a property is selected. What IS selected is the kind of deal that makes a good episode — the sourcing gates screen for deals where findings demonstrably moved the price, which makes NEGOTIATE the expected output — and the video says that out loud: "I picked a deal where the scorecard did real work. That's a choice about what makes a useful episode, not a claim about the average Temple house." Selection is disclosed; outcomes are never manufactured (decision).

**The honesty layer — grade-my-calls.** Taylor grades his own tour-day estimates against the licensed inspector's report and the written quotes, item by item, **including at least one call he got wrong or the inspector cleared.** "I called this one at $1,500–$2,500. The quote came in at $2,240. I flagged the panel; the inspector cleared it — here's what I over-weighted." An analyst who audits himself on camera is the structural opposite of staged, and it is only possible in a frame where the answer key exists.

Tension architecture without dishonesty: rolling curiosity (what did he find), midpoint payoff (what does it total, what zone does it land in), final payoff (what actually happened, was he right).

### 1.4 Property sourcing rule (the decision rule Taylor runs this week)

**Screening universe:** buyer-side closings from the last 9 months (extendable to 12 by the anti-rule). Run each deal through the gates in order; the first deal to clear all six books the videographer; ties go to the tiebreak score.

- **Gate 0 — Deal shape.** Closed buyer-side transaction, Taylor as buyer's agent, closed ≤ 9 months ago so the property still resembles tour day and findings or their repairs are still filmable.
- **Gate 1 — Consent obtainable.** Every titled owner will sign the written release in 1.6. No release, no episode, regardless of story quality. Non-negotiable.
- **Gate 2 — Document trail at the FINDING level, not the file level.** For every finding that will carry a dollar figure on screen, the file must contain that finding's own source: its line in the licensed inspection report plus a written quote, invoice, amendment line, or closing-statement credit that prices it. A file-level "there's an amendment somewhere" does not qualify a finding — one amendment cannot source six cost bands. Two release valves keep this honest without inventing numbers: **(a) the quote-refresh pass** — during prep, Taylor obtains current written contractor quotes for any inspection-confirmed finding that never got priced during the deal, shown on screen tagged `[current quote — MM/YYYY]`; **(b) the severity-only lane** — a finding with no document is shown with severity and inspector language and **no dollar figure**, with the on-camera line: **"No document, no number — I don't put dollars on screen I can't source."** That line is a feature, not a concession.
- **Gate 3 — Findings density, inspector-confirmed.** ≥ 4 findings spanning ≥ 3 of the five scorecard categories, each featured finding appearing in **both** Taylor's tour-day notes **and** the licensed inspection report, with ≥ 1 at Severity 2+ carrying a documented cost. Restricting featured stress points to inspector-confirmed items is what makes the Section 6 compliance line literally true by construction; tour-day flags the inspector cleared are not deleted — they move to the grade-my-calls beat, labeled as exactly that.
- **Gate 4 — Negotiation delta.** Findings measurably moved the deal: price reduction + credits + seller-completed repairs at invoice value ≥ 1.0% of purchase price. The 1.0% floor is a story-quality heuristic, not a market fact (decision): below it, the payoff the hook promises can't be delivered honestly.
- **Gate 5 — No landmines.** Transaction fully closed and funded; no ongoing or threatened dispute; no builder-defect framing required — prefer resale ≥ 5 years old so the no-firsthand-builder-defect-claims rule is never even approached (fact: standing rule); owners comfortable with the general area being named; nothing in the file that would need fair-housing-adjacent territory (schools, safety, who lives nearby) to explain. The house, never the neighborhood's people.

**Tiebreak score (only if ≥ 2 clear):** +2 findings still visually filmable today; +2 seller-completed repairs visible (before/after from re-shot footage — see the photo rule); +1 multiple distinct exterior zones (protects the pattern-interrupt cadence); +1 owner open to an optional ≤ 20-second cameo (never required, never pressured); +1 within 25 minutes of Temple (protects the pro-day schedule). Highest wins; tie to most recent closing.

**Tour-day photo rule (conservative by design).** Photos Taylor took during showings were taken while the seller owned the home, for transaction purposes. Default: do not publish them. Re-shoot everything post-close under the release. Exception path only for non-identifying macro close-ups (a crack fills the frame, nothing else recognizable), and only after the broker-review pass in 1.6 signs off (decision — a prudence line, not a claimed legal ruling).

**Anti-rule:** if nothing clears in two weeks, never relax Gates 1 or 2. Relax Gate 0 to 12 months first, then Gate 3 to 3 findings. Consent and per-finding documents are the two things this format cannot function without.

### 1.6 The consent chain (six artifacts, all stored in the deal folder)

1. **The ask** — call or in-person, confirmed by email, promising exactly what the release delivers, no more: "I'm starting a series that shows buyers what I check before they offer on a house. I'd like to feature yours — the flags I caught on our tour and how they shaped your deal. Here's the honest structure: you approve everything that identifies you — names, faces, anything personal in frame, and how much of the price story gets told. The analysis itself — what I flagged and what it cost — stays mine. You see the full cut before it publishes, and up until it publishes you can pull the plug entirely, no questions. Can I send you the one-page release that says all of that in writing?"
2. **The written release**, mirroring the ask word-for-honest-word: scope (interior/exterior filming; aerial still photos contingent on the 5.2 gate; use on YouTube, templetxhomes.net, and social); **identifiability acknowledgment** in plain language per 1.2 — no anonymity is promised; **identity-element approvals** — name usage (full / first / none), on-camera cameo (yes/no), personal-belongings review at preview; **price-disclosure election** — Texas is a non-disclosure state (fact), so the sale price is not public record and its disclosure level is the owner's choice: exact figures / deltas only ("$7,500 off the ask") / percentages / none; **document clause** — transaction documents shown only in redacted form; **revocation** — full withdrawal right through the moment of publication; **preview commitment** — owner sees the final cut and provides **written approval before upload** (affirmative sign-off, never silence-as-consent); **post-publish concerns** handled in good faith (blurs, trims), takedown at Taylor's discretion — stated honestly rather than implied; **editorial line** — approval rights cover identity and privacy elements plus the whole-video withdrawal right; they do not extend to rewriting the analysis.
3. **Redaction pass** on every document that appears: names, addresses, loan and account numbers, signatures, MLS numbers.
4. **Inspector courtesy clearance** (decision — prudence, not a claimed legal requirement): before report pages appear on screen, Taylor asks the inspector's OK and offers an on-screen credit ("inspection by a licensed Texas inspector"). If declined, the report is represented by Taylor's own scorecard summary card and the findings are described, not photographed. Either path keeps the numbers; one of them also builds a referral relationship.
5. **Owner preview + written approval** — email record: "Approved for publish," plus resolution of any identity/belongings notes. Preview happens early in the edit, not the night before.
6. **Broker pre-review + counterparty hygiene.** The sponsoring broker sees the final cut before publish. This folds the videographer favor into the compliance chain: it satisfies advertising-review prudence (Section 6), and the brokerage is never surprised by its own agent's video. Paired with the script-level hygiene rule: **no characterization of the seller, the co-op agent, the builder, or any other agent's practices — ever.** No "they tried to hide it," no "the listing should have said," no "what other agents don't do." Documents speak; people are not characterized; competitors are not referenced even by implication. Takedown hygiene and NAR Article 15 hygiene in one rule — nothing is owed to the seller side legally; this is about leaving no one with a story to tell a broker.

## 3. The verdict engine

Mirrors Taylor's published inspection-analysis standard verbatim — **severity + likely cost range + stop-the-deal flag** (fact) — and stays entirely in the buyer lane: cash exposure and financing risk for an owner-occupant. No cap rates, no rental math, no return language anywhere in the series.

### 3.1 Scorecard categories (five — direct mapping from Taylor's real showing toolkit)

| # | Category | Contents (from the toolkit) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Foundation & Structure** | Foundation cracks at masonry base, stair-step brick cracking, slab cracks, roof sag read as a structural signal |
| 2 | **Roof & Envelope** | Patch scan, shingle condition, soffit/fascia/gutter rot, flashing |
| 3 | **Water & Drainage** | Grading toward slab, downspout discharge, splash erosion, staining at the slab line |
| 4 | **Big-Ticket Systems** | Water-heater age plate, HVAC age plate, electrical panel condition |
| 5 | **Loan-Killer Flags (VA/FHA MPR)** | Peeling paint pre-1978, missing handrails, broken glazing, inoperable systems — items that threaten Minimum Property Requirements and therefore the financing itself. In a heavy-VA corridor this category earns its own lane |
| | | |

### 3.2 Severity scale (per finding)

- **S1 — Note & monitor.** Age-appropriate wear. Logged, no ask generated. Showing S1s is deliberate: an analyst who logs non-issues as non-issues is believed when he logs an S3.
- **S2 — Budget item.** Real, priceable, negotiable. Requires a per-finding documented cost (Gate 2).
- **S3 — Stop-and-verify.** Taylor's stop-the-deal flag: no offer math is valid until a licensed inspector — and where indicated, a structural engineer or licensed trade — scopes it. S3 is not "walk"; it is "verify before anything else is true."

### 3.3 The per-finding pipeline (severity → sourced cost → leverage)

1. **Severity assigned on camera** (S1/S2/S3) with the one-line reason.
2. **Cost only with a document.** Source tag in the lower-third: `[inspection report]`, `[option-period quote]`, `[current quote — MM/YYYY]`, `[invoice]`, `[closing statement]`. Findings without a document run the severity-only lane with the no-document-no-number line. Planning ranges may exist in the edit as `[PLANNING — REPLACE]`; the video does not publish until zero of those tags survive. Total Repair Exposure (TRE) = sum of documented figures; where a source gives a range, its midpoint is used, and that method is stated once on screen at THE SCORECARD.
3. **Leverage class per S2/S3** — R / C / P: **R**epair by seller pre-close (verified by re-inspection), **C**redit at close (lender caps checked), **P**rice reduction. Rules stated in the video: *documents are leverage, feelings are not* — only findings with paper attached go in the amendment; **one ask per finding, bundled once** — a single amendment, not a drip of demands. S1s never generate an ask.

### 3.4 Verdict thresholds — a disclosed rubric, not a law of nature

On camera, the thresholds are introduced exactly this way: **"These are my lines — the same lines every episode, so the verdicts mean something across houses. They're an agent's framework, not an inspection standard and not a rule about what you can afford."** (decision, labeled as such on screen)

| Verdict | Rule |
|---|---|
| **BUY** | TRE **< 1.5%** of purchase price AND zero S3 flags. Proceed on standard terms; a small credit ask is optional. |
| **NEGOTIATE** | TRE **1.5%–5.0%**, OR any single documented finding **≥ 1.0%** alone, OR an S3 that is scoped, quoted, and curable before closing. The engine produces a documented ask sized to the exposure. |
| **WALK** | TRE **> 5.0%**, OR an S3 that cannot be cured or verified before closing (active structural movement without an engineer letter, unscoped moisture intrusion), OR an MPR item the seller refuses to cure on a VA/FHA deal — the loan cannot close, so the deal cannot — OR access denied for verification. |

**Where the lines come from (stated honestly):** arithmetic anchors plus Taylor's judgment, said as his. On a ~$300K Temple-area purchase, 1.5% ≈ $4,500 and 5% ≈ $15,000 (arithmetic). The judgment call — his, on camera — is that below the first line, grinding a negotiation over reserve-sized items risks the house for little gain, and above the second, post-close exposure stacked on top of down payment and closing costs is where he starts telling his own buyers to reconsider. No claim is made about what "most buyers" hold in reserve or consider normal — those claims aren't sourced, so they don't appear. **Calibration pass (prep week):** run three additional past buyer-side files through the engine; if its verdict contradicts what actually happened in two or more, adjust the lines before filming and say so on camera. A rubric that survived a back-test is presented as exactly that — back-tested, not proven (hypothesis, named test).

**The client-decision rule (script-level, non-negotiable):** the engine never decides for the buyer. At THE VERDICT: **"The scorecard hands my buyer a number and a recommendation. The decision is theirs — my job is that they make it with the full picture."** In the retrospective, both are shown: what the engine output, and what the buyer actually chose. If they differ, that is the story, not a problem. **Overrides, one line each on screen:** an S3 can force WALK at any TRE; a seller cure, re-verified, can move WALK back to NEGOTIATE; the thresholds guide, the flags govern, the client decides.

### 3.5 On-screen artifacts + lead magnet mirror

- **Per-finding stamp (lower-third, ~4s):** `STRESS POINT #3 · WATER & DRAINAGE · S2 · $1,150–$1,600 [option-period quote]`
- **Corner counter:** `EXPOSURE: $6,450` — ticks on each stamp.
- **Tally card (THE SCORECARD):** five categories × (flags / max severity / documented exposure), TRE line, % of price, verdict-zone bar.
- **Lead magnet:** "The Temple House Stress Test Scorecard" — one-page PDF on templetxhomes.net mirroring the on-screen card exactly: five categories, S1/S2/S3 boxes, cost column with a source line, leverage class column, TRE line, the three threshold bands printed **with the same "my lines, not a rule" caveat**, and a footer carrying the not-an-inspector language plus the IABS and Consumer Protection Notice links (Section 6). The viewer downloads the instrument they just watched work — that congruence is the conversion mechanism, and the caveat travels with it.

## 4. Packaging

### 4.0 Identity timing, reconciled once

Two rules touch the credential: the channel's entity-declaration convention (first three sentences) and the video canon (no self-intro before the hook; credential ~0:30, never inside 0:15 — fact, mandatory). Both are satisfied the same way: **every hook is built as exactly two spoken sentences (≤ ~16–18s), making the credential line the third spoken sentence, landing ~0:25–0:30** — after 0:15, at effectively 0:30, and inside the first three sentences. The description opens with the entity declaration in sentence one. This reconciliation is a QA line item, not an accident.

All bracketed values are slots filled from the chosen deal file before publish; every hook carries verification notes, and no video ships with an unverified slot or claim.

### 4.1 Hook variants (each = 2 spoken sentences; formula: [number/contradiction] + [who it's for] + [delayed payoff resolving at the 50–60% mark])

**H1 — Number-led:**
> "[$11,900] — that's what was wrong with this house when my buyer toured it, and [none] of it showed in the photos online. If you're buying in Temple, you're about to see all [six] flags in the order I caught them, and the scorecard that turns them into one decision: buy, negotiate, or walk."

*Formula map: number = TRE; who = Temple buyers; delayed payoff = the scorecard decision → resolves at THE SCORECARD (52–60%). Verification notes: TRE from the tally; "[none] showed in the photos" is used only after checking the actual listing photos against the finding list — if any finding is visible, the line becomes "almost none of it" or the clause drops.*

**H2 — Contradiction-led:**
> "This house appraised and closed on schedule — and it needed [$11,900] of work the day we toured it, and both of those things are true. If you're house hunting in Temple, I'll show you what a [forty]-minute walk actually catches, and the scorecard that told my buyer whether to buy, negotiate, or walk."

*Formula map: contradiction = appraised-and-closed vs. documented repair exposure — file-verifiable, and it teaches the appraisal-is-not-an-inspection lesson; who = Temple house hunters; delayed payoff = the scorecard → 52–60%. Verification notes: use only on a financed deal with an appraisal in file; on a cash deal, the first clause becomes "funded and closed on schedule." No claims about other buyers, other agents, or what anyone else failed to see.*

**H3 — Time-asymmetry, disclosure folded in (strongest anti-staging choice):**
> "It took me about [forty] minutes to find [$11,900] in this house — and because my buyer owns it now, for once I can show you all of it. If you're buying in Temple: every flag from tour day, the real cost behind each one from the actual deal file, and at the end, the call — buy, negotiate, or walk."

*Formula map: numbers = minutes-to-dollars asymmetry, both Taylor's own verifiable facts; who = Temple buyers; delayed payoff = the call → verdict zone at 52–60%, outcome documents at THE RECEIPTS. Verification notes: tour length from Taylor's calendar/notes; "all of it" is licensed by the release — if the owner's price-disclosure election restricts it, the line becomes "almost all of it."*

Banned-language check on all three against the ground-truth list: clean. No dollar-volume claims. No "nobody shows you"-class claims about other agents or buyers. "Agent" only.

## 5. Videographer shot architecture (the one pro day)

### 5.1 Per-finding shot recipe (the repeatable unit — solo-first by design)

Every stress point — pilot and future solo episodes alike — is the same seven shots. Pro day: shots 1–2 on A-cam (videographer, gimbal/handheld) while B-cam covers 3–4 simultaneously. Solo: Taylor shoots 1–2 and 5 on tripod and picks up 3–4 and 6–7 handheld. Same structure, different labor — the format's look lives in the recipe, not the crew.

1. **Approach POV** — moving toward the zone, 5–8s (gimbal pro / chest-height handheld solo).
2. **Context wide** — Taylor at the finding, spot line delivered ("Most people are looking at the kitchen; I'm looking at this."), 7–12s.
3. **Evidence macro** — the crack, the plate, the rot; rack focus; 7–12s.
4. **Hands-on demonstrative** — tape across the crack, level app on the grade, flashlight into the panel; 7–12s.
5. **Severity MCU** — direct to camera: severity call + sourced cost + leverage line; 7–12s.
6. **Stamp plate** — locked-off 4s hold for the lower-third stamp to land clean.
7. **Stills + vertical pair** — one still per finding (lead magnet, blog, GBP) and one native-vertical pickup for Shorts.

**Solo pre-validation (prep week, before the pro day):** Taylor shoots ONE full finding block at his own house with the tripod recipe. One hour of work converts "Episode 2 will look continuous" from an assertion into a checked box — if the solo recipe fails, it gets fixed while the fix is still free (hypothesis, tested before the non-recurring resource is spent).

### 5.2 Preconditions and the aerial gate

- **No release, no shoot.** Signed release verified before the videographer is booked; re-verified on arrival — no release, cameras stay in the truck.
- **Aerial legal gate (decision — compliance prudence):** aerial assets happen only if, before the day: the operator's FAA Part 107 certificate is current, the aircraft registration is current, and airspace for the address is checked and (if controlled) LAANC-authorized. Plan of record: **Taylor-flown stills only**, per his gear rule (fact: aerial photos only until ND filters arrive in August). If the videographer brings their own ND-equipped drone AND clears the same legal gate, short aerial clips are a bonus — never a dependency. If any element is unverified, aerial assets are cut; nothing in the format needs them.

Horton review** | No — filming next weekend, self-shootable (fact) | Same logic, plus a pairing problem: pointing a brokerage-provided videographer at builder-review content — the genre closest to the takedown history and the builder-claims guardrail — is the single worst use of that specific favor |
| **Killeen-vs-Temple** | Blocked on data (fact) | A blocked project cannot schedule a shoot |
| **Stress Test pilot** | Yes — uniquely | Three things only the pro day can buy: **(1)** two-camera, full-recipe coverage of a consented occupied home Taylor cannot casually revisit — one clean day beats four return favors from a client; **(2)** the series-lifetime asset bank — brand kit, thumbnail plate library, desk replication kit — amortized across every future self-shot episode, making them look continuous with the pilot; **(3)** the highest-ceiling pairing available: the channel's strongest measured retention beat type (fact: 0.83) built into a format that terminates in the buyer-rep CTA and a lead magnet that is literally the on-screen instrument |

**The business rationale, labels on:** impressions velocity ~29/day was the binding constraint on the last pillar (fact). The inference — that the channel's near-term lever is making each impression and each viewer-minute convert harder, not publishing more volume — is a strategy judgment (decision), and this format is its direct expression: proven-beat retention architecture, a verdict engine worth subscribing for, and a CTA that converts attention into representation. The measurement plan (2.4) turns that judgment into evidence by Episode 3.

**Continuity check (the series dies if the pilot sets a bar solo production can't clear):** handled structurally, then tested. The finding recipe is tripod-first; the polish layers (intro sting, stamps, desk look) are banked assets, not per-episode production; and the 5.1 solo pre-validation shoots one full block solo before the pro day is even spent. Episode 2 self-shot will look like the same show — a design goal with a test attached, not a promise.

**Sequencing (the three projects compete only for the videographer, and only one needs him):**
