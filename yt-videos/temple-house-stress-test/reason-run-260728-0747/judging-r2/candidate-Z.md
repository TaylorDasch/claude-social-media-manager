# TEMPLE HOUSE STRESS TEST — Pilot + Series Architecture 

**Thesis.** The pilot is a retrospective consent build: Taylor re-walks a house his buyer client now owns, re-runs the stress test he performed on tour day, stamps every finding onto a running scorecard, and pays the video off in two stages — the verdict zone at the midpoint, the real negotiation documents at the end. The frame removes the levers the last takedown used: no active listing, no third party's transaction as the target, a titled owner with a signed written release, and sourced numbers instead of estimates (fact: such closed deals exist per the task). What makes this candidate different in kind is a single governing instrument, the **Broadcast Ledger** (1.3): an explicit accounting of every number the format necessarily makes public and everything derivable from those numbers plus public listing archives. Both of the format's promises — the privacy promise to the owner and the withheld-payoff promise to the viewer — are *derived from* that ledger instead of asserted alongside it, so neither promise can exceed what the on-screen arithmetic actually keeps. The one videographer day goes here — **GO**, conditional on a signed release, a passed scout visit, and a broker-confirmed date (Section 7) — because the two shoot-ready alternatives are self-shootable by the task's own description, the third is blocked on data, and the pro day's non-recurring value is the series-lifetime asset bank.

**Claim labels, stated once.** **(fact)** = task ground truth or verifiable in the deal file. **(external fact — verify)** = a true-in-the-world premise (Texas practice, FAA rules) confirmed once during prep week, never treated as settled by this document alone. **(decision)** = a design choice Taylor can overrule. **(heuristic)** = a rule of thumb doing story work, not a market claim. **(hypothesis)** = a bet with a named test. Any on-screen dollar without a source tag is a defect; the QA gate sweeps for it.

## 1. Deliverable 1 — Locked pilot frame + property sourcing rule

### 1.1 Frame analysis (the fatal-error surface)

**Active-listing build — permanently rejected.** Publicly analyzing an identifiable active listing for defects is the takedown vector restated (fact: the last video was forced down over exactly this structure). It targets a third party's transaction, hands the co-op agent a grievance, and burns the broker relationship at the moment the broker is extending a favor. It also fails on pure game theory: publishing the stress test of a house Taylor's buyer is about to offer on gives the seller's side the buyer's entire negotiation file. No script wording cures a structural problem — the target is the problem. Banned for the series too, including praise episodes: complimenting an active listing is still public commentary on someone else's transaction.

**Live option-period build — rejected in filmed form for one controlling reason: the seller still owns the house.** During a Texas option period the property belongs to, and is usually occupied by, the seller; a buyer's access exists for transaction purposes — touring, inspecting, measuring — not media production, and the buyer cannot grant a license they do not hold (external fact — verify the access-scope framing with the broker once; the design does not depend on the verification going either way, because the rule below moots it). Three further failures: the timing is uncontrollable against a one-day videographer commitment (option periods run days, closings run weeks, deals die); masking cannot cure identifiability (1.2); and a terminated deal leaves defect footage of a soon-to-be-relisted active listing sitting in an edit bay — the takedown vector rebuilt from parts. Bright line for the pilot and the series: **no media-production filming inside or over any property the client does not yet own.** The live-deal energy this frame wanted is captured safely by the tracked-live variant in 1.6.

**Retrospective consent build — selected.** The buyer client holds title, making them the one party who can actually consent; the resolved outcome means every number on screen traces to an inspection report, written quotes, an amendment, or a closing statement (fact: the task confirms closed buyer-side deals exist as candidates). The thesis of the video is not "this house is bad" — it is "here is what a trained eye caught, what the documents priced it at, and how that set the final deal," told with the current owner's written permission. This is not risk-free and the document never claims it is: "cleared" here means every objector the register enumerates is structurally answered before publish, with the enumeration itself re-reviewed each episode (1.7) — not that no one on earth could object.

### 1.2 Identifiability doctrine: consent carries the weight; masking is a courtesy

A recently sold house has a public photographic history — listing photos persist on portals, caches, and screenshots, and a motivated viewer can match a kitchen or a facade (external fact — verify the specific listing's archive during prep, because the Broadcast Ledger uses it). Therefore:

1. **No anonymity is promised to anyone**, because that promise cannot be kept, and a broken promise to the owner is worse than no promise (decision).
2. **The release is written assuming recognition.** Plain-language clause: "People who know this home, or who compare it to its old listing photos, may recognize it. We reduce casual discoverability; we cannot make a house anonymous."
3. **The courtesy is still real:** no street address, no signage, no house numbers, no geotag, no names unless elected; avoid recreating the old listing's hero angle; keep neighbors out of frame; crop aerial stills to the subject property.
4. **Recognition by the seller side is planned for, not wished away.** The former seller and the co-op agent are the likeliest recognizers. The counterparty hygiene rule (1.7, artifact 6) exists for exactly them: no characterization of any person's intent or conduct, findings presented as the buyer's own licensed-inspection results, broker review before publish. If they recognize the house, they find a factual, consented account of a closed transaction designed to give no one a grievance to carry to a broker — a design claim about the content, not a prediction about anyone's feelings; the residual is carried in the register.

### 1.3 The Broadcast Ledger — what the format necessarily makes public, and the two promises derived from it

Every episode of this format, by its own design, publishes:

| # | Broadcast item | Where |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | Total Repair Exposure (TRE) in dollars | Thumbnail badge, hooks, corner counter, tally card |
| B2 | Per-finding severities and per-finding documented costs | Stamps, grade-my-calls |
| B3 | TRE as a percentage of the purchase price | THE SCORECARD (the verdict engine's thresholds are % of price, so the % is not optional) |
| B4 | The verdict zone and the negotiation outcome | THE SCORECARD, THE VERDICT |
| B5 | Public context the video does not control | The old listing's archived ask price and photos, on portals and caches |

**Derivations the ledger forces us to honor:** B1 ÷ B3 reconstructs the purchase price to the precision of the displayed percentage. Any price-reduction figure plus B5's archived ask reconstructs the sale price exactly. Per-component negotiation figures plus an exact concessions total reconstruct each other by subtraction. No consent instrument and no tension model may promise anything these derivations break. Two promises follow.

**Promise 1 — to the owner (the price-disclosure election, two tiers, both keepable):**

Texas is a non-disclosure state, so the sale price is not public record (external fact — verify once; if wrong, the election still works, it just protects less). The archived *ask* price is public regardless. The release therefore offers exactly two tiers and says, verbatim: *"There is no private version of this video's math. The old listing's asking price is already public, and the video's own numbers point at what you paid. Your real choice is between the exact figure and a rounded one — and 'no' to the whole episode is always available."*

- **Tier E — Exact.** Purchase price shown exactly; all on-screen math exact; percentages may carry one decimal.
- **Tier B — Rounded Band (Taylor's recommended default — decision).** The purchase price appears only as a rounded anchor ("about $300K," rounded to the nearest $25,000). The on-screen percentage is computed against that anchor, displayed as a whole percent with an approximation mark ("≈4% of about $300K") — so the number a viewer reconstructs by division *is the anchor already disclosed*, and the whole-percent display makes division coarser than the band itself. TRE, per-finding repair costs, quotes, invoices, and closing credits stay exact — none of them derive the price. The only figures that do derive it — the price itself and any price-reduction-off-ask — are banded: the reduction is shown rounded ("roughly $7K off the ask") at whatever granularity keeps archived-ask-minus-shown-reduction inside the disclosed anchor's band, and any concessions total containing the reduction carries the ≈ mark. **Zone-preservation rule:** the true verdict math runs privately on exact numbers; the displayed rounded figures must read in the same verdict zone as the true figures — if rounding cannot preserve the zone (a true 4.9% that displays as "≈5%" against a 5.0% line), Tier B is unavailable for that property and the owner chooses Tier E or the property fails the screen (Gate 6).
- **QA enforcement:** a dedicated **reconstruction audit** (QA row 13) combines every on-screen number with the public archives and confirms nothing derivable is tighter than the elected disclosure.

The abolished tiers are named so they stay dead: "deltas only" was price disclosure wearing a costume (delta + archived ask = price), and "no price story" is incompatible with a format whose verdict is a percentage of the price. Neither is ever offered.

**Promise 2 — to the viewer (the tension model: the packaging spends the total; the video withholds the conversion):**

The thumbnail badge and every hook state TRE up front — that is the packaging doing its job, and the design owns it rather than pretending otherwise. What the viewer genuinely cannot know, and what the video withholds:

1. **Composition** — which findings build the number, in what order, and which single finding is the biggest (the "biggest one is still coming" open loop is the engine of the first half).
2. **The conversion** — what $11,900 *means*: the thresholds are unknown to the viewer, so whether the number is trivial, negotiable, or a walk-away is a live question until the zone lands at ~6:57. This is the delayed payoff every hook promises ("the scorecard that turns it into one call"), resolving inside the mandated 50–60% window (fact: window mandatory).
3. **The consequence** — what the negotiation actually extracted, in documents, and whether the buyer followed the engine (resolved at THE RECEIPTS / THE VERDICT).

The corner counter is therefore **not a mystery device — it is a promise-keeping device**: it visibly accounts its way toward the number the thumbnail advertised, and the design bets that a known-destination progress meter holds attention through composition suspense (hypothesis — tested by the pilot's retention curve, 2.4). The SCORECARD beat's spoken frame makes the model explicit on camera: *"You've had the number since you clicked. What you don't have is what it means."* No script line anywhere presents the total as news after the hook (QA row 4 sweeps for exactly that).

### 1.4 The staging problem: the verdict is computed, not cast

The outcome is known to Taylor in advance, so the design must never pretend otherwise (inverse-fail #4).

**Rule 1 — disclose the retrospective early.** At ~0:32, on camera, Taylor states that his buyer owns the house and consented (verbatim line, Section 6). Nobody can "discover" in the comments what the video announced in its first minute.

**Rule 2 — the verdict is whatever the file says.** The engine (Section 3) runs on the chosen deal's documents during prep, and the episode reports its output. No verdict is selected before a property is selected. What *is* selected is the kind of deal that makes a useful episode — the gates screen for deals where findings measurably moved the price, which makes NEGOTIATE the expected output — and the video says so out loud: *"I picked a deal where the scorecard did real work. That's a choice about what makes a useful episode, not a claim about the average Temple house."* Selection is disclosed; outcomes are never manufactured (decision).

**The honesty layer — grade-my-calls.** Taylor grades his tour-day estimates against the licensed inspector's report and the written quotes, item by item, **including at least one call he got wrong or the inspector cleared**: "I called this at $1,500–$2,500; the quote came in at $2,240. I flagged the panel; the inspector cleared it — here's what I over-weighted." A self-audit on camera is the structural opposite of staged, and it is only possible in a frame where the answer key exists.

### 1.5 Property sourcing rule (the decision rule Taylor runs this week)

**Screening universe:** buyer-side closings from the last 9 months (extendable to 12 by the anti-rule). Run each deal through the gates in order; the first deal to clear all seven books the scout, and a passed scout books the videographer. Ties go to the tiebreak score.

- **Gate 0 — Deal shape.** Closed buyer-side transaction, Taylor as buyer's agent, closed ≤ 9 months ago so the property still resembles tour day.
- **Gate 1 — Informed consent obtainable.** Every titled owner will sign the 1.7 release *after* hearing the full-cost ask script — including the no-price-secrecy line and the resale-impact disclosure. No release, no episode, regardless of story quality. Non-negotiable.
- **Gate 2 — Documents at the FINDING level, not the file level.** Every finding that will carry a dollar on screen needs its own source: its line in the licensed inspection report plus a written quote, invoice, amendment line, or closing-statement credit that prices it. One amendment cannot source six cost bands. Deal-era documents are preferred; two release valves keep this honest without inventing numbers: **(a) the quote-refresh pass** — a current written contractor quote for any inspection-confirmed finding that never got priced during the deal, shown tagged `[current quote — MM/YYYY]` and subject to the vintage rule (3.3); **(b) the severity-only lane** — an undocumented finding shows severity and inspector language with **no dollar figure**, carried by the on-camera line: **"No document, no number — I don't put dollars on screen I can't source."** That line is a feature, not a concession.
- **Gate 3 — Findings density, inspector-confirmed.** ≥ 4 findings spanning ≥ 3 of the five scorecard categories, each featured finding present in **both** Taylor's tour-day notes **and** the licensed inspection report, with ≥ 1 at Severity 2+ carrying a documented cost. Restricting featured findings to inspector-confirmed items makes the Section 6 compliance line literally true by construction. Tour-day flags the inspector cleared are not deleted — they move to grade-my-calls, labeled as exactly that.
- **Gate 4 — Negotiation delta.** Findings measurably moved the deal: price reduction + credits + seller-completed repairs at invoice value ≥ 1.0% of purchase price (heuristic — a story-quality floor, not a market fact; below it the hook's payoff can't be delivered honestly). At ~$300K that floor is ~$3,000 (arithmetic).
- **Gate 5 — No landmines.** Fully closed and funded; no ongoing or threatened dispute; no builder-defect framing required — prefer resale ≥ 5 years old so the no-firsthand-builder-defect-claims rule (fact: standing rule) is never approached; owners comfortable with the general area being named; nothing in the file that needs fair-housing-adjacent territory (schools, safety, who lives nearby) to explain. The house, never the neighborhood's people.
- **Gate 6 — Visual evidence, scout-verified.** Before the videographer is booked, Taylor runs a 30–45 minute scout at the property and produces a finding-by-finding filmability map. To pass: **≥ 3 findings filmable today** — unrepaired items of any severity count (S1s are filmable and demonstrate calibration), and completed repairs count when the current state visibly tells the story (the new water heater, the re-shingled section) *and* a cleared archival "before" exists per the photo rule; **and every featured S2+ finding** has either current visual evidence or an archival path (cleared tour-day macro, licensed inspector photo per 1.7 artifact 4, or document imagery). The zone-preservation check for Tier B (1.3) runs here too. **No scout pass, no booking** — the one non-recurring resource is never spent on an unverified shot list.

**Tiebreak (only if ≥ 2 clear all seven):** +2 per filmable finding beyond the Gate 6 minimum (cap +4); +1 all featured figures deal-era (no refresh quotes needed — cleanest claims); +1 multiple distinct exterior zones (protects the interrupt cadence); +1 owner open to an optional ≤ 20-second cameo (never required, never pressured); +1 within 25 minutes of Temple (protects the pro-day schedule). Highest wins; tie to most recent closing.

**Tour-day photo rule (conservative by design).** Photos taken during showings were shot while the seller owned the home, for transaction purposes. Default: do not publish them. Exception path, now load-bearing for repaired findings: non-identifying macro close-ups only (the crack fills the frame, nothing else recognizable), each individually approved in the broker pre-review with the approval logged in the folder, and labeled on screen "tour-day photo" (decision — a prudence line, not a claimed legal ruling).

**Anti-rule:** if nothing clears in two weeks, never relax Gates 1, 2, or 6 — informed consent, per-finding documents, and verified filmability are the three things the format cannot function without. Relax Gate 0 to 12 months first, then Gate 3 to 3 findings. If no candidate passes Gate 6, the search widens in time, never in evidence.

### 1.6 Series Law + episode types

**Series Law (binding on every episode type, permanently).** Because masking cannot be promised against listing-photo archives (1.2), **no episode of any format ever shows any property — footage, stills, floor plans, listing images, aerials, or blurred/cropped derivatives — unless its current titled owner has signed the 1.7 release.** There is no "partially masked" tier for third-party-owned homes: release-cleared and shown, or not cleared and never shown. **Companion rule — footage provenance:** identifying footage of a consented property is *episode-bound* — it appears only in that property's own episode; the reusable series brand kit is built exclusively from non-identifying material (5.4), so no owner's house ever wallpapers a story about a different house.

| Episode type | Rule | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard** (default) | Retrospective consent build, full seven-gate screen, every time | The pilot's frame is the series' frame |
| **"Tracked live, filmed after"** (allowed from Episode 3+) | During a real active purchase: **zero media production at the property.** Taylor records dated desk-log entries at his own office — scorecard calls, severities, predicted numbers — with the property de-identified in the logs themselves: no address, street, subdivision, listing photos, listing-copy quotes, or MLS number. Nothing publishes while the deal is live. After closing, with the new owner's release signed, the standard episode is filmed — now with time-stamped receipts proving the calls were made in real time | Captures live-deal authenticity with none of the consent problem. If the deal dies: nothing identifiable ever existed; the logs archive; the episode dies with it — there is no masking tier to fall back to, by design |
| **Active-listing** | Never | See 1.1 — including praise versions |
| **WALK** | Desk case-file format with **zero property imagery from any source** — no footage, no photos (Taylor's, the listing's, or the brand bank's), no floor plans, no derivatives — plus descriptor de-identification: no address, street, subdivision, MLS number, or listing-copy quotes; price only as a rounded band; documents redacted past recognition. Built from scorecard graphics, the non-identifying brand kit, and the buyer's written consent to discuss their own decision | A walked house belongs to someone else and is likely re-listed. Masking cannot be promised, so it is not attempted: the only tellable WALK is one where nothing identifying is ever shown or said |

### 1.7 The consent chain (six artifacts, all stored in the deal folder)

**Artifact 1 — the ask** (call or in person, confirmed by email), promising exactly what the release delivers and disclosing the full cost of saying yes:

> "I'm starting a series that shows buyers what I check before they offer on a house, and I'd like to feature yours — the flags I caught on our tour and how they shaped your deal. Full honesty about what that means. The video is permanent and public. The repair story uses real dollar figures, and the math points at what you paid — we can round the price story, but we can't hide it. Someday when you sell, a buyer's agent could find this video; it will also show that everything big was inspected, fixed, or paid for, with receipts — that cuts both ways and you should weigh it. You approve everything that identifies you, you see the full cut before it publishes, you can pull the plug any time before it goes up, and if you ever list the house you can ask me to unlist the episode while it's on the market. The analysis itself — what I flagged and what it cost — stays mine. Want me to send the one-page release that puts all of that in writing?"

**Artifact 2 — the written release**, mirroring the ask word for word in substance: scope (interior/exterior filming; aerial stills contingent on the 5.2 gate; use on YouTube, templetxhomes.net, and social); **identifiability acknowledgment** (1.2 — no anonymity promised); **no-price-secrecy acknowledgment and two-tier election** (1.3 — Exact or Rounded Band, with the verbatim no-private-math sentence); **resale-impact acknowledgment** — plain language that the video is a permanent, searchable record of the home's inspected-and-resolved defect history, findable by future buyers' agents, appraisers, and insurers, that Texas seller's-disclosure obligations exist independently of the video (external fact — verify phrasing with the broker), and that the episode documents cures with receipts as well as defects; **the for-sale pause clause** — on the owner's written request while the home is actively listed, Taylor unlists the episode within 7 days for the duration of the listing (a keepable promise: unlisting is entirely within his control; the long-tail cost is accepted — decision); **identity elements** — name usage (full / first / none), cameo (yes/no), personal-belongings review at preview; **document clause** — transaction documents shown only redacted; **revocation** — full withdrawal through the moment of publication; **preview commitment** — owner sees the final cut and gives **written approval before upload** (affirmative sign-off, never silence-as-consent); post-publish concerns handled in good faith (blurs, trims), takedown at Taylor's discretion, stated honestly; **editorial line** — approval rights cover identity and privacy plus the whole-video withdrawal right; they do not extend to rewriting the analysis.

**Artifact 3 — redaction pass** on every document shown: names, addresses, loan and account numbers, signatures, MLS numbers.

**Artifact 4 — inspector license check (not a courtesy).** Inspection agreements commonly restrict republication or third-party use of the report (external fact — read the actual agreement during prep; it is the buyer's contract and it is in the file). Default path: written inspector permission to show report excerpts, with an on-screen credit offered ("inspection by a licensed Texas inspector") — most inspectors will trade a credit for a referral relationship. If the agreement restricts and permission is declined: **no report pages and no verbatim report language on screen**; findings are stated in Taylor's own words and priced from independent documents (quotes, invoices, amendment) — and the *fact* that a licensed inspector confirmed each featured item may still be stated, because that is a fact about the transaction, not a reproduction of the report. Either path preserves the Section 6 compliance line; only one shows pages.

**Artifact 5 — owner preview + written approval**, early in the edit, not the night before: an email record reading "Approved for publish," plus resolution of any identity/belongings notes.

**Artifact 6 — broker pre-review + counterparty hygiene.** The sponsoring broker sees the final cut before publish — folding the videographer favor into the compliance chain and satisfying advertising-review prudence (Section 6). Script-level hygiene rule: **no characterization of the seller, the co-op agent, the builder, or any other agent's practices — ever.** No "they tried to hide it," no "the listing should have said," no "what other agents don't do." Documents speak; people are not characterized. This is takedown hygiene and Article 15 hygiene in one (external fact — verify the NAR article number once; the rule stands either way): leave no one with a story to carry to a broker.

## 2. Deliverable 2 — Named recurring segment architecture

### 2.1 What the channel data licenses

Three facts drive the structure (all task ground truth): credibility blocks hemorrhage — 85% → 45% across 0:24–1:22; the scorecard beat at 6:57 in the Fort Hood video is the channel's best-performing beat type (reported at 0.83 relative retention); suggested-feed AVD of 8:59 signals an audience that rewards sustained quality over search bait.

**Metric note (honest epistemics):** the 0.83 figure is the task-reported measurement of one beat in one video, and "relative retention" has a platform-specific baseline definition that must be re-pulled from the channel's analytics before Episode 2 leans on the decimal. Nothing in this plan depends on the decimal — the design depends on the beat-type priority the ground truth states directly ("the channel's best-performing beat type"), which is why the scorecard tally is the midpoint payoff and the spine of the piece, echoed as a stamp every 60–90 seconds rather than spent as one beat.

Design consequences: the setup compresses to ~30 seconds delivered in motion (the leak-zone countermeasure), and the payoff is placed by the canonical formula's 50–60% mandate (fact) — the pilot's alignment of that window with the absolute 6:57 timestamp is a deliberate coincidence, and the *relative* rule is the one the series carries forward (2.4).

### 2.2 Segment map

**Arithmetic base:** target runtime 11:55 = 715s. Payoff anchors: beat start 6:05 = 365s = **51.0%**; verdict-zone landing ~6:57 = 417s = **58.3%**; beat end 7:00 = 420s = **58.7%** — all inside 50–60%. **Edit-drift rule, derived not asserted:** keeping all three anchors inside the window requires 420 ÷ runtime ≤ 60% (runtime ≥ 700s) and 365 ÷ runtime ≥ 50% (runtime ≤ 730s), so the hard band is **11:40–12:10, with both endpoints sitting exactly on the boundary** (700s puts the beat end at 60.0%; 730s puts the beat start at 50.0%). Practical band for ≥ 0.5 points of margin on both sides: **11:46–12:02**. Outside the hard band, the SCORECARD beat itself is re-timed — the anchors move, not the rule (QA row 12 recomputes everything against the final cut).

## 3. Deliverable 3 — The verdict engine

Mirrors Taylor's published inspection-analysis standard verbatim — **severity + likely cost range + stop-the-deal flag** (fact) — and stays entirely in the buyer lane: cash exposure and financing risk for an owner-occupant. No cap rates, no rental math, no return language anywhere in the series (fact: lane rule).

### 3.1 Scorecard categories (five — mapped from Taylor's real showing toolkit, fact)

| # | Category | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Foundation & Structure** | Foundation cracks at masonry base, stair-step brick cracking, slab cracks, roof sag read as a structural signal |
| 2 | **Roof & Envelope** | Patch scan, shingle condition, soffit/fascia/gutter rot, flashing |
| 3 | **Water & Drainage** | Grading toward slab, downspout discharge, splash erosion, staining at the slab line |
| 4 | **Big-Ticket Systems** | Water-heater age plate, HVAC age plate, electrical panel condition |
| 5 | **Loan-Killer Flags (VA/FHA MPR)** | Peeling paint pre-1978, missing handrails, broken glazing, inoperable systems — items that threaten Minimum Property Requirements and therefore the financing itself (fact: in the toolkit). Framed as financing mechanics only |

### 3.2 Severity scale

- **S1 — Note & monitor.** Age-appropriate wear. Logged, no ask generated. Showing S1s is deliberate: an analyst who logs non-issues as non-issues is believed when he logs an S3 — and S1s are usually still filmable, which is why Gate 6 counts them.
- **S2 — Budget item.** Real, priceable, negotiable. Requires a per-finding documented cost (Gate 2).
- **S3 — Stop-and-verify.** Taylor's stop-the-deal flag (fact): no offer math is valid until a licensed inspector — and where indicated, a structural engineer or licensed trade — scopes it. S3 is not "walk"; it is "verify before anything else is true."

### 3.3 The per-finding pipeline (severity → sourced cost → leverage)

1. **Severity on camera** (S1/S2/S3) with the one-line reason.
2. **Cost only with a document.** Lower-third source tag on every figure: `[inspection report]`, `[option-period quote]`, `[invoice]`, `[closing statement]`, `[current quote — MM/YYYY]`. Undocumented findings run the severity-only lane. Planning ranges may exist in the edit only as `[PLANNING — REPLACE]`; the video does not publish until zero of those tags survive. **TRE construction:** the sum of documented figures; where a source gives a range, its midpoint is used and that method is stated once on screen at THE SCORECARD. **Vintage rule:** if any `[current quote]` enters TRE, the mixed vintage travels with the number — the tally card carries a one-line provenance note ("figures: inspection report + written quotes; [n] items priced at [MM/YYYY] current quotes") and all packaging language uses "documented" framing ("$11,900 in documented problems"), never tour-day-dollar framing ("what it cost the day we toured") — that framing is allowed only when every figure is deal-era (QA row 3).
3. **Leverage class per S2/S3** — **R**epair by seller pre-close (verified by re-inspection), **C**redit at close (lender caps checked), **P**rice reduction. Rules stated in the video: *documents are leverage, feelings are not* — only findings with paper attached go in the amendment; *one ask per finding, bundled once* — a single amendment, not a drip. S1s never generate an ask.
4. **Display math per the elected tier (1.3).** Tier E: exact everywhere. Tier B: price as the rounded anchor, on-screen % as a whole percent against that anchor with the ≈ mark, price-reduction figures banded, concessions totals containing them marked ≈, repair figures and credits exact. The true math runs privately; the displayed figures must read in the true zone (zone-preservation rule), verified at Gate 6 and re-verified in QA row 13.

### 3.4 Verdict thresholds — a disclosed rubric, not a law of nature

Introduced on camera, at THE SCORECARD, exactly this way: **"These are my lines — the same lines every episode, so the verdicts mean something across houses. They're an agent's framework, not an inspection standard and not a rule about what you can afford."** (decision, labeled as such on screen)

| Verdict | Rule |
|---|---|
| **BUY** | TRE **< 1.5%** of purchase price AND zero S3 flags. Proceed on standard terms; a small credit ask is optional |
| **NEGOTIATE** | TRE **1.5%–5.0%**, OR any single documented finding **≥ 1.0%** alone, OR an S3 that is scoped, quoted, and curable before closing. The engine produces a documented ask sized to the exposure |
| **WALK** | TRE **> 5.0%**, OR an S3 that cannot be cured or verified before closing (active structural movement without an engineer letter, unscoped moisture intrusion), OR an MPR item the seller refuses to cure on a VA/FHA deal — the loan cannot close, so the deal cannot — OR access denied for verification |

**Where the lines come from, said honestly on camera:** arithmetic anchors plus Taylor's judgment, presented as his. On a ~$300K Temple-area purchase, 1.5% = $4,500 and 5% = $15,000 (arithmetic). The judgment — his, on camera — is that below the first line, grinding a negotiation over reserve-sized items risks the house for little gain, and above the second, post-close exposure stacked on down payment and closing costs is where he starts telling his own buyers to reconsider. No claims about what "most buyers" hold or consider normal — those aren't sourced, so they don't appear. **Calibration pass (prep week):** run three additional past buyer-side files through the engine; if its verdict contradicts what actually happened in two or more, adjust the lines before filming and say so on camera. A back-tested rubric is presented as exactly that (hypothesis, named test).

**Client-decision rule (script-level, non-negotiable):** the engine never decides for the buyer. At THE VERDICT: **"The scorecard hands my buyer a number and a recommendation. The decision is theirs — my job is that they make it with the full picture."** The retrospective shows both: what the engine output and what the buyer chose; if they differ, that is the story, not a problem. Overrides, one line each on screen: an S3 can force WALK at any TRE; a seller cure, re-verified, can move WALK back to NEGOTIATE; the thresholds guide, the flags govern, the client decides.
