# CRITIC — Round 2 (adversarial pass on candidate-a-r2.md, cold start)

WEAKNESS-1 [FATAL]: "**price-disclosure election** — ... its disclosure level is the owner's choice: exact figures / deltas only ('$7,500 off the ask') / percentages / none" (1.6, artifact 2) — The election menu is arithmetically fake, and the document never notices. THE SCORECARD unconditionally shows "% of purchase price computed on screen" (2.2), the tally card carries "TRE line, % of price" (3.5), and QA row 12 REQUIRES "TRE ÷ purchase price matches the on-screen %." So every episode publishes TRE in dollars ($11,900 is literally a thumbnail badge) alongside a percentage — and $11,900 ÷ 4.0% reconstructs the purchase price to ~$297,500 regardless of what the owner elected. The "deltas only" tier is equally illusory: "$7,500 off the ask" plus the archived list price — which the document itself concedes "persist[s] on portals, caches, and screenshots" (1.2) — yields the exact sale price. "Percentages" leaks via the TRE dollars; "none" is incompatible with the format's own mandatory on-screen math, and no reconciliation clause exists anywhere (the H3 note shows the author considered election impact on content, but never on the verdict engine's output). Three of the four privacy tiers are either reconstructable or format-breaking; only "exact" is coherent. This is measured against the document's own standard: "a broken promise to the owner is worse than no promise" (1.2). The consent instrument — the load-bearing wall of the entire frame ("consent carries the weight") — hands the owner precisely such a broken promise, in writing, "mirroring the ask word-for-honest-word."

WEAKNESS-2 [FATAL]: "what stays withheld is only what the viewer genuinely cannot know — the exposure total, the verdict zone the engine outputs, and what the negotiation actually extracted" (Thesis; repeated in 1.3: "The withheld payoffs are legitimate unknowns: the exposure total...") — Flatly contradicted by the document's own packaging. All three hook variants disclose the exposure total in the first spoken sentence: H1 opens "[$11,900] — that's what was wrong with this house"; H2 says "it needed [$11,900] of work"; H3 says "to find [$11,900] in this house." The thumbnail carries a "$11,900" badge (4.3), and Shorts S4 puts it in a title (5.4). There is no packaging path under which the exposure total is withheld past second zero. Yet 2.2 stakes the midpoint payoff on "Total Repair Exposure revealed" at THE SCORECARD, and the corner counter's whole device is ticking suspensefully toward a number every viewer already read on the thumbnail before clicking. The centerpiece "reveal" of the retention architecture — the beat the entire 50–60% placement doctrine, the edit floor, and the runtime lock exist to protect — is pre-spoiled by the same document three sections later. The tension model as stated is false on its face; what actually survives as a withheld payoff is only the verdict zone, which means the engineered midpoint beat is roughly half as loaded as every retention calculation assumes.

WEAKNESS-3 [MAJOR]: "the edit may breathe between 11:35 and 12:30 without re-cutting the payoff" (2.2, edit-drift rule; enshrined as "edit floor 11:35" in Locked Decisions) — This band fails the document's own QA row 12, which requires "payoff start, completion, and close ÷ runtime all land inside 50–60%." At the claimed floor of 11:35 (695s), the window close (7:00 = 420s) = 420/695 = 60.4% — outside. At the claimed cap of 12:30 (750s), the payoff start (6:05 = 365s) = 365/750 = 48.7% — outside on the low end. The QA-compliant band is actually 11:40–12:10 (700–730s), not 11:35–12:30; both advertised endpoints violate the gate that is supposed to enforce them, and even the floor's showpiece computation (417/695 = 60.0%) sits exactly on the boundary with zero seconds of margin. This error survives in a revision whose header brags of a "full arithmetic audit" and whose QA row 12 exists specifically to catch "arithmetic drift." The audit audited everything except its own new rule.

WEAKNESS-4 [MAJOR]: "you're about to see all [five] flags in the order I caught them" (H1) vs. "+2 findings still visually filmable today" as a mere TIEBREAK (1.4) — Filmability is not a gate. The decision rule books the videographer for "the first deal to clear all six" gates, and the tiebreak only fires "if ≥ 2 clear" — so the selected property can clear every gate with most findings repaired and invisible (Gate 4 actively REWARDS "seller-completed repairs at invoice value," i.e., selects for houses where the defects were fixed). The seven-shot recipe requires an "Evidence macro — the crack, the plate, the rot" per stress point; repaired rot cannot be filmed. The proposed patch is incoherent on its own terms: the tiebreak awards "+2 seller-completed repairs visible (before/after from re-shot footage — see the photo rule)" — but "before" footage of a repaired defect cannot be re-shot after the repair by definition, and the photo rule default-bans the only existing before imagery ("Photos Taylor took during showings... Default: do not publish them"). The format's core visual promise can be unfulfillable by the exact property its own selection rule chooses, discovered only after the non-recurring pro day is booked.

WEAKNESS-5 [MAJOR]: "every foreseeable objector identified and structurally answered before publish" (1.1/1.6's definition of "cleared") — The register omits the most foreseeable injured party: the consenting owner's future self. The video creates a permanent, searchable, agent-authored ledger of the house's defects, severities, and the concessions extracted — findable by every future buyer's agent, appraiser, and insurer the day the owner lists. The document articulates this exact game-theory defect to kill the active-listing frame — "hands the seller's side the entire negotiation file for free" (1.1) — and then commits the identical leak, time-shifted, against its own client's future sale, plus a Texas seller-disclosure footprint (the video is a public record of known-defect history). The ask script sells the owner "a series that shows buyers what I check" and never warns them the video may cost them money at resale; the register's six rows cover recognition, offense, revocation, inspectors, the broker, and drones — nothing on the owner's asset value. The informed-consent quality of the one signature the entire frame depends on is thereby overstated, and the "every foreseeable objector" claim is false as written. NAR-duty optics compound it: an agent monetizing his own client's defect file for lead generation is precisely the story a hostile broker or commenter tells.

WEAKNESS-6 [MAJOR]: "impressions velocity ~29/day was the binding constraint on the last pillar (fact)" (Section 7) vs. the 2.4 measurement plan's mechanical bands ("≥ 0.78... 0.70–0.77... < 0.70") — At ~29 impressions/day, the pilot's early-window audience is tens to low hundreds of views; a per-timestamp relative-retention estimate at that sample size has a confidence interval wider than all three decision bands combined, so distinguishing 0.78 ("confirmed, carry unchanged") from 0.77 ("test alternate placement") is measurement theater. The document applies a "small-sample caveat" to instrument 2 (AVD) but not to instrument 1 — the one that drives the Episode 3 decision. Worse, the hypothesis is confounded by design: the payoff is engineered "at effectively the same timestamp" (absolute 6:57) AND inside 50–60% of runtime simultaneously, so even a clean pass cannot say which placement rule was confirmed — and "carry to Episode 2 unchanged" is undefined the moment Episode 2 has a different runtime, since the two rules then diverge. "A test instead of a belief" is the claim; an underpowered test of an unidentifiable hypothesis is the design.

WEAKNESS-7 [MAJOR]: "Next test: a deal I'm logging right now — you'll see that house after my buyers close, with receipts." (2.2, YOUR SHORT LIST, labeled "honest series tease") — This on-camera line violates the document's own claim discipline three ways. (1) It guarantees a contingent outcome: "you'll see" presumes the live deal closes, while the same document states "deals die" (1.1) and "termination kills the episode" (1.5) — QA row 7 sweeps for "'guaranteed' outcomes" and this is one. (2) Its factual predicate — that a real deal is actually under desk-logging at publish — is verified by no gate and no QA row, in a plan whose slot rule says "no video ships with an unverified slot or claim." (3) It collides with the format calendar: tracked-live is "allowed from Episode 3+" (1.5), but "Next test" positions it as the next episode, while the continuity check commits Episode 2 to a standard solo build. The pilot's scripted closing promise is one the series' own constitution forbids keeping on schedule and its own risk logic says may be impossible to keep at all.
