# TEMPLE HOUSE STRESS TEST — Pilot + Series Architecture 

**Thesis.** The pilot is a retrospective consent build (Frame B): Taylor re-walks a house his buyer client already closed on, re-runs the exact stress test he performed on tour day, stamps every finding onto a running scorecard, and pays the video off with the real negotiation documents — his on-site estimates graded against the licensed inspector's report and the actual contractor quotes. The verdict is not "did he buy it" (disclosed early, honestly); the verdict is the scorecard's output and the dollar delta it produced, withheld to the 55–60% mark and the final act respectively. This is the only frame of the three that is structurally un-take-downable, the only one that ships with real documents instead of planning ranges, and the only one that can be scheduled around a one-day videographer commitment. The one professional day is spent here — GO — because both queued alternatives are explicitly self-shootable, and the pro day's unique value is banking series-lifetime assets against the one format built to repeat.

## 1. Locked pilot frame + property sourcing rule

### 1.1 Why Frame B beats A and C (the fatal-error analysis)

**Frame A (live active-listing build) is banned, permanently.** It is the takedown vector restated: an identifiable active listing, publicly criticized, before the buyer's offer. This is the exact structure that got the last video forced down. It fails inverse-fail #1 by design, not by accident. It also has a hidden game-theory defect: publishing the stress test of a house Taylor's buyer is about to offer on hands the seller's side Taylor's entire negotiation file for free. A cannot be fixed with careful wording — the target is a third party with standing, a co-op agent with a grievance, and a broker relationship on the line. Banned.

**Frame C (live option-period build) fails on consent and scheduling, not on honesty.** Three structural problems:

1. **The consent gap is unsolvable during filming.** During the option period the *seller* owns the house. A buyer's access under a showing or inspection appointment is licensed for transaction purposes — not for media production. Filming a video essay about the house's defects inside a home the seller still owns, without the seller's knowledge, is a consent problem no address-masking fixes. The one party who can't consent is the one whose property it is.
2. **Timing is uncontrollable against a one-day videographer commitment.** Option periods run days; closings run weeks; deals die. You cannot book the single non-recurring pro resource against a window a lender or an appraiser controls.
3. **The back-door takedown vector.** If the deal terminates over the findings, the house goes back on market — and now there exists footage of its defects, shot inside it, about an active listing. Masking in a market Temple's size is fragile; a co-op agent can recognize a house from its kitchen.

C is not dead as a *series* lane — see 1.4 — but it cannot carry the pilot.

**Frame B is structurally un-take-downable.** Buyer = current owner = the consent-giver. The property is off-market; no third party has standing. The outcome is resolved, so real documents exist — inspection report, repair amendment, final numbers — which means every figure on screen is sourced, satisfying the "specific real numbers only" rule without a single labeled planning range surviving to publish. And the frame carries a built-in non-disparagement shield: **the house was bought.** The video's thesis is not "this house is bad"; it is "here is what a trained eye caught, what each item cost in real quotes, and how those documents set the final price." The current owner is the client who benefited. There is nobody left to be angry.

### 1.2 The staging problem, solved by honest architecture (inverse-fail #4)

Frame B's outcome *is* known in advance, so the design must never pretend otherwise. The rule: **disclose the retrospective early; withhold the numbers, not the fact of the purchase.**

- At 0:35–0:48 (inside THE GROUND RULES), Taylor says on camera that his buyer owns this house now and consented to the video. No viewer can later "discover" it was staged — it was announced.
- The withheld payoffs are things the viewer genuinely doesn't know: **(1)** which finding moved the price and by how much — the scorecard tally, resolving at 56–63%; **(2)** the real negotiation outcome with documents — the final act; **(3)** an honesty layer unique to this frame: **Taylor grades his own tour-day estimates against the licensed inspector's report and the actual quotes.** "I called this one at $1,500–$2,500. The quote came in at $2,240. I called the panel wrong — here's what I missed." An analyst who audits himself on camera is the opposite of staged; it is the single strongest authenticity move available to the channel.

Tension architecture without dishonesty: rolling curiosity (what did he find), midpoint payoff (what does it total, what's the verdict), final payoff (what actually happened, was he right).

### 1.3 Property sourcing rule (the decision rule Taylor runs this week)

**Screening universe:** Taylor's buyer-side closings from the last 9 months. Run every deal through the gates in order; the first deal that clears all six gates books the videographer. If two or more clear, apply the tiebreak score.

**Gate 0 — Deal shape.** Buyer-side closed transaction, Taylor was the buyer's agent, closed ≤ 9 months ago (house still resembles tour day; findings or their repairs still filmable).

**Gate 1 — Consent obtainable.** Every titled owner will sign the written media release (spec in 1.5). No release, no episode — regardless of how good the story is.

**Gate 2 — Document trail.** The deal file contains the licensed inspection report AND at least one of: repair amendment, seller credit on the closing statement, or contractor quotes gathered during the option period. This gate is what makes inverse-fail #3 (invented numbers) impossible: if the documents don't exist, the deal doesn't qualify.

**Gate 3 — Findings density.** ≥ 4 findings spanning ≥ 3 of the five scorecard categories (Section 3), with ≥ 1 finding at Severity 2 or higher carrying a documented cost. Fewer than four findings can't sustain the segment architecture; one category can't demonstrate the system.

**Gate 4 — Negotiation delta.** Findings measurably moved the deal: total extracted value (price reduction + credits + seller-completed repairs at invoice value) ≥ 1.0% of purchase price. Below that, the payoff doesn't land and the hook can't be written honestly.

**Gate 5 — No landmines.** No ongoing dispute on the transaction; no builder-defect framing required (prefer resale ≥ 5 years old — the no-firsthand-builder-defect-claims rule stays untouched); owners comfortable with the general area being named; nothing in the file that would require fair-housing-adjacent commentary to explain.

**Tiebreak score (only if ≥ 2 deals clear):** +2 findings still visually filmable today (or pre-repair photos from tour day exist — Taylor owns his own photos and the release covers their use); +2 seller-completed repairs visible (before/after is the best possible B-roll); +1 exterior variety (multiple facades, standing-drone photo angles); +1 owner willing to appear for an optional ≤ 20-second cameo (never required); +1 within 25 minutes of Temple (protects the pro-day schedule). Highest score wins; tie goes to the most recent closing.

**Anti-rule:** if no deal clears all six gates within two weeks, do not relax Gate 1 or Gate 2. Relax Gate 0's recency to 12 months first, then Gate 3's density to 3 findings. Consent and documents are never negotiable.

### 1.4 Series-forward sourcing rules (so the pilot's rule scales)

| Frame | Status | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| **B — retrospective consent** | Default for every on-site episode | Full gate screen above, every time |
| **C — live option-period** | Allowed from Episode 3+ only | (a) buyer's written consent before filming; (b) footage shot by Taylor only during his own legitimate access, minimal and non-disruptive; (c) publication ONLY after the buyer closes and, now as owner, ratifies the release post-closing; (d) **if the deal terminates, the footage is archived and never published** — termination kills the episode, it does not trigger harder masking. This rule deletes C's back-door takedown vector entirely |

### 1.5 The consent chain (the "cleared" half of the deliverable)

The ground truth demands a *documented* consent chain. It has five artifacts, all stored in the deal folder:

1. **The ask** (call or in-person, then confirmed by email): "I'm starting a series showing buyers exactly what I check before they make an offer. I'd like to feature the walkthrough I did on your house — the flags I caught and how they shaped the deal. You'd approve what's shown, your address never appears, and you can see the video before it goes live. Can I send you the one-page release?"
2. **The written media release**, covering: interior/exterior filming including drone still photos; use on YouTube, templetxhomes.net, and social; discussion of findings and transaction documents in redacted form; **price-disclosure election** — Texas is a non-disclosure state, so the sold price is not public record and disclosing it is the owner's choice: exact figures / deltas only ("$7,500 off the ask") / percentages only; **anonymity election** — names used, first name only, or no names; optional on-camera cameo yes/no; fixed policy stated in the release: street address and street signage never shown, no geotag; revocation window through the publish date; final-cut preview offered as a courtesy (identity and personal-belongings concerns honored; editorial control of the analysis not granted).
3. **Redaction pass** on every document shown: names, loan numbers, account numbers, signatures, addresses within documents.
4. **Preview confirmation** — email record that the owner saw the final cut and raised no identity/belongings objection.
5. **Counterparty hygiene rule (script-level):** no characterization of the seller's or co-op agent's intent, ever — no "they tried to hide," no "the listing agent should have." Documents speak; people are not characterized. This removes the only grievance surface Frame B leaves open. Nothing is owed to the seller side legally; this rule is about leaving no one with a story to tell a broker.

## 2. Named recurring segment architecture (timestamp bands + retention design)

### 2.1 The retention problem this structure exists to solve

Two facts from channel data dictate everything: **(1)** credibility blocks hemorrhage — 85% → 45% across 0:24–1:22 on prior videos, a 40-point leak in under a minute of setup; **(2)** the scorecard beat at 6:57 in the Fort Hood video hit 0.83 relative retention, the channel's best-performing beat type. Therefore: the setup is compressed to 28 seconds delivered in motion, and the scorecard is not one beat — it is the **spine**, echoed every 60–90 seconds as a stamp and paid off in full at the proven position.

### 2.3 Structure-convention compliance check

- **Pattern interrupt every 60–90s:** location or energy changes at 0:50, 2:05, 2:15, 3:25, 4:30 (exterior→interior), 5:35, 6:35, 7:25 (site→desk), 9:20 (desk→doorstep), 10:30. Maximum gap: ~70 seconds. Every interrupt is a location/energy change — never graphic-only.
- **A/B alternation in 7–12s blocks:** inside every stress point, A-blocks are Taylor on camera (wide/MCU), B-blocks are evidence inserts (macro, hands, demonstrative). Stamps ride on the final B-block of each finding.
- **Lower-thirds only:** stamps, cost bands, and the counter are lower-third/corner elements. The full-tally card at THE SCORECARD is staged as a physical/desk prop or side-panel over the standup — never a full-screen graphic over Taylor's face.
- **One creative element:** the Scorecard Stamp System (stamps + counter + tally card + verdict stamp). Everything else is existing convention.
- **4–6 stress points supported:** the pilot map shows five; the architecture flexes to four (stretch bands 2–4 by ~15s each) or six (compress interrupts, cap recipe at 60s each) depending on what the chosen deal file actually contains. Findings shown are only ever the findings that actually occurred.

## 3. The verdict engine

Built to mirror Taylor's published inspection-analysis standard verbatim: **severity + likely cost range + stop-the-deal flag.** Buyer lane only: the engine prices *cash exposure and financing risk for an owner-occupant* — no cap rates, no rental math, no return language anywhere.

### 3.1 Scorecard categories (five — direct mapping from Taylor's real showing toolkit)

| # | Category | What's in it (from the toolkit) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Foundation & Structure** | Foundation cracks at masonry base, stair-step brick cracking, slab cracks, roof sag as structural signal |
| 2 | **Roof & Envelope** | Roof patches/repairs, shingle condition, soffit/fascia/gutter rot, flashing |
| 3 | **Water & Drainage** | Grading toward slab, downspout discharge, splash erosion, moisture staining |
| 4 | **Big-Ticket Systems** | Water-heater age plate, HVAC age plate, electrical panel condition/brand flags |
| 5 | **Loan-Killer Flags (VA/FHA MPR)** | Peeling paint pre-1978, missing handrails, broken glazing, inoperable systems — anything that fails Minimum Property Requirements and threatens the financing itself. Heavy-VA corridor makes this category disproportionately valuable to the audience |

### 3.2 Severity scale (per finding)

- **S1 — Note & monitor.** Cosmetic or age-appropriate wear. Goes on the card, generates no ask. Showing S1s is deliberate: an analyst who logs non-issues as non-issues is more credible when he logs an S3.
- **S2 — Budget item.** Real, quotable, negotiable. Requires a documented cost band.
- **S3 — Stop-and-verify.** Taylor's "stop-the-deal flag": nobody proceeds until a licensed inspector — and where indicated, a structural engineer or licensed trade — scopes it. An S3 is not "walk"; it is "no offer math is valid until this is verified."

### 3.3 Severity → cost → leverage conversion (the pipeline every finding runs)

1. **Severity assigned on camera** (S1/S2/S3), with the one-line reason.
2. **Cost band attached — documented sources only.** Source tag appears in the lower-third: `[inspection report]`, `[contractor quote]`, `[invoice]`. Planning ranges may exist in the edit as `[PLANNING — REPLACE]` and the video does not publish until zero of those tags survive. Total Repair Exposure (TRE) = sum of band midpoints; the midpoint method is stated on screen once, at THE SCORECARD, for honesty.
3. **Leverage conversion.** Each S2/S3 becomes one ask, classed R / C / P: **R**epair by seller before close (verified by re-inspection), **C**redit at close (lender caps checked), **P**rice reduction. Rules stated in the video: *documents are leverage, feelings are not* — only findings with paper attached go in the amendment; **one ask per finding, bundled once** — a single amendment, not a drip of demands. S1s generate no ask, ever.

### 3.4 Verdict thresholds (% of purchase price)

| Verdict | Rule |
|---|---|
| **BUY** | TRE **< 1.5%** of purchase price AND zero S3 flags. Proceed on standard terms; small-credit ask optional. |
| **NEGOTIATE** | TRE **1.5%–5.0%**, OR any single documented finding **≥ 1.0%** by itself, OR any S3 that is *scoped, quoted, and curable before closing*. Proceed only with concessions equal to the documented exposure. |
| **WALK** | TRE **> 5.0%**, OR any S3 that cannot be cured or verified before closing (active structural movement with no engineer letter, unscoped moisture intrusion), OR a Loan-Killer flag the seller refuses to cure (the loan cannot close, so the deal cannot), OR the seller denies access for verification. |

**Why these numbers (stated in the video as "my framework as an agent — not an inspection standard"):** on a ~$300K Temple-area purchase, 1.5% is ~$4,500 — beneath that line, items sit inside a normal first-year ownership reserve, and burning negotiation capital on them risks the house without meaningful gain. 5% is ~$15,000 — beyond that line, post-close cash exposure stacks on top of down payment and closing costs past what most owner-occupant buyers hold in reserve, and appraisal/lender risk compounds on financed deals. Between the lines is precisely the job: converting documents into concessions. (Illustrative repair figures used in calibration — e.g., foundation pier work, full HVAC replacement — are labeled planning ranges in prep documents and are replaced by the deal file's real quotes before anything is filmed.)

**Override rules (stated on screen in one line each):** an S3 can force WALK at any TRE; a seller cure, re-verified, can move a WALK back to NEGOTIATE; thresholds guide the decision — the flags govern it.

### 3.5 On-screen artifacts + lead magnet mirror

- **Per-finding stamp (lower-third, ~4s):** `STRESS POINT #3 · WATER & DRAINAGE · S2 · $1,150–$1,600 [contractor quote]`
- **Running counter (corner):** `EXPOSURE: $6,450` — ticks on each stamp.
- **Full-tally card (THE SCORECARD):** five categories × (flags / max severity / exposure band), TRE line, % of price, verdict-zone bar.
- **Lead magnet:** "The Temple House Stress Test Scorecard" — one-page PDF on templetxhomes.net mirroring the exact card: five categories, S1/S2/S3 boxes, cost column with source line, leverage class column, TRE line, the three threshold bands printed, and a footer carrying the not-an-inspector language plus IABS and Consumer Protection Notice links. The viewer downloads the same instrument they watched — that congruence is the conversion mechanism.

## 5. Videographer shot architecture (the one pro day)

### 5.1 Per-finding shot recipe (the repeatable unit — designed solo-first)

Every stress point, pilot and solo episodes alike, is built from the same seven shots. On the pro day, shots 1–2 are A-cam (videographer, gimbal/handheld) while B-cam covers 3–4 simultaneously; solo, Taylor shoots 1–2 and 5 on tripod and picks up 3–4 and 6–7 handheld afterward. Identical structure, different labor.

1. **Approach POV** — moving toward the area, 5–8s (gimbal pro / chest-height handheld solo).
2. **Context wide** — Taylor at the finding, delivers the Spot line ("Most buyers walk right past this"), 7–12s.
3. **Evidence macro** — the crack, the plate, the rot; rack focus; 7–12s.
4. **Hands-on demonstrative** — tape measure across the crack, level app on the grade, flashlight into the panel; 7–12s.
5. **Severity MCU** — Taylor direct to camera: severity call + documented cost band + leverage line; 7–12s.
6. **Stamp plate** — locked-off 4s hold for the lower-third stamp to land clean.
7. **Stills pair** — one photo of the finding (lead magnet + blog + GBP), one vertical clip pickup for Shorts.

### 5.2 Run-of-day (one day, ~9.5 hours, buffered)

| Window | Block |
|---|---|
| 07:45–08:15 | Arrive; verify signed release on file (no release, cameras stay in the truck); walk order-of-shoot; light check |
| 08:15–09:00 | Morning-light exteriors: establishing set (all facades), **credential-in-motion walking shot** (the 0:30 beat), drone **stills** by Taylor — photos only per the ND-filter constraint; if the videographer brings ND'd drone gear, upgrade to short aerial clips as bonus, never as dependency |
| 09:00–11:30 | Exterior stress points (foundation, roofline/soffit, drainage) — full recipe each + vertical pickups after each block |
| 11:30–12:00 | Cold-open cinematic package: slow push-ins on the three best evidence macros; THE WALK-UP approach footage |
| 12:00–12:30 | Break; card offload + backup (both bodies) |

| **Stress Test pilot** | Yes — uniquely | Three things only the pro day can buy: **(1)** two-camera, full-recipe coverage of a consented location Taylor cannot casually re-access (it is a client's occupied home — one clean day beats four return visits); **(2)** the series-lifetime asset bank — brand kit, thumbnail plate library, desk-setup replication kit — that amortizes across every future self-shot episode and makes them look continuous with the pilot; **(3)** ceiling-setting on the one format actually engineered for repeatable series economics and aimed at the channel's proven best retention beat and its highest-value CTA (buyer representation) |

**Why this is also the right business bet, not just the right production bet:** the format is built around the scorecard beat — the channel's best-performing retention type (0.83) — and terminates in the buyer-rep CTA plus a lead magnet that is literally the instrument demonstrated. With impressions velocity ~29/day as the binding constraint, the channel cannot buy reach with volume; it can only make each impression convert harder — which is a packaging-and-format problem, which is what the pilot is.

**Inverse-fail #5 check on the GO:** the pro day is structured so the pilot does not set a bar Taylor can't clear solo — the finding recipe is tripod-first, and the polish elements (intro sting, stamps, desk look) are *banked assets*, not per-episode production. Episode 2 self-shot will look like the same show.

1. This week: run the property screen across the last 9 months of buyer-side closings; get the release signed; pull and redact the deal documents.
