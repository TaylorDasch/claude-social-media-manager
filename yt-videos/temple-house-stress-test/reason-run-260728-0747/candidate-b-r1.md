# TEMPLE HOUSE STRESS TEST — Pilot + Series Architecture (Candidate B, Round 1)

**Thesis.** The pilot is a retrospective consent build: Taylor re-walks a house his buyer client already owns, re-runs the stress test he performed on tour day, stamps each finding onto a running scorecard, and pays the video off with the deal's real documents — his tour-day calls graded against the licensed inspection report and the written quotes in the file. The verdict is computed from those documents, not chosen in advance; the fact of the purchase is disclosed inside the first minute, and what stays withheld is only what the viewer genuinely cannot know: the total, the verdict the engine outputs, and what the negotiation actually extracted. This frame removes every lever the last takedown used — no active listing, no third-party target, an owner who signed a release — while shipping with sourced numbers instead of planning ranges. The one videographer day goes here: GO, conditional on a signed release and a broker-confirmed date, because both queued alternatives are self-shootable by the task's own description and the pro day's non-recurring value is the series-lifetime asset bank.

**Claim discipline used in this document.** Three labels appear throughout: **(fact)** — stated in the task's ground-truth pack or verifiable in the deal file; **(decision)** — a design choice Taylor is free to overrule; **(hypothesis)** — a bet with a named test. Anything carrying a dollar figure or a behavioral claim without one of these labels is a defect; the QA gate at the end sweeps for exactly that.

## 1. Locked pilot frame + property sourcing rule

### 1.1 The frame analysis

**Active-listing build — rejected permanently.** An identifiable active listing, publicly analyzed for defects before the buyer's offer, is the structure that produced the takedown (fact). It targets a third party's transaction, hands the co-op agent a grievance, and puts the broker relationship on the line at the exact moment the broker is doing Taylor a favor. It also leaks strategy: publishing the stress test of a house Taylor's buyer is about to offer on gives the seller's side the negotiation file for free. No wording fixes this; the target is the problem. Rejected for the pilot and for the series, including "positive verdict" versions — public commentary on someone else's active transaction is the exposure, regardless of tone (decision).

**Live option-period build — rejected in its filmed form, for one controlling reason: the seller owns the house.** During a Texas option period the property still belongs to — and is usually still occupied and controlled by — the seller. A buyer's access exists for transaction purposes: tours, inspections, measuring. It is not a license to produce media inside someone else's home, the buyer cannot grant one, and closing later does not retroactively authorize footage shot when the seller owned the place. Masking does not cure it either (see 1.2 — masking barely works at all), and if the deal terminates, defect footage of a re-listed active listing exists in an edit bay, which is the takedown vector rebuilt from parts. So the rule is a bright line: **no media-production filming inside or over a property the client does not yet own. Ever. Pilot or series.** What survives from this frame's honest appeal — the "this is happening right now" energy — is captured a safe way in Section 1.5 (decision).

**Retrospective consent build — selected.** The buyer client now holds title, which makes them the one party who can actually consent, and the resolved outcome means the numbers on screen come from an inspection report, written quotes, an amendment, and a closing statement rather than from estimates (fact: the task confirms such closed deals exist). The thesis of the episode is inherently non-disparaging: the house was bought — the story is "what a trained eye caught, what the documents priced it at, and how that shaped the deal," told with the current owner's written permission.

What this frame is **not** is risk-free, and this document will not claim it is. The residual risks are named, mitigated, and accepted in 1.6 — that is what "cleared" means here: not "nobody could object," but "every foreseeable objector identified and structurally answered before publish."

### 1.2 The identifiability doctrine: consent is the shield, masking is a courtesy

A recently sold house has a public photographic history — listing photos persist on portals, caches, and screenshots, and anyone motivated can match a kitchen or a facade. So this plan does not promise anonymity, because that promise cannot be kept, and a broken promise to the owner is worse than no promise (decision).

The doctrine:

1. **Consent carries the weight.** The release is written on the assumption the house may be recognized. The owner signs knowing that.
2. **Masking is a stated courtesy with disclosed limits.** No street address, no street signage, no house numbers, no geotag, no names unless elected — and the release says in plain English: "People who know the home, or who compare it to its old listing photos, may recognize it. We reduce casual discoverability; we cannot make a house anonymous."
3. **The courtesy is still real.** Avoid recreating the old listing's hero angle; frame tight; keep neighboring properties out of frame; drone stills (if the legal gate in 5.2 clears) cropped to the subject property.
4. **Recognition by the seller side is planned for, not wished away.** The people most likely to recognize the house are the former seller and the co-op agent. The counterparty hygiene rule (1.6, artifact 6) exists for exactly them: nothing in the script characterizes any person's intent or conduct, the findings shown are the buyer's own licensed inspection results, and the broker reviews the cut before publish. If they recognize the house, what they find is a factual, consented, complimentary-to-no-one-and-hostile-to-no-one account of a closed transaction.

### 1.3 The staging problem: the verdict is computed, not cast

Two rules keep the retrospective honest:

**Rule 1 — disclose the retrospective early, withhold only what the viewer can't know.** At ~0:32–0:45, on camera, Taylor states that his buyer owns the house and consented (verbatim line in Section 6). Nobody can "discover" in the comments what the video announced in its first minute. The withheld payoffs are legitimate unknowns: the exposure total, the verdict zone it lands in, which finding moved the price, and what the negotiation produced in documents.

**Rule 2 — the verdict is whatever the file says.** The engine (Section 3) runs on the chosen deal's documents during prep, and the episode reports its output — BUY, NEGOTIATE, or WALK-that-became-a-cure, whatever the math yields. No verdict is selected before a property is selected. What IS selected is the kind of deal that makes a good episode: the sourcing gates below screen for deals where findings demonstrably mattered, and the video is allowed to say that out loud ("I picked a deal where the scorecard did real work — that's a choice about what makes a useful episode, not a claim about the average Temple house"). Selection is disclosed; outcomes are not manufactured (decision).

The third honesty layer is the strongest one available to an analyst brand: **Taylor grades his own tour-day calls against the licensed inspector's report and the written quotes** — including at least one call he got wrong or the inspector cleared. "I flagged the panel; the inspector cleared it — here's what I over-weighted." An on-camera self-audit is the structural opposite of staging, and it is only possible in a frame where the answer key exists.

### 1.4 Property sourcing rule (the decision rule Taylor runs this week)

**Screening universe:** buyer-side closings from the last 9 months (extendable to 12 by the anti-rule). Run each deal through the gates in order; first deal to clear all six books the videographer; ties go to the tiebreak score.

- **Gate 0 — Deal shape.** Closed buyer-side transaction, Taylor as buyer's agent, closed ≤ 9 months ago so the property still resembles tour day and findings or their repairs are still filmable.
- **Gate 1 — Consent obtainable.** Every titled owner will sign the written release in 1.6. No release, no episode, regardless of story quality. Non-negotiable.
- **Gate 2 — Document trail at the FINDING level, not the file level.** For every finding that will carry a dollar figure on screen, the file must contain that finding's own source: its line in the licensed inspection report plus a written quote, invoice, amendment line, or closing-statement credit that prices it. A file-level "there's an amendment somewhere" does not qualify a finding — one amendment cannot source six cost bands. Two release valves keep this honest without inventing numbers: **(a) the quote-refresh pass** — during prep, Taylor obtains current written contractor quotes for any inspection-confirmed finding that never got priced during the deal, shown on screen tagged as a current quote with its date; **(b) the severity-only lane** — a finding with no document gets shown with severity and inspector language and **no dollar figure**, with the on-camera line: "No document, no number — I don't put dollars on screen I can't source." That line is a feature, not a concession.
- **Gate 3 — Findings density, inspector-confirmed.** ≥ 4 findings spanning ≥ 3 of the five scorecard categories, each featured finding appearing in **both** Taylor's tour-day notes **and** the licensed inspection report, with ≥ 1 at Severity 2+ carrying a documented cost. Restricting featured stress points to inspector-confirmed items is what makes the compliance line in Section 6 true by construction; tour-day flags the inspector cleared are not deleted — they move to the grade-my-calls beat, labeled as exactly that.
- **Gate 4 — Negotiation delta.** Findings measurably moved the deal: price reduction + credits + seller-completed repairs at invoice value ≥ 1.0% of purchase price. The 1.0% floor is a story-quality heuristic, not a market fact (decision): below it, the payoff the hook promises can't be delivered honestly.
- **Gate 5 — No landmines.** Transaction fully closed and funded; no ongoing or threatened dispute; no builder-defect framing required — prefer resale ≥ 5 years old so the no-firsthand-builder-defect-claims rule is never even approached (fact: standing rule); owner comfortable with the general area being named; nothing in the file that would need fair-housing-adjacent territory (schools, safety, who lives nearby) to explain. The house, never the neighborhood's people.

**Tiebreak score (only if ≥ 2 clear):** +2 findings still visibly filmable today; +2 seller-completed repairs visible (before/after from re-shot footage — see the photo rule below); +1 multiple distinct exterior zones (protects the pattern-interrupt cadence); +1 owner open to an optional ≤ 20-second cameo (never required, never pressured); +1 within 25 minutes of Temple (protects the pro-day schedule). Highest wins; tie to most recent closing.

**Tour-day photo rule (conservative by design).** Photos Taylor took during showings were taken while the seller owned the home, for transaction purposes. Default: do not publish them. Re-shoot everything post-close under the release. Exception path only for non-identifying macro close-ups (a crack fills the frame, nothing else recognizable), and only after the broker-review pass in 1.6 signs off (decision — a prudence line, not a claimed legal ruling).

**Anti-rule:** if nothing clears in two weeks, never relax Gates 1 or 2. Relax Gate 0 to 12 months first, then Gate 3 to 3 findings. Consent and per-finding documents are the two things this format cannot function without.

### 1.6 The consent chain (six artifacts, all stored in the deal folder)

1. **The ask** — call or in-person, confirmed by email, and it must promise exactly what the release delivers, no more: "I'm starting a series that shows buyers what I check before they offer on a house. I'd like to feature yours — the flags I caught on our tour and how they shaped your deal. Here's the honest structure: you approve everything that identifies you — names, faces, anything personal in frame, and how much of the price story gets told. The analysis itself — what I flagged and what it cost — stays mine. You see the full cut before it publishes, and up until it publishes you can pull the plug entirely, no questions. Can I send you the one-page release that says all of that in writing?"
2. **The written release**, whose terms mirror the ask word-for-honest-word: scope (interior/exterior filming; drone still photos contingent on 5.2's legal gate; use on YouTube, templetxhomes.net, and social); **identifiability acknowledgment** in plain language per 1.2 — no anonymity is promised; **identity-element approvals**: name usage (full / first / none), on-camera cameo (yes/no), personal belongings review at preview; **price-disclosure election** — Texas is a non-disclosure state (fact), so the sale price is not public record and its disclosure level is the owner's choice: exact figures / deltas only ("$X off ask") / percentages / none; **document clause** — transaction documents shown only in redacted form; **revocation** — full withdrawal right through the moment of publication; **preview commitment** — owner sees the final cut and provides written approval before upload (affirmative sign-off, not silence); **post-publish concerns** handled in good faith (blurs, trims) with takedown at Taylor's discretion — stated honestly rather than implied; **editorial line** — approval rights cover identity and privacy elements plus the whole-video withdrawal right; they do not extend to rewriting the analysis. The ask and the release now say the same thing, which is what makes the consent informed.
3. **Redaction pass** on every document that appears: names, addresses, loan and account numbers, signatures, MLS numbers.
4. **Inspector courtesy clearance** (decision — prudence, not a claimed legal requirement): the inspection report is the transaction's work product, but before report pages appear on screen, Taylor asks the inspector's OK and offers an on-screen credit ("inspection by a licensed Texas inspector"). If declined, the report is represented by Taylor's own scorecard summary card and the findings are described, not photographed. Either path keeps the numbers; one of them also builds a referral relationship.
5. **Owner preview + written approval** — email record: "Approved for publish," plus resolution of any identity/belongings notes.
6. **Broker pre-review + counterparty hygiene.** The sponsoring broker sees the final cut before publish. This is the videographer's own broker favor folded into the compliance chain: it satisfies advertising-review prudence (Section 6), and it means the brokerage is never surprised by its own agent's video. Paired with the script-level hygiene rule: **no characterization of the seller, the co-op agent, the builder, or any other agent's practices — ever.** No "they tried to hide it," no "the listing should have said," and no "what other agents don't do." Documents speak; people are not characterized; competitors are not referenced even by implication. This is both takedown hygiene and NAR Article 15 hygiene in one rule.

Design consequences: the setup compresses to ~30 seconds delivered in motion; the scorecard is not one beat but the spine — echoed every 60–90 seconds as a stamp, paid off in full inside the mandated window.

### 2.3 Convention compliance check

- **Pattern interrupt every 60–90s** (fact: required): changes at 0:20, 0:50, 1:55, 2:05, 3:10, 4:10 (ext→int), 5:10, 6:05 (walk→standup), 7:00 (site→desk), ~8:05 (stand-and-pin energy shift), 9:05 (desk→doorstep), 10:15. Longest gap: 70s (9:05→10:15). Every interrupt is a location or energy change, never graphic-only.
- **A/B alternation in 7–12s blocks** inside every stress point: A = Taylor on camera, B = evidence inserts; stamps ride the final B-block.
- **Lower-thirds only**: stamps, counter, and cost lines are lower-third/corner elements; the tally card is a physical prop and a side-panel — never a full-screen graphic over the face.
- **One creative element**: the Running Scorecard system. Everything else is existing convention.
- **Flex to 4–6 findings**: five mapped; four = stretch bands 2–4 by ~15s; six = cap recipes at 55s. Only findings that actually occurred and cleared Gate 3 are ever shown.

### 2.4 Measurement plan (what makes this a test instead of a belief)

Pilot success is judged on: (1) relative retention at THE SCORECARD vs. the 0.83 prior; (2) AVD vs. the 8:59 suggested-feed benchmark; (3) CTR on the verdict-concealed thumbnail; (4) scorecard-PDF downloads and buyer-rep contacts attributed to the video. If the scorecard beat underperforms its prior badly, the Episode 2+ placement rule gets re-derived rather than defended (hypothesis, with its exit condition stated).

## 3. The verdict engine

Mirrors Taylor's published inspection-analysis standard — **severity + likely cost range + stop-the-deal flag** (fact: his stated rubric) — and stays entirely in the buyer lane: cash exposure and financing risk for an owner-occupant. No cap rates, no rental math, no return language anywhere in the series.

### 3.1 Scorecard categories (five, mapped from the real showing toolkit — fact)

| # | Category | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Foundation & Structure** | Foundation cracks at masonry base, stair-step brick cracking, slab cracks, roof sag read as a structural signal |
| 2 | **Roof & Envelope** | Patch scan, shingle condition, soffit/fascia/gutter rot, flashing |
| 3 | **Water & Drainage** | Grading toward slab, downspout discharge, splash erosion, staining at the slab line |
| 4 | **Big-Ticket Systems** | Water-heater age plate, HVAC age plate, electrical panel condition |
| 5 | **Loan-Killer Flags (VA/FHA MPR)** | Peeling paint pre-1978, missing handrails, broken glazing, inoperable systems — items that threaten Minimum Property Requirements and therefore the financing itself. In a heavy-VA corridor this category earns its own lane (decision, built on the toolkit fact) |

### 3.2 Severity scale

- **S1 — Note & monitor.** Age-appropriate wear. Logged, no ask generated. Showing S1s is deliberate: an analyst who logs non-issues as non-issues is believed when he logs an S3.
- **S2 — Budget item.** Real, priceable, negotiable. Requires a per-finding documented cost (Gate 2).
- **S3 — Stop-and-verify.** The stop-the-deal flag: no offer math is valid until a licensed inspector — and where indicated a structural engineer or licensed trade — scopes it. S3 is not "walk"; it is "verify before anything else is true."

### 3.3 The per-finding pipeline (severity → sourced cost → leverage)

1. **Severity on camera** with a one-line reason.
2. **Cost only with a document.** Source tag in the lower-third: `[inspection report]`, `[option-period quote]`, `[current quote — MM/YYYY]`, `[invoice]`, `[closing statement]`. Findings without a document run the severity-only lane with the no-document-no-number line. Total Repair Exposure (TRE) = sum of documented figures; where a source gives a range, its midpoint is used and that method is stated once on screen at THE SCORECARD.
3. **Leverage class per S2/S3** — R / C / P: **R**epair by seller pre-close (verified by re-inspection), **C**redit at close (lender caps checked), **P**rice reduction. Stated rules: documents are leverage, feelings are not; one bundled amendment, not a drip of asks; S1s never generate an ask.

### 3.4 Verdict thresholds — a disclosed rubric, not a law of nature

On camera, the thresholds are introduced exactly this way: **"These are my lines — the same lines every episode, so the verdicts mean something across houses. They're an agent's framework, not an inspection standard and not a rule about what you can afford."** (decision, labeled as such on screen)

| Verdict | Rule |
|---|---|
| **BUY** | TRE < 1.5% of purchase price AND zero S3 flags. Proceed on standard terms; a small credit ask is optional. |
| **NEGOTIATE** | TRE 1.5%–5.0%, OR any single documented finding ≥ 1.0% alone, OR an S3 that is scoped, quoted, and curable before closing. The engine produces a documented ask sized to the exposure. |
| **WALK** | TRE > 5.0%, OR an S3 that cannot be cured or verified before closing (active structural movement without an engineer letter, unscoped moisture intrusion), OR an MPR item the seller refuses to cure on a VA/FHA deal — the loan cannot close, so the deal cannot — OR access denied for verification. |

**Where the lines come from (stated honestly):** arithmetic anchors, not population statistics. On a ~$300K purchase, 1.5% ≈ $4,500 and 5% ≈ $15,000 (arithmetic). The judgment call — Taylor's, said as his — is that below the first line, grinding a negotiation over reserve-sized items risks the house for little gain, and above the second, the post-close exposure stacked on top of down payment and closing costs is where he starts telling his own buyers to reconsider. No claim is made about what "most buyers" hold in reserve or consider normal — those claims aren't sourced, so they don't appear. **Calibration pass (prep week):** run three additional past buyer-side files through the engine; if its verdict contradicts what actually happened in two or more, adjust the lines before filming and say so on camera. A rubric that survived a back-test is presented as exactly that — back-tested, not proven (hypothesis, named test).

**The client-decision rule (script-level, non-negotiable):** the engine never decides for the buyer. The on-camera framing at THE CALL: **"The scorecard hands my buyer a number and a recommendation. The decision is theirs — my job is that they make it with the full picture."** In the retrospective, both are shown: what the engine output, and what the buyer actually chose. If they differ, that is the story, not a problem. Overrides stated in one line each: an S3 can force WALK at any TRE; a seller cure, re-verified, can move WALK back to NEGOTIATE; the thresholds guide, the flags govern, the client decides.

### 3.5 On-screen artifacts + lead magnet mirror

- **Per-finding stamp (lower-third, ~4s):** `STRESS POINT #3 · WATER & DRAINAGE · S2 · $1,150–$1,600 [option-period quote]`
- **Corner counter:** `EXPOSURE: $6,450` — ticks on each stamp.
- **Tally card (THE SCORECARD):** five categories × (flags / max severity / documented exposure), TRE, % of price, verdict bar.

## 4. Packaging

### 4.0 Identity timing, reconciled once

Two rules touch the credential: the channel's entity-declaration convention (first three sentences) and the video canon (no self-intro before the hook; credential ~0:30, never inside 0:15 — fact, mandatory). Both are satisfied the same way: **every hook is built as exactly two spoken sentences, making the credential line the third spoken sentence, landing ~0:22–0:28** — after 0:15, at effectively 0:30, and inside the first three sentences. The description opens with the entity declaration in sentence one. This reconciliation is a QA line item, not an accident.

All bracketed values are slots filled from the chosen deal file; each hook carries its own verification notes and no video ships with an unverified slot or claim.

### 4.1 Hook variants (each = 2 sentences, ≤ ~16s spoken; formula: number/contradiction + who it's for + delayed payoff resolving at the 50–60% mark)

**H1 — Number-led:**
> "[$11,900] — that's what was wrong with this house when my buyer toured it, and [none] of it showed in the photos online. If you're buying in Temple, you're about to see all [six] flags in the order I caught them, and the scorecard that turns them into one decision: buy, negotiate, or walk."

*Formula: number = TRE; who = Temple buyers; payoff = the scorecard decision → resolves at THE SCORECARD (52–60%). Verification notes: TRE from the tally; "[none] showed in the photos" is used only after checking the actual listing photos against the finding list — if any finding is visible, the line becomes "almost none of it" or drops the clause.*

**H2 — Contradiction-led:**
> "This house appraised and closed on schedule — and it needed [$11,900] of work the day we toured it, and both of those things are true. If you're house hunting in Temple, I'll show you what a [forty]-minute walk actually catches, and the scorecard that told my buyer whether to buy, negotiate, or walk."

*Formula: contradiction = appraised-and-closed vs. documented repair exposure — a real, file-verifiable contradiction that also teaches the appraisal-is-not-an-inspection lesson; who = Temple house hunters; payoff = the scorecard → 52–60%. Verification notes: use only on a financed deal with an appraisal in file; on a cash deal, swap the first clause to "funded and closed on schedule." No claims about other buyers, other agents, or what anyone else failed to see.*

**H3 — Time-asymmetry, disclosure folded in:**
> "It took me about [forty] minutes to find [$11,900] in this house — and because my buyer owns it now, for once I can show you all of it. If you're buying in Temple: every flag from tour day, the real cost behind each one from the actual deal file, and at the end, the call — buy, negotiate, or walk."

*Formula: numbers = minutes-to-dollars asymmetry, both Taylor's own verifiable facts; who = Temple buyers; payoff = the call → verdict zone at 52–60%, outcome documents in THE RECEIPTS. Folding ownership into the hook makes it the strongest anti-staging choice. Verification notes: tour length from Taylor's calendar/notes; "all of it" is licensed by the release — if the owner's elections restrict price disclosure, the line becomes "almost all of it."*

Banned-language check on all three against the ground-truth list: clean. No dollar-volume claims. No claims about other agents or other buyers. "Agent" only.

## 5. Videographer shot architecture (the one pro day)

### 5.1 Per-finding shot recipe (solo-first by design)

Every stress point — pilot and future solo episodes — is the same seven shots. Pro day: shots 1–2 on A-cam while B-cam covers 3–4. Solo: 1–2 and 5 on tripod, 3–4 and 6–7 handheld pickups. Same structure, different labor; the format's look lives in the recipe, not the crew.

1. **Approach POV** — moving toward the zone, 5–8s.
2. **Context wide** — Taylor at the finding, spot line delivered ("Most people are looking at the kitchen; I'm looking at this."), 7–12s.
3. **Evidence macro** — crack, plate, rot; rack focus; 7–12s.
4. **Hands-on demonstrative** — tape across the crack, level app on the grade, flashlight in the panel; 7–12s.
5. **Severity MCU** — direct to camera: severity + sourced cost + leverage line; 7–12s.
6. **Stamp plate** — locked-off 4s hold for the stamp to land clean.
7. **Stills + vertical pair** — one still per finding (lead magnet, blog, GBP) and one native-vertical pickup for Shorts.

**Solo pre-validation (prep week, before the pro day):** Taylor shoots ONE full finding block at his own house with the tripod recipe. One hour of work converts "Episode 2 will look continuous" from an assertion into a checked box — if the solo recipe fails, it gets fixed while the fix is still free (hypothesis, tested before the non-recurring resource is spent).

### 5.2 Preconditions and the drone legal gate

- **No release, no shoot.** Signed release verified in hand before the videographer is booked; re-verified on arrival.
- **Drone gate (decision — compliance prudence):** aerial stills happen only if, before the day: the operator's FAA Part 107 certificate is current, the aircraft registration is current, and airspace for the specific address is checked and (if controlled) LAANC-authorized. The task's gear rule — aerial photos only until ND filters arrive in August (fact) — is an image-quality constraint layered on top, not a substitute for the legal gate. If any element is unverified, drone assets are cut; nothing in the format depends on them.
