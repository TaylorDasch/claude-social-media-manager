# CRITIQUE — Candidate A

Adversarial attack on the candidate. Cold-start, candidate-only view.

---

WEAKNESS-1 [FATAL]: **Anti-duplication frame collapses in execution.** The candidate brags in Section 8 that this is a "$300K across 4 places" comparison frame, "not a tour of one place." But the script body literally does exactly that — Section 0:30 tours The Groves, 1:15 tours Western Hills, 2:42 tours Mesa Ridge, 4:30 tours Three Creeks. Each segment introduces the neighborhood, names a builder, gives an address, lists a lower-third, and shows interior B-roll. That IS four single-subdivision tours stitched together with a price tag wrapper. The "same-price comparison" payoff only appears in the $/sqft chart at 5:15 — five minutes in, after the algorithm has already decided whether to keep retaining the viewer. The hook promised "four neighborhoods side-by-side at the same exact price" but the structure is sequential, not parallel. A genuine side-by-side frame would intercut: same kitchen angle in all four houses, same backyard pan, same commute clock. This script does not do that. It accidentally executes the very Canyon Ridge format it claims to differentiate from, only 4x.

WEAKNESS-2 [FATAL]: **Hook is data-recital, not a hook.** "Right now in Temple, $300,000 buys a 1,473 sqft new build — or a 2,450 sqft resale. Today I show you both, on-site." That is a thesis statement, not a hook. There is no curiosity gap, no stakes, no pattern interrupt, no "wait what." A real hook would be a contradiction or a number that breaks expectation cold ("$300,000 in Temple buys you a house your neighbor's kitchen window looks straight into" or "$300,000 in Temple has a 977-square-foot lie hiding in it"). The candidate's hook also commits the "today I show you" frame, which is a tour-vlog cliché that telegraphs the next 8 minutes to a sophisticated buyer. First 8 seconds will lose ~35-40% on this hook because it sounds like every other "what $300K buys in [city]" video on YouTube. The pricing-comparison genre is saturated — the candidate needs a hook that fights for the click, not a hook that confirms the title.

WEAKNESS-3 [MAJOR]: **The "Honest Moment" sounds genuinely honest but is structurally a softball.** Insurance costs are not Taylor's negative — they are Texas's negative. He's pinning a market-wide cost increase on the buyer's budgeting habits, which lets him look credible while telling them nothing about his services, his neighborhoods, or his market that's actually unflattering. A real honest moment in the BSW-buyer lane would be: "I tell BSW physicians not to buy in The Groves at Lakewood Ranch because the 0.10-acre lot resale value is going to compress in 2027 when the next 1,200 Centex doors come online" — that's a negative he owns. Or "Western Hills has appreciated 4% over 3 years versus Belton at 11% — if you care about appreciation, this is the wrong pocket." Insurance reality is a national-relocator-101 talking point that any agent can deliver. It's an "I'll be honest about Texas" moment, not an "I'll be honest about myself or my market" moment. Lower trust ROI than the candidate claims in Section 9.

WEAKNESS-4 [MAJOR]: **Comp-honesty problem at 2306 Healy.** The script claims "$279,990, 1,473 square feet, completed 2025. That's the cheapest 2025 new-build in the $275-325K tier on the MLS today." That is a strong claim that depends entirely on the MLS snapshot timestamp and is going to be wrong within days. The script anchors retention and authority on this single comp — and on the explicit "$190/sqft on Healy" math — but if 2306 Healy goes pending tonight (which is statistically likely for a $280K new build in a 77-DOM market that just had its median pulled down by sub-$300K closings), the entire hook is dead-on-arrival the moment a viewer clicks the link 2 days post-upload. There is no language hedging the comp ("at the time of this recording" appears only buried in the description, not in the spoken script). Worse, the $190/sqft figure is computed on a sub-1,500 sqft house where builder fixed costs (driveway, foundation, kitchen, two baths, garage) inflate $/sqft mechanically — that's not honest math, that's small-house math. The Sugar Brook comparison at $130/sqft on 2,450 sqft is partly an artifact of that same denominator effect. Taylor's voice rule is "data-first, honest"; this comp-spin violates it.

WEAKNESS-5 [MAJOR]: **Voice drift on "subscribe so you catch that one" + "see you on the next address" + "I'm not promising anything specific."** These are generic YouTuber/agent cadences. Taylor's voice is investor-analyst — closer to a research-note tone. "See you on the next address" is exactly the kind of cute closer his lane discipline rules call out as drift. Multiple lines read like a generic real estate channel host: "I drop one of these every couple weeks," "Now the chart you actually came here for," "Quick check," "Three quick comps so you can calibrate your own search." Compare against the candidate's stated voice anchor ("data-first, concise, honest"). The script has 9 minutes of mostly-good data interrupted by 8-10 lines of generic-host language that will pull regression viewers (relocators sophisticated enough to know the genre) out of the trance.

WEAKNESS-6 [MAJOR]: **CTA #1 at 2:30 — "Comment NEW or RESALE" — is the lowest-yield engagement format possible and consumes 12 seconds of retention at the highest-attrition moment of the video.** At 2:30, the viewer is on the edge of clicking off (industry standard 30-50% drop by minute 3). The script chooses that exact moment to introduce a binary comment prompt that 95% of viewers will ignore and that gives the algorithm nothing meaningful — false-positive engagement at best, attrition trigger at worst. A real retention rescue at 2:30 would be either a stakes-raise ("here's the one comp on this list that already had two offers this week") or a tease for the Honest Moment ("there's a $800/month line item nobody's talking about — I'll get to it at 6:15"). The candidate burns the 2:30 slot on a CTA that should have been at 5:30 or 8:00.

WEAKNESS-7 [MAJOR]: **Specificity is shallow on streets, restaurants, commute landmarks, parks.** The script names addresses (good) but barely names anything else. Western Hills gets "drive five minutes east" with no street axis (Adams? 31st? Avenue H?), no commute landmark ("five minutes east of what — the BSW main campus? the 35 interchange?"). Mesa Ridge gets "Pepper Creek Trail" and "South 31st commercial" but no specific restaurants, schools, parks, HEB. Three Creeks gets "Lake Belton six minutes away" but no specific lake-access point (Stillhouse Hollow? Belton Lake Outdoor Recreation Area? Westcliff Park? They are not the same drive). For a relocator deciding between four neighborhoods, the texture they actually need — "the closest HEB is 6 minutes," "Western Hills Park is at Sugar Brook and 13th," "Three Creeks kids go to Lakewood Elementary, here's the GreatSchools rating" — is missing. The candidate compensates with builder names and addresses, but those don't sell a *life*, they sell a *transaction*. Buyer-lane content needs life texture.

WEAKNESS-8 [MAJOR]: **Banned-word scan finds at least one direct hit and several near-misses.** "Established trees" appears twice. "Mature trees on the lot" at 3:30 appears in the Shadow Canyon segment — "mature" + "trees" is exactly the kind of generic real estate phrasing the candidate's voice rules avoid. "Real backyard" is filler. "Real trees" in the Western Hills segment is filler — what does "real" trees mean versus what other kind? "Established" appears five times across the script ("an established street," "established Temple location," "established neighborhoods," "established trees," "established resale"). Not technically on the banned list but a tell of generic-agent drift. The pinned comment uses "Live MLS data behind this video" — fine — but the description opens with "I put four neighborhoods side-by-side at the exact same price and showed the real square footage gap," which is brochure-voice. The voice rules specifically call out "no generic real estate fluff" and this language pattern is fluff-adjacent.

WEAKNESS-9 [MAJOR]: **Title CTR projection is weak versus realistic Temple TX competition.** Title 5 (baseline, "What $300,000 Actually Buys in Temple TX in 2026") will lose head-to-head to titles that already rank in the niche — competitors run formats like "I Was Wrong About Temple TX — $300K Reality Check," "$300K in Temple TX vs Belton — Honest Numbers," or "Why I'd Buy Belton Over Temple at $300K (and Why I'd Be Wrong)." The candidate's "Actually" is meant to carry honesty signal, but it's a worn-out clickbait token in 2026 — every "what $X buys in [city]" video has used it. None of the five title options name the BSW or military hooks that are this channel's actual differentiation. Title 2 ("1,473 sqft NEW or 2,450 sqft RESALE?") is the strongest variant but the candidate ranks it second to the bland baseline. The recommendation is risk-averse, not CTR-optimized.

WEAKNESS-10 [MAJOR]: **Description first 200 chars is a stat dump, not click bait.** "$300,000 in Temple TX, May 2026: 902 active listings, median sold $275K, and 62% of sales closing under $300K. I put four neighborhoods side-by-side at the exact same price and showed the real square footage gap." For a SERP preview, this opens with numbers that mean nothing to a cold relocator searching "moving to Temple TX." The "62% of sales closing under $300K" stat — meant to anchor relevance — instead reads as inside-baseball MLS-speak. Compare with what would actually pull a click in SERP: a contradiction ("$300K in Temple TX buys a 1,473 sqft new build — or a 2,450 sqft resale. Same price, 977 more square feet. Here's the gap."). The candidate has the right material in the hook but recycles the wrong opening into the description.

WEAKNESS-11 [MAJOR]: **60-second Short is a long-form chop-down, not a Short.** The cutdown plan in Section 6 takes the hook, the math chart, the insurance segment, AND the scorecard — four distinct payloads in 60 seconds. That's 15 seconds per payload, which is below the TikTok/Reels attention threshold of ~3 seconds per beat. Worse, the Short ends with "Full video and live list — link in bio. I'm Taylor" — that is a long-form sign-off pasted onto a Short. Real Shorts end with a question, a stakes-raise, or a "wait until you see [thing in long form]" trigger that pushes the click. The candidate's Short title — "$300K in Temple TX = 1,000 More Sqft? (here's the math)" — is closer to a long-form title than a vertical-video title. TikTok would punish this. Reels would punish this. The candidate confused "make a short version" with "edit the long form down."

WEAKNESS-12 [MAJOR]: **Three Creeks MUD warning is structurally invalidating, not just informative.** The script explicitly tells viewers Three Creeks has a MUD ("Your water-and-sewer bill is not the city's bill. You will see a separate MUD assessment on your property tax statement"), then puts Three Creeks on the final scorecard as the "WANT BELTON ISD + LAKE" recommendation. The honest move would be to recommend AGAINST Three Creeks for buyers who can't tolerate variable MUD assessments, or to recommend a Belton non-MUD alternative (which the script never identifies). Instead, the candidate flags the MUD risk and then implicitly tells the viewer to accept it — that's not honest negativity, that's bait-and-switch. The Honest Moment claim at Section 9 ("Honesty signal: without it, the video reads as a clean resale-wins-on-sqft victory") is undermined by the fact that the script still resolves Three Creeks as a positive recommendation despite the MUD.

WEAKNESS-13 [MINOR]: **BSW workaround segment at 2:00 is a non-sequitur drop-in.** The script names 1103 Ridgeview as "the BSW workaround under $300K," then never explains what neighborhood Ridgeview is in, what its build year or builder is (only "2007"), or how the BSW commute time is actually verified. It floats between Western Hills (1:15) and CTA #1 (2:30) without integrating into either. A BSW-physician viewer would want exactly this comp explained — how close to the OB unit, how close to Trauma, distance to St. Louis vs Memorial campuses — and gets 12 seconds of "$299,250, 2,226 sqft, near BSW, under three hundred" with no follow-up. Then the comp is reused in the final scorecard at 7:48 ("WANT BSW IN 5 MIN → Ridgeview Dr cul-de-sacs") with no further specificity. Wasted lane-defining asset.

WEAKNESS-14 [MINOR]: **Pinned comment leaks the BSW + Belton physicians who searched the description's #BSWHealth tag.** "Top 1.4% Bell County" is repeated three times in the package (intro, lower-third, pinned comment). The same authority claim three times reduces its impact — by the third repetition, a sophisticated relocator reads it as marketing. The pinned comment would carry more weight if it surfaced an unexpected data point ("of those 380 Temple closings the past 90 days, 71 were physicians or healthcare workers — BSW-relocation is a measurable cohort, not a marketing line") rather than restating credentials.

WEAKNESS-15 [MINOR]: **Insurance math has a verification gap baked into the script.** "Texas homeowner insurance on a $300,000 house in Bell County right now runs $2,800 to $3,800 a year." That's a $1,000 range on the line item that the script frames as the credibility-defining honest moment. A $1,000/year range is too wide to be authoritative — it means "I'm not sure." The relocator can't budget from "$250 to $320 a month" with confidence. If Taylor pulled a real quote from a real insurer for a real $300K house in 76502 in May 2026, the script should give the actual number ("State Farm quoted $3,140 on a $300K Western Hills resale on May 22") and then range up/down by 10%, not throw out a $1,000 spread.

WEAKNESS-16 [MINOR]: **"I drop one of these every couple weeks" is a frequency commitment the channel doesn't keep.** If the next video is "$400K in Temple TX" and the channel actually drops every 3-4 weeks (typical for solo-agent YouTube), this line creates a credibility-loss vector for repeat viewers. Better to say nothing about cadence or to say "I'm filming the $400K version next" without specifying timing.

VERDICT: The candidate dresses up four single-subdivision tours as a "side-by-side comparison" but the script structure is sequential, not parallel — collapsing the anti-duplication frame and turning the Canyon Ridge weakness into the Canyon Ridge weakness times four.
