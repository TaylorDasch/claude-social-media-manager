## 9. HONEST MOMENT — SELECTION AND DEFENSE

**Selected:** **Portal listing-price inflation gap.**

### Why this over the four alternatives

**Considered and rejected:**

- **Avenue H shift-change traffic spike** — Too micro-local. Burns 60 sec on something only relevant to two streets. Doesn't generalize across the four-house frame. Save for a BSW commute video.
- **Belton MUD reality** — Already covered organically in the Crimson Sky segment. Repeating it as the Honest Moment makes the video feel anti-Belton. Lane discipline says Temple vs Belton is fair, but a 60-sec dedicated Belton-negative tilts the video unfair.
- **New-construction 0.10-acre lot sizes** — Already shown visually at Healy Drive. Doesn't need a dedicated segment — the aerial shot at 0:45-2:30 carries it. Re-stating it as the Honest Moment is redundant.
- **Texas homeowner insurance reality ($2,800-$3,800/yr)** — Strong candidate. Real, undertaught, and high-stakes. Reason it loses: it doesn't tie to the *comparison frame* of this video. Insurance applies equally to all four houses, so it doesn't sharpen the buyer's decision *between* the comps. Better as its own video. Tease at the end: "Coming up — what your $300K Temple house actually costs to insure."

### Why portal listing-price inflation wins

1. **It directly serves the decision frame of the video.** Every viewer is going to leave this video and go look at Zillow. Inoculating them against the portal price gap *changes the action they take next*. The other negatives are situational; this one is universal to every viewer.

2. **It pairs with the verified MLS ground truth.** The data already exists in the script: $275K median sold vs ~$315K typical list. The 77-day DOM median (2x 2022). The MLS-vs-portal gap is the single most defensible, least-disputable claim Taylor can make. It cites a Bell County MLS snapshot dated 2026-05-25.

3. **It's the kind of insight a top-1.4% agent has that the average channel can't replicate.** Random YouTube agents don't have MLS access or aren't allowed to cite sold comps. Taylor can — and it's the moat. Honest Moments should always lean on *what only the seller of this content can prove*. Avenue H shift-change traffic? Anyone with a car can verify. MUD tax? Public record. Portal gap? Requires MLS access + the willingness to publish a number that hurts the portal traffic Taylor partially benefits from.

4. **It costs Taylor something to say.** A real Honest Moment has stakes. Naming the portal gap mildly antagonizes the platforms that send traffic. Saying "the portals are lying to you by omission" is the kind of line that earns trust *because* it's an unforced critique of a tool the viewer uses daily. That trust is the asset compounding for the channel.

5. **It generalizes across the channel.** Once the audience internalizes "MLS sold > portal list," every future video gets a free retention boost — they trust the comp framing on the next video before Taylor even pulls a number.

### Risk and mitigation

**Risk:** Portal companies don't love being called out. Practical impact on a 10K-sub channel: zero. Editorial right to cite public market data is clear. Mitigation: cite the source (Bell County MLS, 2026-05-25), use the word "lag" rather than "lying" if Taylor wants to soften — but the script intentionally keeps "lying to you by omission" because the line is honest and earns the trust premium.

**Risk:** Viewers feel the segment is a Trojan horse for `hire me`. Mitigation: the script explicitly invites the viewer to use *any local agent they trust*, not just Taylor. That phrasing is in the script verbatim. The CTA earned by trust shows up at 8:30 — not stapled onto the Honest Moment.
