## DESIGN NOTE — why this is a framework, not a tour

The viewer is never walked house-to-house. They answer **one question — "what's the one thing you won't give up?"** — and that single answer sorts them into one of four buying lanes. Each neighborhood appears only as the **answer to a lane**, never as a destination. Every lane carries the **same three numbers in the same order — price / square feet / dollars per square foot — held against the same ~$250,000 check** so the trade is mathematically visible, not asserted. The spine is the **Trade-Off Grid**, built one row at a time. The payoff is **not** a ranking and **not** a restatement — it's a single new cost number that flips the decision, then a flowchart that ties each lane to a real life-situation. Buyer-priority-first, not place-first: that inversion is the anti-duplication wedge (§6).

> Format keys: **[ON-SCREEN: …]** = lower-third / overlay text. **[B-ROLL: …]** = footage cue. **[BEAT @ x:xx]** = marked retention reset. **[GRID]** = the persistent Trade-Off Grid graphic, built one row at a time. Per §7, full-frame visuals are reserved for maps and data cards (the head-to-head showdown and the final grid earn full frame); everything else stays lower-third overlay. Voice = data-first, honest, no hype, Scars-and-All. Talking-head to camera unless a cue says otherwise.
