Five notes before you write an offer, with the math shown so you can re-run it:
(1) Returns below are unlevered yield-on-cost (NOI ÷ all-in), NOT a market cap rate.
(2) At the median (~$190K all-in, $1,000 rent), a bottoms-up expense load is ~64% — tax alone is ~$3,000 at a ~2% Bell County rate with no homestead — so NOI is ~$4,280 and yield-on-cost is ~2.3%. Finance it (~$150K loan at 7.5–8.5%) and you're cash-flow NEGATIVE, roughly −$700 to −$800/mo. That's the filter, not a reason to skip the area: it's why this works for cash/low-leverage buyers and not a financed median buyer.
(3) The real return here is a value-add / forced-equity play: buy ~$120K + ~$40K rehab against a ~$190K ARV creates ~$30K of equity (~19% of all-in) on day one; you can refi ~$142,500 back out (leaving ~$17,500 in the deal), and the tenant pays down your loan from there. Judge it on created equity + paydown + appreciation, not year-one yield. (Flip-to-sell instead? 70% rule: $190K ARV × 70% − $40K rehab = MAO ~$93K, below the median.)
(4) Furnished MTR — same below-median buy, ~$170K all-in, ~65% furnished expense load: at the MID of the band (~$1,550) yield-on-cost is ~3.8%, modestly ahead of a below-median LTR (~3.4%) and clearly ahead of the median LTR (~2.3%); near the TOP of the band (~$1,800) it's ~4.4% and clearly ahead. The top band is UNVERIFIED — confirm furnished rents before you furnish.
(5) Hire a structural engineer on any pier-and-beam home, not just a general inspector.
