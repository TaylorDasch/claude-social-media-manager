**[OPEN — 0:00–0:14 | CONTRADICTION HOOK]**

Round Rock has the lower property tax rate. So why does the same physical house cost you roughly the same in annual property tax in Round Rock as it does in Temple? And why is the biggest financial argument for families — bigger than the tax gap, bigger than the price gap — almost never mentioned in a Temple-vs-Round-Rock comparison?

If you're priced out of the Austin corridor and choosing between Temple and Round Rock, this is the seven-category breakdown — plus three things you probably haven't run the math on yet.

Note before Category One: every dollar figure in this video is a Q1 2026 baseline. The market shifts every thirty days. Confirm with your agent the morning you write your offer.

**[CATEGORY 1: PRICE — 0:50–2:15]**
[CUT TO: G-02 — side-by-side property card]

Median home price, Q1 2026. Round Rock between three-ninety and four-seventeen-thousand. Temple roughly two-eighty-five to two-ninety-nine. About a hundred-ten to a hundred-forty thousand dollar gap on equivalent square footage.

Cleaner version: price-per-square-foot. Round Rock around one-ninety-one. Temple around one-sixty-three. About seventeen percent more in Round Rock for the same physical structure.

Practical translation. Three hundred fifty thousand in Round Rock today buys a sixteen-to-eighteen-hundred square foot home from the eighties or nineties — almost always cosmetic update needed. Same three-fifty in Temple buys a twenty-one-to-twenty-four-hundred square foot newer build, often four-bed two-and-a-half-bath, west side. Same money, different houses.

**[CATEGORY 2: PROPERTY TAX — THE RATE TRAP — 2:15–3:50]**
[CUT TO: G-04 tax math callout]

The contradiction hook pays off here.

Effective property tax in Temple is roughly two-point-three-nine percent. Round Rock is roughly one-point-six-eight. Round Rock looks like the obvious winner. It is not.

Texas property tax is ad valorem — applied to assessed value. Round Rock's lower rate is being applied to a higher base.

The math, on a three-hundred-fifty-thousand-dollar primary residence with the new one-hundred-forty-thousand-dollar ISD homestead exemption from Texas Prop Thirteen. Temple: about six thousand seven hundred sixty-four a year, around five-sixty-three a month in escrow. Round Rock, same three-fifty: about forty-six twenty-five a year, three-eighty-five a month. Round Rock genuinely wins on a three-fifty home.

Here's the trap. The same physical house — same square footage, same build year, same finishes — costs about four-fifty in Round Rock. Annual tax bill on that four-fifty Round Rock home is about sixty-four-fifty. Almost identical to Temple's six-seven-sixty-four on the three-fifty equivalent.

For an equivalent house, the absolute dollar tax bills are roughly the same. The lower rate myth costs Round Rock buyers a hundred-thousand-plus in extra principal AND identical tax dollars. That's the trap.

[CUT TO: G-05 MUD warning callout]

Both markets also have MUDs and PIDs. Bell County MUD One: point-seven-eight percent. River Farm MUD One: one full point. Round Rock's Brushy Creek MUD: about point-four-three. Some newer Williamson County subdivisions push another full one-point-four. Two identical houses on the same street can carry monthly payments differing by hundreds based on which side of a MUD line. Always verify the tax address before writing an offer.

**[CATEGORY 3: COMMUTE — 3:50–5:20]**
[CUT TO: G-06 commute time map]

Round Rock is fifteen to twenty miles north of downtown Austin. Temple is sixty-five miles north. Different commuters.

Round Rock to downtown Austin during morning rush: forty-five to sixty minutes most days. Reverse commute heading north out of Austin off-peak: twenty-five minutes. Job in downtown Austin, North Austin tech, the Domain, Apple Parmer, Samsung — Round Rock is the right answer. Period.

Temple to downtown Austin off-peak: forty-five minutes. Morning rush, with I-35 reconstruction zones around Georgetown: seventy-five-plus minutes. Daily downtown-Austin commute from Temple is not realistic.

Where Temple wins: BSW Temple Medical Center is under fifteen minutes from anywhere in town. Round Rock to BSW Temple: forty-five to sixty minutes one way. Fort Hood is thirty miles from Temple, fifty-five from Round Rock. Temple is a viable Fort Hood commute. Round Rock is not.

Hybrid case. Two seventy-minute Temple drives a week is one-forty windshield minutes. Five forty-five-minute Round Rock drives is two-twenty-five. Less weekly windshield time from Temple if you're hybrid one to two days a week.

Verify your specific Google Maps drive time at your specific commute window. These averages move with construction.

**[CATEGORY 4: SCHOOLS — 5:40–7:15]**
[CUT TO: G-07 TEA rating callout]

Hardest category to talk about cleanly because there's a real gap.

Texas Education Agency 2025 ratings. Round Rock ISD: B with a score of eighty-seven. Seventy-two percent of RRISD campuses got an A or B. Cactus Ranch Elementary is ranked tenth in the state. Canyon Vista Middle is top twenty-five. RRISD is empirically one of the strongest public districts in Central Texas — that's the data, not marketing.

Temple ISD: C with a score of seventy-seven. Eight-point year-over-year improvement, real momentum, but still a C.

What most agents won't tell you. The west side of Temple — the high-growth corridor where most new builds happen — is zoned into Belton ISD, not Temple ISD. Belton ISD got a B. So if schools are non-negotiable and you're shopping Temple, filter your home search by Belton ISD attendance zones on the west side.

Verify Belton ISD attendance zone on the specific home you offer on. Boundaries shift. Builder marketing materials sometimes claim ISD zoning the actual address doesn't have.

If your kids are middle or high school and ranking is the deciding factor: RRISD wins outright. If you're flexible on which side of Temple and you target Belton ISD: you close most of that gap without the Round Rock price tag.

**[CATEGORY 5: LIFESTYLE — LEAD WITH THE NUMBER NOBODY RUNS — 7:15–9:15]**
[CUT TO: G-08 childcare cost callout]

The financial argument bigger than tax, bigger than the price gap, that almost no comparison video covers honestly: childcare.

Texas state average for full-time infant care is eight-seventy-seven to nine-seventy-five a month. In the Round Rock and North Austin metro, real infant care is twelve-thirty to fourteen-fifty a month — sometimes three-fifty a week at higher-end centers. In Temple, you're tracking close to the state average — call it nine hundred a month for a comparable program.

Run the math. Three-hundred to five-hundred dollars a month difference per child. Over two years of full-time infant care, that's fifteen to twenty thousand dollars more in Round Rock than Temple. Per child. For two kids in daycare — common in dual-income relocations — thirty to forty thousand dollars over two years.

Compare to the median price gap of a hundred-twenty-thousand on the house. Childcare alone closes a third of that gap before you've paid your mortgage for a year.

Verify by quoting three centers in each market for your kid's specific age range. Daycare pricing is hyper-local and high-end Round Rock centers have waiting lists that affect what you actually pay.

That's the financial reality. Now the honest amenities tradeoff.

[CUT TO: drone B-roll Round Rock — Premium Outlets, downtown]

Round Rock wins on amenities, and let me make this concrete. **If you're a household that eats out three nights a week, your dining-and-walkable-retail option pool in Round Rock is something like four-to-five times deeper than Temple's right now.** Temple's downtown revival is real but it's a five-year project, not a today reality. Round Rock has Premium Outlets, IKEA, Round Rock Express stadium, dense corporate dining, breweries, the Domain twenty minutes south. Big-suburb amenities, walkable retail, more nightlife. If that's how you spend your time and your money, Round Rock is the right answer.

**[HONEST RR WINS — RR-BASED COMMUTER, AUDIENCE-NEUTRAL]**

The other honest Round Rock win. If your job requires you to be physically at a Round Rock or North Austin tech campus five days a week — Dell, Apple Parmer, Samsung, the Domain — your commute math from Temple is sixty-plus extra hours a year in the car compared to commuting from Round Rock. That's a real cost. Burnout, missed family dinners, fuel and wear on the vehicle. I cannot honestly tell that buyer to live in Temple. The math doesn't pencil. Round Rock wins for that profile, full stop.

[CUT TO: drone B-roll Belton Lake]

Temple is quieter. The 2026 city budget allocated twenty and a half million dollars to parks and recreation. Belton Lake is fifteen minutes from anywhere — boating, fishing, hiking. Real downtown revival on Adams Avenue and around the Train Depot district. Family-oriented, slower-paced, more outdoors than retail.

Lifestyle isn't a trump card. Both math directions are real money — childcare in Temple's favor, dining-density and full-time-tech-commuter math in Round Rock's. Don't skip past either.

**[CATEGORY 6: INVENTORY + MARKET TRENDS — 9:15–10:50]**
[CUT TO: G-09 DOM / sale-to-list / months supply table]

Both markets normalized. Not 2021.

Days on market: Temple averaging eighty-six to a hundred-ten days. Round Rock at eighty-seven to ninety-six. Both give you time to inspect, negotiate, not waive contingencies.

Sale-to-list ratios. Temple at ninety-seven-point-four to ninety-eight-point-four percent. Round Rock at ninety-six-point-nine to ninety-seven-point-six. Both negotiating successfully under list. Neither overheated.

Inventory. Round Rock at three-point-six to four months of supply — leaning seller-favorable but not aggressively. Temple has over a thousand active listings city-wide and deeper buyer optionality.

Forward look — and this is where these markets diverge.

[CUT TO: G-10 — $1.5B data center pipeline graphic]

Round Rock is land-constrained. Rezoning downtown for vertical because they're running out of greenfield. Municipal future is densification.

Temple is the opposite. About one-and-a-half billion dollars of confirmed data center capital under construction. Rowan Digital's three-hundred-megawatt facility on a seven-hundred-acre South Temple site, serving global hyperscalers by twenty-twenty-seven. Meta's data center campus, eight hundred million dollars, over seven-hundred-fifty thousand square feet under construction.

Why does this matter for residential buyers? Data centers are commercial property tax payers. They drop massive commercial revenue into the city's general fund without adding traffic, without adding strain on schools, and without competing for residential housing.

Temple's commercial-to-residential tax base ratio over the next decade — driven by these projects — is going to shift meaningfully toward commercial. That's directionally important: it means the city has alternatives to raising residential rates as it grows. Round Rock's tax-base future is more dependent on residential growth, which is land-constrained.

I'm not telling you Temple's rate drops next year. I'm saying the structural forces over a ten-year hold favor Temple residential carriers. If you're buying a primary residence and planning to live there past 2030, that's a real factor — even if you can't put an exact dollar on it.

**[CATEGORY 7: BUYER PROFILE — 10:50–12:35]**
[CUT TO: G-11 — 5-profile decision matrix graphic]

Closing position. Five profiles, sharp verdicts.

**One.** Daily downtown-Austin commuter, OR full-time Round-Rock-tech-campus commuter (Dell-engineer-style, five days a week, no flexibility). **Round Rock. Don't even consider Temple.** The commute will eat you. Round Rock wins outright on commute alone.

**Two.** Software engineer or tech worker with elementary or middle-school kids and Round Rock ISD's rating is your line in the sand. **Round Rock.** RRISD depth is real, top campuses nationally ranked. Pay the premium, accept the smaller house, accept the higher childcare, get the schools.

**Three.** Remote-first, hybrid one-or-two-days-a-week, or fully remote tech worker — with or without kids. **Temple.** Hundred-twenty-thousand-plus in equity saved on the house. Fifteen-to-twenty-thousand-plus per child saved on daycare. **The real tradeoff: Temple's restaurant scene is fifteen years behind Round Rock's right now and you'll feel that on Friday nights for the first year. The downtown revival is real but it's not yet the version that matches Round Rock's Domain proximity. That's a genuine cost. The math still holds — but go in with eyes open.**

**Four.** Baylor Scott and White clinician relocating, OR Fort Hood military or military spouse. **Temple.** Not close. BSW from Round Rock is a two-hour-daily commute and you don't get Level One trauma access from Round Rock. Fort Hood from Round Rock is fifty-five miles. Don't even consider it.

**Five.** Single, no kids, want denser dining, walkable nightlife, suburban density. **Round Rock.** Temple is family-oriented and quieter — that's a mismatch. Pay the premium.

If you fall between two profiles — say a hybrid SWE with kids — weigh: childcare savings + house equity (Temple) versus school ranking + amenity density (Round Rock). For most families on a four-hundred-thousand budget the delta closes harder toward Temple than people expect — but Round Rock ISD is real authority. Don't let me tell you to sacrifice your kid's education to save twenty thousand a year in daycare. Run your own math, on your own kids, on your own commute.
