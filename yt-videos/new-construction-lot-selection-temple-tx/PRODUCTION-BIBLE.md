## 2. Mission Lock

- **One viewer:** a Temple-area buyer about to reserve or compare a new-construction lot.
- **One decision:** which exact lot fits the buyer's stated priorities, and which material facts still need proof before reservation.
- **One proof:** Taylor applies the same five checks to three real Temple-area lots using one fixed floor plan/confirmed placement, then shows all A/B/C outcomes after every check.
- **Business outcome:** direct `LOT` conversations with buyers at the highest-intent pre-reservation moment.
- **Distinct job:** Taylor's published videos explain builders, models, communities, new versus resale, agents, costs, and general mistakes. This episode isolates the permanent parcel decision.
- **Non-goals:** builder ranking, engineering diagnosis, legal opinion, insurance conclusion, tax quote, current availability, current premium, promotion, or universal winner.

## 3. The Editorial Thesis

> You can change flooring. You can add cabinets. You cannot move the lot.

The sales process naturally makes the house visually dominant. Taylor slows the decision down and compares the part buyers cannot take with them: the exact parcel and what the documents say around it.

This is not “what builders hide.” It is “what a buyer should independently verify.”

## 4. Buyer Profile Used for the Demonstration

The score only makes sense against stated preferences. Use this explicitly synthetic/composite profile. Do not imply that it describes a current client, and do not infer preferences from any protected characteristic:

- Wants a genuinely usable backyard, not just a large square-foot number.
- Values afternoon shade on the main outdoor sitting area.
- Prefers less cut-through traffic and less direct rear-window exposure.
- Wants room for a future shed or small pool but has not confirmed either is allowed.
- Will not accept an unresolved drainage question before reservation.

The comparison also requires one fixed floor plan and confirmed builder plot/site plan on all three lots. If the builder can mirror or reposition the plan, use only the confirmed placement. Otherwise, orientation, yard-envelope, and sightline comparisons are not valid.

Offer the same five-check method and the same access to information consistently. Say on camera: `This is a fit test for these priorities, not a universal ranking of lots.`

## 5. Five-Check Lot Scorecard

| Check | What Taylor observes | What Taylor verifies | Stop line |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Water and grade questions | Location of visible grading, swales, curbs, inlets, neighboring grade, and low points | FEMA is mapped-hazard screening; approved drainage documents show intended design; civil/project engineer addresses grading/design adequacy; an inspector reports observable conditions only within scope | Never diagnose drainage from appearance; the builder supplies records/representations but does not independently clear them; Zone X does not mean zero risk |
| 2. Orientation and daily exposure | Compass direction of patio, primary windows, garage, and main living side | Plat/site-plan north arrow plus confirmed floor-plan placement or written mirror/reposition option; phone compass is secondary | Say `more favorable orientation for the shade goal`, not guaranteed shade or energy savings |
| 3. Documented constraints and apparent remaining area | Shape, shown building lines, known corridors, boxes/inlets, and apparent area around the confirmed placement | Before reservation: phase plat, exact builder plot/site plan, recorded restrictions, written builder answers. Later: title commitment/final survey when issued | Overlay label: `ILLUSTRATIVE - NOT A SURVEY, SITE PLAN, OR BUILDABILITY APPROVAL`; never place a hypothetical pool/shed footprint |
| 4. Street position and observed access friction | Entrance route, collector position, corner turn, mailbox/trash pattern, current construction route | Date/time-labeled observations; repeat only if this factor matters | Not a traffic study or normal-condition guarantee; no crime, safety, household, or demographic claims |
| 5. Future edge, adjacency, and privacy fit | Vacant land, next phase, road stub, drainage/public-space edge, sightlines | Classify each source as recorded final plat, approved preliminary plat, pending application, adopted future-land-use/thoroughfare plan, zoning designation, or recorded easement/restriction; add issuer/status/date | Never promise permanence; plans and controlling documents may be amended; do not claim a view Taylor cannot lawfully verify |

### Two separate score rows

- **Buyer fit:** `FITS` / `TRADEOFF` / `CONFLICTS`.
- **Proof status:** `VERIFIED` / `NEEDS DOCUMENT` / `NEEDS PROFESSIONAL`.

Taylor can decide that an orientation fits a preference. He does not visually `pass` drainage, title, legal effect, insurance, tax, soil, or buildability. Do not total arbitrary points into a fake scientific score. A missing must-verify fact can override several preferences.

## 9. Proof Standard

Every A/B/C result gets at least one of these:

1. **Field receipt:** a safe, date-stamped observation from a public right-of-way or permitted location.
2. **Document receipt:** recorded phase plat, builder plot/site plan, restriction, later-issued survey/title exception, or applicable official record with personal information redacted.
3. **Official map receipt:** the current applicable FEMA, city, or county source with date visible; additional sources appear only if decisive to an actual lot.
4. **Professional handoff:** a clearly worded question for the inspector, civil engineer, title company, attorney, insurer, lender, provider, or city—not Taylor's diagnosis.

## 10. Production Design

### A-roll

- Hook and final CTA: Taylor on the subdivision edge from a lawful location; record both last.
- Check explanations: voiceover or one desk/parked-car setup.
- Never enter an active construction zone without written permission and required PPE.
- Do not record or publish intelligible third-party conversations; avoid minors; strip geotags and browser/map coordinates.
- Drone footage is optional, not required. If used, verify Part 107, registration/Remote ID, airspace, lawful takeoff/landing, and operations-over-people compliance.

### Field proof

- Three lots should be comparable enough that the parcel decision—not three unrelated price points—drives the result.
- Prefer one subdivision or builder phase and one fixed floor plan/confirmed placement for tighter controls.
- Film six core receipts per lot: wide; curb/grade; combined side/rear; compass/placement; infrastructure; and access/future edge.
- Name the subdivision only with permission and when doing so materially preserves local proof. Withhold active lot identifiers.
- Blur exact lot numbers, house/street signs, license plates, faces, workers, buyers, unique document identifiers, QR/barcodes, and private paperwork. Recognize that road geometry and neighboring homes can still reidentify a lot.

### Safe solo fallback

If three complete cases cannot be secured, do not claim a three-lot field audit. Convert the episode into one permitted real-lot investigation and rewrite the title, hook, thumbnail, and proof promise. Do not trespass for the sake of the format.
