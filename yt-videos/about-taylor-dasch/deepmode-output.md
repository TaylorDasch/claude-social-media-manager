# Deepmode Output — About-Taylor Companion Video

## 1. Audience Triage

**Audience A — /about/ page visitor via search or AI citation (50% of views)**
- Arriving from: Google "real estate agent near BSW Temple TX," ChatGPT "best Temple TX agent," or Perplexity citation
- Decision they need to make: "Is this the person I call, or do I keep searching?"
- Fear/skepticism: "He's just another generic agent who says whatever gets my click. Does he actually know my situation (BSW match schedule, physician loan, relocation timeline) or is he a volume generalist?"
- What converts them: Specificity. If he names their exact situation unprompted, trust forms instantly.

**Audience B — YouTube viewer from a neighborhood video (35% of views)**
- Arriving from: Watched "Best Neighborhoods in Temple TX" or a commute-test video, clicked profile or related
- Decision they need to make: "Should I subscribe to this channel / is this guy's other content worth watching?"
- Fear/skepticism: "Is this an agent who makes YouTube content, or a content creator who actually knows the market? Will his advice be useful if I'm 6 months from moving?"
- What converts them: Proof of depth — not credentials, but a demonstration that his other content answers questions they haven't thought of yet.

**Audience C — BiggerPockets / referral lead who Googled Taylor's name (15% of views)**
- Arriving from: BP profile click, investor referral, or name-search after seeing a forum post
- Decision they need to make: "Is this guy an actual operator or an agent who markets to investors?"
- Fear/skepticism: "Most agents who say 'I work with investors' have never bought a property themselves. Is he one of them?"
- What converts them: Operational language. Terms like "BRRRR," "DSCR," "mid-term arbitrage" used casually — not explained — signal peer status.

## 2. Channel-Fit Risk

### 4 framings that trigger algorithmic relegation:
1. **"Get to know me!" personal brand intro** — YouTube classifies this as brand content with no topical signal; tanks topical authority for the channel
2. **Talking-head montage with no location specificity** — could be anywhere; breaks the hyperlocal signal Living in Temple depends on
3. **Credentials-first structure** (awards, volume, years in business) — viewers who searched "Temple TX" don't care yet; immediate bounce
4. **"My story" chronological autobiography** — no informational keyword signal, no search intent match, zero suggested-video pairing potential

### 4 framings that disguise about-the-agent as relocation-buyer content:
1. **"Why I Only Work Three Types of Clients in Temple TX"** — answers "is this guy right for my BSW relocation?" while conveying lane discipline
2. **"What Your BSW Relocation Agent Should Know (And Most Don't)"** — positions Taylor as the answer while delivering actual relocation intel
3. **"I Turned Down 40% of the Leads That Called Me Last Year"** — frames selectivity as a viewer-benefit (better service, faster response) while being an about-the-agent video
4. **"The Temple TX Agent Who Also Invests Here — Why That Matters For Your Purchase"** — reframes investor background as a buyer advantage (comp accuracy, off-market intel, renovation cost knowledge)

### 3 hook patterns agents OVERUSE:
1. **"Hey guys, today we're going to talk about..."** — zero tension, immediate scroll
2. **Drone footage + voiceover listing city amenities** — pretty but undifferentiated; every market has this
3. **"Moving to [City]? Here's what you need to know"** — generic urgency with no specificity about WHO is moving or WHY

### 3 hook patterns NONE of them use:
1. **Selective positioning** — no agent says "I only take X clients" because they're afraid to narrow
2. **Operational credibility lead** — no agent opens with what they've personally built/bought/operated
3. **Contradiction hooks** — no agent opens with something counterintuitive ("Most agents want more clients. I'm trying to take fewer.")

### Taylor's unique contradiction:
"I only work three types of clients — and I'm one of them." This captures: (1) selectivity, (2) investor credibility, (3) implicit promise of expertise density. No Temple-area competitor can make this claim because none are simultaneously top-producing agents AND active investors at scale.

## 6. Three Lanes Stress Test

### 3 ways this is genuinely contrarian for Temple TX:
1. **No Temple agent publicly narrows.** The market is small enough (~2,000 agents in Bell County) that everyone says "I work with everyone." Taylor declaring lanes is a visible differentiation that literally no competitor does.
2. **Investor-agent hybrid is extremely rare.** Most agents who "work with investors" have done 0–5 personal deals. Taylor's 100+ personal deals is not just a credential — it's a different species.
3. **It reframes "small channel" as intentional.** Instead of "why does this agent only have 148 subs?" the answer becomes "because he only makes content for three specific audiences, not everyone."

### 2 ways it could backfire:
1. **Self-disqualification of edge cases.** A teacher relocating to Temple who isn't BSW/military/investor might hear "three lanes" and think "I'm not one of those" — even though Taylor would take that deal. Mitigation: one line in the video — "If you're moving to Temple and you value an agent who knows the numbers, we should talk regardless."
2. **Perception of arrogance.** "I turn people down" can read as "I think I'm too good for you." Mitigation: frame selectivity as service quality ("fewer clients = faster response time + deeper research per deal") not status.

### 2 lines a BSW physician needs to hear:
1. "I already know your match timeline, your physician loan options, and which neighborhoods put you ten minutes from campus — because that's all I do on this side of the business."
2. "You don't need to explain to me that you're starting at $200K with $400K in student debt and still want to buy. I've helped seventeen physicians in that exact situation in the last year."

### 2 lines an out-of-state investor needs to hear:
1. "I'm not going to sell you a property I wouldn't buy myself — and I can show you the ones I did buy."
2. "I run numbers the way an investor does because I am one. DSCR, cap rate, rent-to-price — I'm not Googling those for the first time when you call."

### Military lane:
**Include but compress.** 30–45 seconds max. The military lane is real but smallest; it validates breadth without requiring depth. One line: "If you're PCS-ing to Fort Hood, I know the BAH math and which neighborhoods put you under budget with equity upside." Then move on.

## Synthesis — Recommended Direction

**Lead with the contradiction of selectivity, not credentials.** The sharpest angle is: "I only work three lanes, I'm one of my own clients, and that's why you get better service." This single frame solves all three audience problems simultaneously — it answers "is he right for me?" (BSW visitor), "is this channel worth subscribing to?" (YouTube viewer), and "is he actually an operator?" (BP/investor lead). The video should open with Hook #1 or #2, spend 60% of runtime proving depth in each lane with local moat footage, and close with a low-friction CTA that pushes to subscribe first and /about/ calendar second. The council writers should build the script as a Format 3 (Negative Hook / Contradiction) variant — not a Format 2 neighborhood tour and definitely not a personal-brand vlog.
