**[0:35–0:55 — THESIS + RETENTION PROMISE. LOWER-THIRD: "Healthcare-first retirement."]**
Here's the thesis. Temple has one of the strongest healthcare-to-cost combinations in Central Texas — and the reason you've rarely heard it framed that way is simple: Temple is a working medical city, not a brand-built retirement town. That's not a knock. That's the whole point. I'll walk you through the healthcare, the taxes, the real cost, and the neighborhoods — and at the end, the honest list of who should *not* move here. Numbers first.

**[0:55–1:52 — HEALTHCARE. B-roll: BSW exterior, then Olin E. Teague VA. GRAPHIC: "Level I trauma center · ~636 beds (verify current).":]**
Start with the thing that quietly matters most as you age: healthcare. Because here's the real fear nobody says out loud — it's not the golf course, it's what happens when something goes wrong at 2 AM. Baylor Scott & White here in Temple is a Level I trauma center — by their designation, the only one on I-35 between Dallas and Austin — with somewhere around 636 beds and more than thirty specialties on one campus. Confirm the current numbers for yourself, but the scale is real: this is the regional hub people drive *to* for care.

[Beat — graphic to talking head.] And that depth changes the math of getting older. Temple isn't a place where your specialist is two hours away — the specialists are *here*, because the patients come here. How fast you actually get seen still depends on your insurance, your referral, and the specialty, so call your own doctors before you move. But for a city this size, that coverage is genuinely unusual.

[B-roll: VA medical center.] And if you're a military retiree, there's a full VA hospital right in town — the Olin E. Teague Veterans' Medical Center. Having both the VA and Baylor Scott & White in one mid-sized city is rare — and for a lot of veterans, that dual access is the whole reason they even look at Temple.

**[1:52–2:12 — PULL-QUOTE. Talking head, direct, slower.]**
Here's how I'd sum up everything I've watched happen here: most retirees who land in Temple came for the hospital and stayed for the cost of living. The ones who leave came for the cost of living and left because of the boredom. That one sentence is basically the whole decision. Hold onto it.

**[2:12–3:12 — MONEY: TAXES. GRAPHIC: "Over-65 school-tax freeze (verify current rules).":]**
Now the money, because Texas does two things most states don't. First — no state income tax. Your Social Security, your pension, your 401(k) withdrawals — none of it gets taxed at the state level. If you're moving from a state with a 5% income tax and drawing around 60 grand a year, that's roughly three thousand dollars a year you keep. From California, it can be over five.

[Beat.] Second — and most people don't learn this until *after* they buy — the over-65 school-tax freeze. The year you turn 65 and file the over-65 homestead exemption with the appraisal district, your school district taxes freeze at the dollar amount you owed — and they stay frozen even as your home appreciates. There are additional over-65 exemptions stacked on top. I'm not going to put an exact savings figure on camera, because it depends on your tax rate, your home's value, and the current exemption amounts — and those change. What I'll tell you is it's meaningful, and you should run your real numbers with Bell County's appraisal district.

[Lower-third: "Don't miss the deadline."] And here's the part almost everybody misses: that freeze can transfer if you move within Texas — but it's proportional, not dollar-for-dollar, and there's a filing deadline. Don't trust a video for the exact form. Ask the appraisal district which certificate to file, and file it the year you move — or you can lose the benefit entirely.

**[3:12–3:57 — MONEY: COST + COMPARISON. GRAPHIC: comparison card "Temple vs Georgetown (verify medians).":]**
Now stack that on top of home prices, and the price gap becomes hard to ignore. The median home in Temple has been running around 255 thousand; Georgetown's closer to 425; Fredericksburg is higher still. A median isn't the same house, so compare it honestly — age, condition, lot, HOA, tax district. But in the same budget, you typically get more home in Temple — *and* you've got that Level I trauma center in town. For Level I adult trauma, Georgetown is tied into the Austin and Round Rock network up the road.

[Beat.] A retired couple's monthly cost here lands in a wide range depending on whether you carry a mortgage, but it's well below the Austin metro. Build your own budget from the real line items — payment or cash, taxes, insurance, HOA, utilities, healthcare, transportation — and verify the medians the month you actually shop, because they move.

**[3:57–4:57 — HOUSING. B-roll: slow drives through newer single-story streets; cut to an older home with a tarped roof if available.]**
So what do people actually buy? Here's the pattern I see constantly. Folks come in wanting acreage — a ranch-style place with land. Then, once roof, foundation, and HVAC costs enter the math, they tend to narrow toward newer single-story homes instead. On a fixed income, a fifteen-thousand-dollar foundation repair or a twelve-thousand-dollar roof is a budget-breaking event — and newer construction, say 2018 and later, comes with builder warranties, modern HVAC, and single-story layouts built for accessibility.

[Beat — talking head.] I'm not going to tell you which neighborhood fits *you* based on your age — that's your call, against real criteria. The criteria that matter for most retirees: distance to the hospital, single-story inventory, HOA cost, lot size, the age of the home's systems, and trail or lake access. Temple has several newer communities to weigh against those — Bella Terra and Lake Pointe lean newer, lower-maintenance, and close to the hospital; Three Creeks adds Belton Lake trails; Canyon Creek trades newer construction for bigger lots and mature trees. Match the home to your criteria — not to your age bracket.

**[4:57–6:12 — THE HONEST NEGATIVE (the promised payoff). Talking head, serious. LOWER-THIRD: "Who should NOT retire here.":]**
Okay — the part I promised at the top. Here's the one reason you might pick Georgetown over Temple, and then a few smaller catches.

[Beat — number card "THE BIG ONE" on screen.] The big one: Temple does not have a Sun City. There is no large 55-plus, gated, golf-cart, activities-director community here — there's exactly one 55-plus apartment community, and that's it. So if that resort lifestyle is what you're picturing — the amenity center, the organized calendar, the golf cart to your neighbor's door — then Georgetown is genuinely the better call. That's the one. I'd rather you hear it from me now than figure it out after you've already moved.

[Number card "and a few smaller catches".] Now the smaller catches. The summers are brutal — mid-to-upper 90s, June through August, a stretch of days over a hundred, and your summer electric bill climbs; plan your outdoor life around October through May. This is a car-dependent city — the local micro-transit is limited and there's no reliable rideshare like Austin, so if you expect to lean on transit or rideshare down the road, test it before you buy: doctor visits, groceries, church, airport runs, the late-night backup ride. And there's no continuing-care retirement community — no single campus that carries you from independent living through memory care; the nearest ones are in the Austin metro, which matters most if you're retiring solo and planning for escalating care.

**[6:12–7:07 — TAYLOR'S TAKE. Talking head, personal, warmer.]**
Let me make this concrete. My own parents are looking at retiring here, from Mansfield. What I tell them is exactly what I'll tell you: it's not as upscale as where they live now — but the money goes dramatically further. A 350-thousand-dollar home here buys what 550-plus gets you in Mansfield, and the healthcare is comparable or better, because Baylor Scott & White is a Level I trauma center. The tradeoff is dining and entertainment — you'll drive to Austin, about an hour, when you want a nice dinner or a show. That's the honest deal.

[Beat.] And the real reason most people land here? Family. A lot of folks come to be near kids and grandkids who already work at Baylor Scott & White or out at Fort Cavazos. Close enough to visit daily, affordable enough to buy outright or with a small mortgage, and the hospital's right there for peace of mind.

Figure out which one you are before you start looking — that single decision saves you a year of regret.
