## FULL WORD-FOR-WORD SCRIPT (~1,380 words / ~8.5 min)

**[0:00-0:16 — HOOK. Talking head, no name. Visual change ~7s: cut from face → quick Georgetown Sun City stock → back to face.]**
Every retirement guide in Texas sends you to Georgetown's Sun City. I want to make the case for the city about 35 minutes north that nobody markets to retirees — Temple — and then I'll tell you exactly who should ignore me and go to Georgetown anyway.

**[0:16-0:34 — Identity + frame (now outside 15s). B-roll: BSW campus exterior.]**
I'm Taylor Dasch with EG Realty. I've closed over $28 million in transactions here in Temple and Belton, and I work with retirees and downsizers most weeks of the year. So this isn't a brochure. It's the honest version — what's strong, what's missing, and who Temple is genuinely wrong for.

**[0:34-0:54 — Thesis. LOWER-THIRD: "Healthcare-first retirement."]**
Here's my thesis. Temple has one of the strongest healthcare-to-cost combinations in Central Texas — and the reason you've rarely heard it framed that way is that Temple is a working medical city, not a brand-built retirement town. That's actually the whole point. Let me walk you through it, numbers first.

**[0:54-1:50 — HEALTHCARE. B-roll: BSW, VA hospital. GRAPHIC: "Level I trauma center · ~636 beds (verify)."]**
Start with the thing that quietly matters most as you age: healthcare. Baylor Scott & White in Temple is a Level I trauma center — by their designation, the only one on I-35 between Dallas and Austin — with somewhere around 636 beds and more than thirty specialties on one campus. I'd confirm the current numbers, but the scale is real: this is the regional hub people drive *to* for care.

That depth matters. Temple isn't a town where the specialist is two hours away — the specialists are here, because patients come here. How fast you're seen still depends on your insurance, your referral, and the specialty, so call your own doctors before you move. But the coverage for a city this size is unusual.

And if you're a military retiree, there's a full VA hospital right in town — the Olin E. Teague Veterans' Medical Center. Having both the VA and Baylor Scott & White in one mid-sized city is rare, and it's a real draw.

**[1:50-2:10 — Pull-quote. Talking head.]**
Here's how I'd sum up what I've seen: most retirees who land in Temple came for the hospital and stayed for the cost of living. The ones who leave came for the cost of living and left because of the boredom. Remember that — it's the whole decision in one sentence.

**[2:10-3:10 — MONEY: taxes. GRAPHIC: "Over-65 school-tax freeze (verify current rules)."]**
Now the money, because Texas does two things most states don't. First — no state income tax. Your Social Security, your pension, your 401(k) withdrawals don't get taxed at the state level. If you're moving from a state with a 5% income tax and drawing around 60 grand a year, that's roughly three thousand dollars a year you keep. From California, it can be over five.

Second — and most people don't learn this until after they buy — the over-65 school-tax freeze. Once you turn 65 and file the over-65 homestead exemption with the appraisal district, your school district taxes freeze at the dollar amount you owed that year, and they stay frozen even as the home appreciates. There are additional over-65 exemptions on top of that. I'm not going to put an exact dollar figure on your savings on camera, because it depends on your rate, your home's value, and the current exemption amounts — and those change. What I'll tell you is it's meaningful, and you should run your actual numbers with Bell County's appraisal district.

One thing almost everybody misses: that freeze can transfer if you move within Texas, but it's proportional — not dollar-for-dollar — and there's a filing deadline. Don't trust a video for the exact form; ask the appraisal district which certificate to file and do it the year you move, or you can lose the benefit.

**[3:10-3:55 — MONEY: cost + comparison. GRAPHIC: comparison card "Temple vs Georgetown (verify medians)."]**
Stack that on top of home prices and the gap becomes hard to ignore. The median home in Temple has been running around 255 thousand; Georgetown's closer to 425; Fredericksburg is higher still. Now — a median isn't the same house, so compare honestly: age, condition, lot, HOA, tax district. But in the same budget, you typically get more home in Temple, *and* you've got that Level I trauma center in town. For Level I adult trauma, Georgetown is tied into the Austin and Round Rock network up the road.

A retired couple's monthly cost here lands in a wide range depending on whether you carry a mortgage — but it's well below the Austin metro. Build your own budget from the real line items: payment or cash, taxes, insurance, HOA, utilities, healthcare, transportation. And verify the medians the month you actually shop, because they move.

**[3:55-4:55 — HOUSING. B-roll: single-story drives in a few newer Temple neighborhoods.]**
So what do people actually buy? Here's the pattern I see a lot. Folks come in wanting acreage — a ranch-style place with land. Then, once roof, foundation, and HVAC costs enter the math, they tend to narrow toward newer single-story homes instead. On a fixed income, a fifteen-thousand-dollar foundation repair or a twelve-thousand-dollar roof is a budget-breaking event, and newer construction — say 2018 and later — comes with builder warranties, modern HVAC, and single-story layouts built for accessibility.

I won't tell you which neighborhood fits *you* based on your age — that's your call against real criteria. But the criteria that matter for most retirees are: distance to the hospital, single-story inventory, HOA cost, lot size, the age of the home's systems, and trail or lake access. Temple has several newer communities — Bella Terra and Lake Pointe lean newer and lower-maintenance and sit close to the hospital; Three Creeks adds Belton Lake trails; Canyon Creek trades newer construction for bigger lots and mature trees. Match the home to your criteria, not your age bracket.

**[4:55-6:10 — THE HONEST NEGATIVE. Talking head, serious. LOWER-THIRD: "Who should NOT retire here."]**
Now the part that gets skipped when Temple gets reduced to "it's cheap." Here's who should *not* retire in Temple.

First, the big one: Temple does not have a Sun City. There's no large 55-plus, gated, golf-cart, activities-director community here — just one 55-plus apartment community. So if that resort lifestyle is what you're picturing for retirement — the amenity center, the organized calendar — then Georgetown is genuinely the better call, and I'd rather you hear that from me now than figure it out after you've moved.

Second, the summers are brutal. Mid-to-upper 90s, June through August, with a stretch of days over a hundred, and your summer electric bill climbs. Plan your outdoor life around October through May.

Third, this is a car-dependent city. The local micro-transit is limited and there's no reliable rideshare like you'd have in Austin. If you expect to lean on transit or rideshare down the road, test it before you buy — doctor visits, groceries, church, airport runs, the late-night backup ride.

And fourth, there's no continuing-care retirement community — no single campus that takes you from independent living through memory care. The nearest ones are in the Austin metro. If you're retiring solo and planning for escalating care, that's a real gap worth weighing.

**[6:10-7:05 — TAYLOR'S TAKE. Talking head, personal.]**
Let me make this concrete. My own parents are looking at retiring here, from Mansfield. What I tell them is what I'll tell you: it's not as upscale as where they are now — but the money goes dramatically further. A 350-thousand-dollar home here buys what 550-plus gets you in Mansfield, and the healthcare is comparable or better, because Baylor Scott & White is a Level I trauma center. The tradeoff is dining and entertainment — you'll drive to Austin, about an hour, when you want a nice dinner or a show. That's the honest deal.

And the real reason most people land here? Family. A lot of folks come to be near kids and grandkids who already work at Baylor Scott & White or out at Fort Cavazos. Close enough to visit daily, affordable enough to buy outright or with a small mortgage, and the hospital's right there for peace of mind.

**[7:05-7:50 — VERDICT + CTA. Talking head.]**
So, my verdict. Temple is the right retirement move for practical people who put healthcare, cost, and family first. It's the wrong move for people who want a resort lifestyle. Figure out which one you are before you start looking — that single decision saves you a year of regret.

If you're the practical type, I put together a full written breakdown — the tax-freeze details, the neighborhoods, the healthcare picture — and it's linked in the description and pinned in the comments.
