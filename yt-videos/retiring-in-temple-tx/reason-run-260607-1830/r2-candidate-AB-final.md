## FULL WORD-FOR-WORD SCRIPT (~1,420 words / ~8.5–9 min)

**[0:00–0:16 — HOOK (two sentences, so identity can be sentence 3). Talking head, no name. Visual beat ~7s: face → 1-sec cut of a Sun City golf-cart/amenity → back to face.]**
A lot of Texas retirement advice points you to Georgetown's Sun City. I'm going to make the case for the city about 35 minutes north that rarely gets marketed as a retirement town — Temple — and then give you the one honest reason you might still pick Georgetown.

**[0:16–0:34 — IDENTITY (sentence 3, after 0:15) + FRAME. B-roll: BSW campus exterior, slow push-in.]**
I'm Taylor Dasch with EG Realty. I've closed over $28 million in transactions here in Temple and Belton specifically, and I work with retirees and downsizers most weeks of the year. So this isn't a brochure — it's the honest version: what's strong, what's missing, and who Temple is genuinely wrong for.

**[0:34–0:54 — THESIS + RETENTION ROADMAP. LOWER-THIRD: "Healthcare-first retirement."]**
Here's the thesis. Temple has one of the strongest healthcare-to-cost combinations in Central Texas — and the reason you've rarely heard it framed that way is simple: Temple is a working medical city, not a brand-built retirement town. That's not a knock. That's the whole point. I'll walk you through the healthcare, the taxes, the real cost, and the neighborhoods — and then the honest list of who should *not* move here. Numbers first.

**[0:54–1:52 — HEALTHCARE. B-roll: BSW exterior, then Olin E. Teague VA. GRAPHIC: "Level I trauma center (verify current beds)."]**
Start with the thing that quietly matters most as you age: healthcare. Because here's the real fear nobody says out loud — it's not the golf course, it's what happens when something goes wrong and you need a hospital fast. Baylor Scott & White here in Temple is a Level I trauma center — by their own designation, the only one on I-35 between Dallas and Austin — with more than six hundred beds and over thirty specialties on one campus. Confirm the current numbers for yourself, but the scale is real: this is the regional hub people drive *to* for care.

[Beat — graphic to talking head.] And that depth changes the math of getting older. Temple isn't a place where your specialist is two hours away — the specialists are *here*, because the patients come here. How fast you actually get seen still depends on your insurance, your referral, and the specialty, so call your own doctors before you move. But for a city this size, that coverage is genuinely unusual.

[B-roll: VA medical center.] And if you're a military retiree, there's a full VA hospital right in town — the Olin E. Teague Veterans' Medical Center. Having both the VA and Baylor Scott & White in one mid-sized city is rare — and for a lot of veterans, that dual access is the whole reason they even look at Temple.

**[1:52–2:12 — PULL-QUOTE. Talking head, direct, slower.]**
Here's how I'd sum up everything I've watched happen here: most retirees who land in Temple came for the hospital and stayed for the cost of living. The ones who leave came for the cost of living and left because of the boredom. That one sentence is basically the whole decision. Hold onto it.

**[2:12–3:14 — MONEY: TAXES. GRAPHIC: "Over-65 school-tax freeze (verify current rules)."]**
Now the money, because Texas does two things most states don't. First — no state income tax. Your Social Security, your pension, your 401(k) withdrawals — none of it gets taxed at the state level. Just how much that's worth depends on your income mix and your old state's rates, so run it with your CPA — but for a lot of people coming from a high-tax state, it's thousands of dollars a year, every year.

[Beat.] Second — and most people don't learn this until *after* they buy — the over-65 school-tax freeze. The year you turn 65 and file the over-65 homestead exemption with the appraisal district, your school district taxes get capped at the amount you owed that year, even as the home's value rises. There are additional over-65 exemptions on top. I'm not going to put an exact savings figure on camera, because it depends on your tax rate, your home's value, and the current exemption amounts — and those change. What I'll tell you is it's meaningful, and you should run your real numbers with Bell County's appraisal district.

[Lower-third: "Don't miss the deadline."] And here's the part almost everybody misses: that freeze can transfer if you move within Texas — but it's proportional, not dollar-for-dollar, and there's a filing deadline. Don't trust a video for the exact form. Ask the appraisal district which certificate to file, and file it the year you move — or you can lose the benefit entirely.

**[3:14–3:59 — MONEY: COST + COMPARISON. GRAPHIC: comparison card "Temple vs Georgetown (verify medians)."]**
Now stack that on top of home prices, and the price gap becomes hard to ignore. The median home in Temple has been running around 255 thousand; Georgetown's closer to 425; Fredericksburg is higher still — verify those the month you actually shop, because they move. A median isn't the same house, so compare honestly: age, condition, lot, HOA, tax district. But in the same budget, you typically get more home in Temple — *and* you've got that Level I trauma center in town. For Level I adult trauma, Georgetown is tied into the Austin and Round Rock network up the road.

[Beat.] A retired couple's monthly cost here lands in a wide range depending on whether you carry a mortgage, but it's well below the Austin metro. Build your own budget from the real line items — payment or cash, taxes, insurance, HOA, utilities, healthcare, transportation.

**[3:59–4:59 — HOUSING. B-roll: slow drives through newer single-story streets; cut to neutral roof/HVAC/inspection visuals.]**
So what do people actually buy? Here's the pattern I see constantly. Folks come in wanting acreage — a ranch-style place with land. Then, once roof, foundation, and HVAC costs enter the math, they tend to narrow toward newer single-story homes instead. On a fixed income, a fifteen-thousand-dollar foundation repair or a twelve-thousand-dollar roof is a budget-breaking event — and newer construction, say 2018 and later, may still carry part of a builder or structural warranty, plus modern HVAC and single-story layouts built for accessibility. Always verify the actual warranty documents.

[Beat — talking head.] I'm not going to tell you which neighborhood fits *you* — that's your call, against real criteria. The criteria that matter for most retirees: distance to the hospital, single-story inventory, HOA cost, lot size, the age of the home's systems, and trail or lake access. Temple has several newer communities to weigh against those — Bella Terra and Lake Pointe lean newer, lower-maintenance, and close to the hospital; Three Creeks adds Belton Lake trails; Canyon Creek trades newer construction for bigger lots and mature trees. Match the home to your criteria.

**[4:59–6:14 — THE HONEST NEGATIVE (the promised payoff). Talking head, serious. LOWER-THIRD: "Who should NOT retire here."]**
Okay — the part I promised at the top. Here's the one honest reason you might pick Georgetown over Temple, and then a few smaller catches.

[Beat — number card "THE BIG ONE" on screen.] The big one: Temple does not have a Sun City equivalent — a large, master-planned, 55-plus community built around golf carts, an amenity center, and an activities director. Temple has senior-living and apartment options, so check those for yourself — but the resort-style retirement *ecosystem*, the kind Georgetown is built around, isn't here. So if that lifestyle is what you're picturing for retirement, Georgetown is genuinely the better call. That's the one. I'd rather you hear it from me now than figure it out after you've already moved.

[Number card "and a few smaller catches".] Now the smaller catches. The summers are brutal — mid-to-upper 90s, June through August, a stretch of days over a hundred, and your summer electric bill climbs; plan your outdoor life around October through May. This is a car-dependent city — Uber and the local micro-transit exist, but don't assume Austin-level availability at night or for recurring medical trips, so test your real routes before you buy. And if you'll eventually need a true continuing-care setup — one campus that carries you from independent living through memory care — verify what's actually licensed here before you commit; Temple's care ecosystem isn't built out the way Georgetown's or Austin's is, and that matters most if you're retiring solo.

**[6:14–7:09 — TAYLOR'S TAKE. Talking head, personal, warmer.]**
Let me make this concrete. My own parents are looking at retiring here, from Mansfield. What I tell them is exactly what I'll tell you: it's not as upscale as where they live now — but the money goes noticeably further. A similar budget buys you meaningfully more home here than in Mansfield, and the healthcare is comparable or better, because Baylor Scott & White is a Level I trauma center. The tradeoff is dining and entertainment — you'll drive to Austin, about an hour, when you want a nice dinner or a show. That's the honest deal.

[Beat.] And the real reason most people land here? Family. A lot of folks come to be near kids and grandkids who already work at Baylor Scott & White or out at Fort Cavazos. Close enough to visit daily, affordable enough to buy outright or with a small mortgage, and the hospital's right there for peace of mind.

**[7:09–7:55 — VERDICT + CTA (closes the hook loop). Talking head, confident close.]**
So here's my verdict, and it ties right back to where we started. If you want that resort-style retirement — the master-planned community, the activities director — then take this as permission to go look hard at Georgetown. But if you put healthcare, cost, and family first, Temple is the better fit, and most retirees I meet are in that second group. Figure out which one you are before you start looking — that single decision saves you a lot of second-guessing later.

[Beat — lower-third with the URL.] If you're the practical type, I put together a full written breakdown — the tax-freeze details, the neighborhoods, the healthcare picture — and it's linked in the description and pinned in the comments. And if you want to talk through whether Temple actually fits *your* situation, my number's down there too. I'm Taylor Dasch. I'll see you in the next one.
