# 1. SINGLE BEST CONCEPT

**One sentence:**
A buyer's field procedure for Morgan's Point Resort built on one verifiable fact from the July 20, 2026 MLS records — the four active listings that carry the town's own name in the city field start at $315,000, while every one of the six homes under $275,000 has its city field set to Belton — so a buyer who shops this town by its name prices it $110,000 too high, crosses it off as unaffordable, and never learns that the half they could afford is also the 1970s half that carries a second price tag at inspection.

**Why this and not the tour, and not the inventory-count version:**

The obvious version of this finding is "you're missing listings." That version is weak, and it points the buyer the wrong way. Count the four listings that carry the town's name and look at what they cost: $315,000, $330,000, $665,000, $869,000 — a median of $497,500 `[DERIVED: median of the 4 = ($330,000+$665,000)/2]`. The true active median is $330,000 `[CONFIRMED]`. The name-carrying view isn't a thin slice of the same market. It is a **50.8% more expensive** market `[DERIVED: $497,500 ÷ $330,000 − 1]`, with a floor $110,000 above the real one `[DERIVED: $315,000 − $205,000]`.

So the buyer's actual failure mode is not "there's nothing here." It's **"I can't afford here"** — a conclusion reached in about eleven seconds, from a listing set whose entire bottom half is filed under a different city's name. That buyer never calls anyone. They are lost silently, and no agent ever hears about it.

That is the video: **the price illusion, its mechanism, and the three things it hides.** And it chains cleanly — the six sub-$275,000 actives are not just the affordable band, they are disproportionately the *old* band (four of the six were built before 1980: 1971, 1977, 1979, 1979) `[CONFIRMED, §E]`. Finding the affordable half and understanding the inspection math are the same problem, which is why this is one video and not two.

**Structural note — this is v2 of the folder's spine, not a re-do.** The prior council concept (`reason-run-260518-1647`) established that MPR is multiple markets stacked on one set of MLS rows and that the median is a misleading anchor. This extends it with two things the May version did not have: the city-field price illusion (§H1) and the vintage inversion (§B). The spine is not re-litigated.

Three changes from the obvious implementation, all deliberate:

1. **Upper-left, not lower-left.** The lower-left third is where the YouTube scrubber and a phone thumb live. A label nobody reads discharges no obligation.
2. **The chip is the backup, not the mechanism.** Every claim that needs a label is *also* labeled out loud in the sentence that carries it. The chip is a redundancy for skimmers, not the compliance system. If the chip were removed entirely in the edit, the video would still be compliant.
3. **It changes on segment boundaries and on flagged claims only** — roughly 14 state changes in 10 minutes, not 90. A chip that flickers is a chip nobody reads.

**No fifth chip exists, and that is enforced by exclusion:** any number that cannot take one of the four labels — keyword volume estimates, portal behavior, third-party AVM values — is not spoken on camera and does not appear in a graphic. That rule is what keeps the label set honest.

# 6. CONTRARIAN THESIS

## The statement

> **Morgan's Point Resort is not an expensive lake town with a few cheap houses in it — it is an affordable, aging town with a small, new, expensive fringe, and the MLS city field happens to sort those two things almost perfectly, so the town's own name functions as a price filter that removes its affordable half and its inspection problem at the same time.**

## The argument

**1. The sort is not approximate. It is clean, and it is a census, not a sample.**
Fifteen active listings on July 20, 2026 — all of them, not a sample of them. Four carry the city value "Morgans Point Resort," eleven carry "Belton" `[CONFIRMED, §H1]`. Now look at what falls on each side:

| | n | Median list | Cheapest | Median build yr | Pre-1980 count |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| City field = "Morgans Point Resort" | 4 | **$497,500** `[DERIVED]` | $315,000 | **2012.5** `[DERIVED]` | **0** |
| City field = "Belton" | 11 | $272,500 `[CONFIRMED]` | **$205,000** | **1977** `[DERIVED]` | **8** |

Every one of the six actives under $275,000 is on the Belton side. **All eight pre-1980 actives are on the Belton side — eight of eight** `[DERIVED from the §E active table]`. Not one of the four name-carrying listings predates 2002 `[CONFIRMED, §E]`. A single text field is separating a 1977 median from a 2012 median and a $272,500 median from a $497,500 median. That is not skew. That is separation.

**2. The thesis makes no claim about any portal, and therefore cannot be falsified by one.**
It asserts a field value in the July 20, 2026 records and the price and vintage distribution on either side of it. Nothing about Zillow, Realtor, Redfin, or any search engine `[per §H2]`. That restraint is what makes it durable: any system that touches the town name at any point — a saved search set to a city, an MLS hotsheet, an out-of-area agent typing the town into a criteria box, an AI answer engine reading city-tagged records — inherits the same split, and the split is the finding. The prescription is likewise portal-independent: **search by map area or zip, then confirm against the map.**

**3. It does not accuse anyone, which is why a local can't push back on it.**
Both entries are defensible. MPR is its own incorporated city `[CONFIRMED]`; it is also the Belton mailing/ISD orbit and all 29 records show Belton ISD `[CONFIRMED]`. Listing agents filing under Belton are doing the ordinary, reasonable thing. The claim is not that anyone erred — it is that **the aggregate of a reasonable habit produced a price signal nobody measured.**

## What it displaces

The default video for this town is "great lake community, prices from the low $200s to $800s, something for everyone." That framing quotes the $330,000 median as an anchor — which is the exact number this town's structure makes meaningless, because the fifteen homes behind it span $151 to $310 per square foot and 1966 to 2026 `[CONFIRMED, §E]`. **The median isn't a center here. It's an average of two different towns.**

# 7. THE BUYER MISTAKE PREVENTED

## The mistake, stated precisely

**The buyer crosses Morgan's Point Resort off the list because they conclude "I can't afford this town" — and they are wrong, and nobody ever finds out they were here.**

This is not the mistake it looks like. The obvious version — "you're missing listings" — produces a mildly inconvenienced buyer who keeps shopping. The real version produces a buyer who **makes a final decision, correctly reasoned from bad inputs, in about eleven seconds, and never contacts a human being.** There is no lead, no inquiry, no bounce anyone can measure. It is the only category of buyer mistake that is invisible to the person best positioned to fix it.

## The mechanism, verified

On July 20, 2026, the four active listings carrying the town's name in the MLS city field ran **$315,000 · $330,000 · $665,000 · $869,000 — median $497,500** `[CONFIRMED prices; DERIVED median: ($330,000+$665,000)/2]`. A buyer whose ceiling is $275,000 reads that as a town that starts $40,000 above their maximum and runs to nearly nine hundred thousand. The reasoning is sound. The conclusion is wrong.

Because **all six actives under $275,000 are filed under Belton** `[CONFIRMED, §H1]`:

| List | Street | Built | $/sf |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| $205,000 | Cliffside | 1979 | $205 |
| $230,000 | Bobcat | 2000 | $160 |
| $244,900 | Morgans Point | 1977 | $172 |
| $249,500 | Hickory | 1979 | $199 |
| $265,000 | Sherwood | 2006 | $160 |
| $272,500 | Cliffside | 1971 | $152 |

The true floor is **$205,000** — **$110,000 below** the name-carrying floor `[DERIVED: $315,000 − $205,000]`.

## Why fixing the price problem alone would be malpractice

Because the affordable half is disproportionately the old half. **Four of those six were built 1971, 1977, 1979, 1979** `[CONFIRMED, §E]`. Their median $/sqft is **$166** against $188 active-wide `[DERIVED: median of $205, $160, $172, $199, $160, $152]`.

And the closed record says buyers are already voting: of the seven homes that closed in the roughly sixty days ending July 20, 2026, the median build year is **2000** — twenty-one years newer than the 1979 active median — and **zero homes built before 1985 closed** `[CONFIRMED, §B]`. Meanwhile **eight of the fifteen actives were built before 1980** `[CONFIRMED]`. The pre-1980 stock is not the bargain. It is the overhang. `[OPINION on "overhang"; the counts are CONFIRMED]` `n=7 — small, and said so on camera` `[per §I]`.

So a video that only fixed the search would deliver a buyer into a 1971 house carrying a Temple-new-construction mental model of what a house costs to own. **That is why the price problem and the inspection problem are one video and not two:** the listing that rescues the buyer's budget and the listing that threatens it are the same listing.

## What the video hands the viewer instead

1. **Search by map area or zip, not by the town name. Confirm against the map.** `[per §H2]`
2. **Read the city field on every listing you tour.** It tells you which half of this town you're standing in.
3. **Rank by $/sqft, not list price.** The active range is $151–$310 `[CONFIRMED, §E]` and list price will not tell you which is which.
4. **Check the subdivision, not just the city.** Above roughly $600,000 you are likely in Rancho Del Lago or Campus at Lakewood Ranch — newer developments inside the same city limits, not MPR Sections 1–9 `[CONFIRMED, §E]`.
5. **Price the decade, not just the house.** On a pre-1980 listing: roof, foundation, HVAC, panel, supply lines, and a **bound insurance quote before the option period ends.** `[OPINION — procedure]`
6. **Verify what the MLS cannot carry.** HOA per property (27 of 29 None, 2 Mandatory) `[CONFIRMED]`; Belton ISD on all 29 records, confirm your exact address with the district `[CONFIRMED]`; water, dock, shoreline and current lake level with the City of Morgan's Point Resort and the USACE Belton Lake Resource Manager's Office — **no listing sheet grants lake access** `[per §F]`; drive times measured by you, at your hour `[per HARD RULE 7]`.

## The cost of the mistake, and why the CTA follows from it

A buyer at $250,000 who crosses this town off gets a smaller, newer house somewhere else at the same payment — a defensible outcome. What they never get is the **choice**, because the six listings that would have made it a choice were filed under another city's name. The video's entire job is to convert that silent exit into a decision made on the real numbers.

Which is exactly what the CTA offers and no more: a shortlist pulled by map area rather than city name, sorted by $/sqft with build year on every line, subdivision flagged, and the real monthly payment with age-of-home costs in it. **It does not offer to verify water tiers** — Taylor states at 2:40 that no waterfront, water-access, or dock field exists in these records `[per §F]`, and an offer to sort "true" water lots from "pretenders" would spend the exact credibility that admission bought. The ask is smaller than the video earned. That is why it converts. `[OPINION]`
