## 1. THE ONE CONCEPT

**"I Toured Three $250K Homes in Morgan's Point Resort. Only One Was Actually on the Lake."**

A same-price comp walk built around the MPR median sold price. Three real recent solds, all closed at or near $249,500, each representing a different water tier:

- **Tier 1 LAKEFRONT** — USACE-leased shoreline, dock-permittable
- **Tier 2 LAKE-VIEW or LAKE-ACCESS** — hilltop view or short walk to ramp/marina, no shoreline rights
- **Tier 3 INLAND MPR** — the address + Belton ISD, no functional water relationship

Filmed from USACE Owl Creek Park (public access). Home shots are drive-by from public road + drone over public airspace + map/parcel overlays. No listing-access dependency. No trespass. No tourism-board angle.

The video is the embodied, on-camera version of the live page's most counterintuitive insight: same MLS, same zip code, same price, three completely different products. Buyers who don't know the difference write Zillow-comp offers and either overpay for tier 2/3 thinking it's tier 1, or panic-walk from tier 1 because the septic + FEMA + USACE disclosures look scarier than the inland comp set.

### Why this beats the obvious neighborhood-tour angle

A tour says "look how nice it is." This video says "here is the specific mistake most MPR buyers make before they call me." It survives the inverse-fail rubric because:

- **MPR-specific by construction.** The 3-tier mistake cannot be ported to any other Texas lake town — it depends on MPR's no-HOA + mixed-vintage + USACE-shoreline + SUP-required combo.
- **Doesn't duplicate the page.** It's the live demo of the page's framework, not a re-read.
- **Useful even if the viewer never calls.** The Water-Tier Quick Check at 8:45 is a saveable asset.
- **Honest negatives baked in.** Septic, FEMA, windstorm, SUP — all stated on-camera with sources.
- **Survives skeptical fact-check.** USACE Belton Lake Resource Manager's Office is named on-camera as the dock-permit authority.

## 6. BIG IDEA / CONTRARIAN THESIS

Morgan's Point Resort is not one market — it is three markets stacked on the same MLS rows under the same zip code. The MLS does not separate them. Zillow does not separate them. Most listing photos do not honestly disclose which tier they belong to. The contrarian thesis: the median sold price of $249,500 is a misleading anchor for any individual MPR offer, because it averages three different products into one number. The buyer's job — and the agent's job — is to pull the rows apart by water tier, comp each tier against itself, and only then form an offer thesis. The honest version of the MPR market does not fit in a single comp number.

---

## 7. THE BUYER MISTAKE THIS VIDEO PREVENTS

The primary mistake: buyers see the MPR median, see a $249K listing, assume they are buying "a Belton Lake home" and offer accordingly. They later discover the home has no USACE shoreline lease (it is lake-access, not lakefront), has no dock-permit pathway, has a septic with deferred maintenance, sits in a FEMA AE flood zone that triples insurance, and cannot legally short-term-rent without a Specific Use Permit.

The opposite mistake is equally common: buyers see a true lakefront listing with full disclosures (septic age, FEMA zone, windstorm requirement, USACE rules) and walk away thinking the home is "more risky" than the inland comp, when in reality the lakefront is the better long-term hold and the inland comp simply has fewer disclosure items to read.

Both mistakes come from the same root cause: not separating the three tiers before underwriting the offer. This video gives the buyer the framework to avoid both.
