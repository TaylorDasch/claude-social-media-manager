## 4. HONEST-TRADEOFF ANGLE (neutral, symmetric)
New construction's real scars in Temple aren't cosmetic: some newer Bell County / Temple-area developments sit inside MUD or PID districts — Bell County has named ones on the books, like MUD No. 1 at the Three Creeks community in Belton — that can add $2,000–$5,000 a year and push an effective rate from about 2.37% to roughly 3.5%; on a $280K home that's $267 a month and stacks toward six figures over a 30-year mortgage. You're paying about a 16.7% per-square-foot premium ($174 vs $149) for new; volume-builder quality runs genuinely inconsistent on high-output product, so a third-party structural inspection is mandatory, not optional; and incentives are usually tied to the builder's preferred lender, where the spread can outcost the headline discount. Resale's scars are just as real and the opposite shape: older roofs, HVAC, plumbing, and a foundation that's been moving on Blackland Prairie clay for decades, so budget for repairs by inspection rather than assuming a flat number; sellers often give fewer visible incentives than a builder clearing finished inventory; and the genuinely good resale homes — right lot, right neighborhood, close to BSW — draw competing offers precisely because they're scarce, which shrinks your leverage on the very homes you want most. Neither path is the safe one. The right call is whichever set of scars fits your life, budget, and timeline — which is the entire reason to run the numbers before a model home frames the decision for you.
