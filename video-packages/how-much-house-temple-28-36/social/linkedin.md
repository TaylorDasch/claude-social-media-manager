The most expensive mistake I see first-time buyers make isn't the house they pick.

It's the question they ask the bank.

They ask "how much can I qualify for?" — and then they shop at that number. That's how you end up house-poor: a nice kitchen, a nice bathroom, and no money left to live.

Here's the question that actually protects you: "What payment lets me still save, invest, and cover a surprise repair?"

The 28/36 rule answers it:

→ Keep your housing payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance) under 28% of gross monthly income.
→ Keep your total monthly debt under 36%.

What that looks like in Temple, TX:

• $7,000/month income → about a $1,960 housing payment
• With our ~2.25% effective property-tax rate, that's roughly the $260,000–$280,000 range
• Which happens to be right around Temple's $270,000 median price

One thing most people underestimate: your down payment moves the answer more than the interest rate does. On a $260K home, going from 5% down to 20% down can cut the payment ~$350/month — the difference between comfortable and stretched.

I bought my own first home here in Temple, and I've helped 100+ buyers since. The ones who feel good a year later all have one thing in common: they bought at a payment, not a ceiling.
